# Findings
- The weights encode a fixed maze/pellet-like layout but not a clean symmetric one: obstacle/reward norms are scattered/moderate, top obstacle and reward cells are mostly disjoint (top-8 overlap 0), symmetry is low/moderate, and linearized sensitivity shows no clean symmetric maze/pellet pattern [E4, E32, E34, E35].
- The learned policy is not a competent Pac-Man controller: with border walls only it is mostly down (some left/right), it does not avoid the bottom border wall, and it rarely flips for adjacent obstacle/reward/mover [E6, E8, E13, E32].
- The policy is dominated by the fixed interior wall pattern: the full wall set alone selects left with high probability, adding adjacent rewards/movers/obstacles rarely changes the argmax, and the full-maze flow field is all left with high confidence [E16, E20, E22, E24, E32].
- Rewards and moving objects are not systematically sought or avoided: in a full Pac-Man-like state the action grid is mostly left/up with negative mean reward-toward and mover-away dot products [E21], and top reward/obstacle cells produce small inconsistent probability changes [E10].
- The previous-frame channel has little control over action: most previous-frame scenarios remain down, with only one left flip when combined with a mover below [E13].
- Agent-channel raw W1 norms are scattered, but linearized sensitivity gives a moderate top peak at (8,6) (78.1 vs 66.0 at (6,6) and 58.2 at (2,8)); this is not a sharply isolated start detector [E30, E35].
- Linearized sensitivity top cells often coincide with W1 top cells for mover ((1,5)/(2,5)), reward ((6,6)), and obstacle ((7,5)), but no clean designed maze emerges [E35].
- Overall: the weights encode a fixed maze/pellet-like layout, but PPO converged to a degenerate wall-biased controller—mostly down with border walls, left with the full maze, and high confidence—rather than learning pellet-seeking and ghost avoidance; sensitivity adds only a moderate agent peak, not a clean start/maze solution [E16, E21, E22, E24, E32, E35].
