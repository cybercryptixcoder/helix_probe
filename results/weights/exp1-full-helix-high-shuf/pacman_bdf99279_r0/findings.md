# Findings
- The output layer has a strong baseline left-action bias: baseline logits are about [0.05, −4.54, −5.12, 3.74, −13.33], so action 3 (left) usually wins and action 4 (right) is heavily suppressed (E24, E34, E37).
- Left wins in most probes—empty grid, walls/corridors, many reward/mover/box cases—but not all: full observations can select up or noop, e.g. mover_up_d1, a22_reward_up, a77_reward_down (E11, E13, E14, E37).
- Reward and mover positions do modulate logits, sometimes by large amounts: reward_left left−right ≈ +15.91, reward_right right−left ≈ −16.42, mover_left left−right ≈ +16.41, mover_right right−left ≈ −16.11 (E37).
- That modulation is dominated by the baseline left/right asymmetry and is not a clean Pac-Man navigator: all reward probes still argmax left, reward_right does not make right win, mover_left does not suppress left, and only mover_up_d1 flips to up (E37).
- After removing baseline bias, stimulus-specific effects are mixed: some reward/mover probes are small, others are larger and context-dependent; no consistent reward-chasing or mover-avoidance pattern emerges (E34, E36, E37).
- The policy is not a competent Sokoban box-pusher: target-beyond-box alignment does not reliably raise the push logit (E14), and a wall behind a box does not reliably make illegal pushes less likely (E13).
- The policy is not a Snake-style no-reverse controller: the agent’s previous cell does not lock the action, and self_from_* probes mostly still choose left (E22).
- The previous-frame channel is used only weakly/contextually to infer mover direction: approaching vs retreating changes some logits but not the dominant action (E18, E22).
- W1 row norms are scattered across grid positions and channels with no clean spatial focus, consistent with a weakly learned/degenerate policy rather than a precise maze solver (E36).
- The observation schema—agent, moving objects, reward targets, obstacles/walls, and previous frame—plus weak/contextual mover and previous-frame sensitivity still fits a simplified Pac-Man, but the learned behavior is poor and left-biased (E18, E22, E34, E36, E37).
- Best game identification: simplified Pac-Man; confidence about 80% (E18, E22, E34, E36, E37).
