- The network’s policy is dominated by action 3 (left): left is the most frequent argmax across object columns (E6), reward directions (E8), agent-only positions (E16), and random states (E23), with no-op second, especially at the left wall (E16).
- The policy does not steer toward task-relevant objects: moving a ball/object across columns (E6), placing a reward target in each cardinal direction (E8), or sweeping reward/moving objects (E21) does not produce the expected left/right/up/down shifts.
- W1 input weights show no spatial specialization: per-channel row-norm profiles are flat (E18, E19, E24), channel means are all about 4.6–5.1 (E12, E24), and top cells are scattered rather than concentrated in top rows for reward or a bottom row for the agent (E12, E19, E24).
- Ablating any input channel changes logits only modestly and no single channel dominates (E14), consistent with the policy largely ignoring the grid contents.
- The output layer favors no-op and left and suppresses up/down: W3 column sums are [9.14, -5.36, -8.33, 5.59, 3.47] and b3 is positive (E12), which fits a game where only horizontal movement is useful, not one requiring vertical movement or shooting.
- These results rule out a learned Breakout paddle-steering policy, Pac-Man pellet-seeking, and Space Invaders alien-targeting (E6, E8, E12, E18); the network is a degenerate left/no-op policy.
- The most likely training game is Breakout or a Breakout-like horizontal-paddle game, because the action bias is horizontal-only (left/right/no-op, up/down suppressed) and the environment contains a moving ball and reward targets, but the network failed to learn the game and the weights do not encode Breakout-specific structure (E12, E16, E24).
