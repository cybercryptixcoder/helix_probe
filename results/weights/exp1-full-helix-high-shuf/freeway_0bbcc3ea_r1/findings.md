# Findings
- Channel 1 (moving objects) has low average first-layer row norms (interior mean 3.29, border mean 3.49) but not uniformly low: high rows include (9,1)=7.22, (9,9)=6.87, (7,8)=6.79, (6,3)=6.37, (8,7)=6.31, (4,9)=6.26; it is functionally used: moving objects change logits and sometimes argmax in position/heading-dependent ways [E15, E24, E27, E28, E29, E34, E38, E40].
- Moving-object effects are context- and previous-position-sensitive: at (4,4), M_right switches argmax 2→0 with logits [7.85, -0.93, 2.88, -11.38, -2.79], while at (3,3) all M placements leave argmax=0 with mixed logit changes; M(4,6) with prev-left vs prev-right gives different logits [E28, E29, E34].
- In the E32 straight-ahead sample, a moving object ahead changes argmax away from current direction in 7/9 states vs obstacle ahead in 3/9; current-direction mean logit drops base 5.48→4.14 (moving) vs 4.28 (obstacle) [E32].
- Across E38’s 14-state sweep, there is no simple approach>recede>obstacle threat ordering: mean forward logit is baseline -4.40, moving_approach -3.60, moving_recede -4.48, obstacle -4.21, reward -3.75; mean no-op is baseline 4.06, moving_approach 4.75, moving_recede 4.37, obstacle 3.88, reward 4.79. Moving objects reduce forward-argmax counts (approach/recede 1 vs baseline/obstacle/reward 2), but approaching moving objects do not most suppress forward logits [E38].
- At (3,3) no previous frame, baseline argmax=0 logits [5.51, -4.17, 1.59, -5.19, -9.29]; adjacent rewards, moving objects, and obstacles all leave argmax=0, with direction-specific logit changes that are not simple attraction/repulsion (e.g., R_up raises no-op, O_down raises no-op and down, M_left lowers no-op) [E29].
- Channel 2 (reward targets) has broad interior weight structure with high rows such as (7,4)=8.54, (8,2)=7.83, (4,2)=6.51, (6,9)=6.41, consistent with reward/food appearing at varying interior cells rather than a fixed goal [E1, E15, E40].
- Channel 3 (obstacles) has structured interior and border weights with peaks such as (3,8)=7.08, (2,1)=6.82, (6,4)=6.50, but E40 does not isolate a Pac-Man-style fixed maze in channel 3: high W1 rows are scattered across all channels [E1, E40].
- Channel 0 (agent) has strong structured weights, especially (3,3)=9.54, with other high rows such as (5,0)=7.30, (2,1)=7.15, (9,4)=6.83, (0,3)=6.83, (8,4)=6.64; channel 4 (previous frame) has structured weights such as (2,8)=6.53 [E1, E5, E40].
- The output biases are b3=[3.142, -1.862, -0.029, -1.269, 0.173], favoring action 0 (no-op); zero-input logits are [7.11, 3.01, 3.86, -1.76, 4.56] with argmax no-op [E21].
- The previous-frame channel strongly gates action choice: at (4,4), no previous frame → argmax down [2.50, -2.86, 6.11, -10.03, -10.36]; prev same/down → argmax no-op; prev up/left/right → argmax down; E2 reports a differing (4,4) baseline, so setup matters [E2, E6].
- Reward, moving-object, and obstacle perturbations do not follow a simple static attraction/repulsion rule: effects are mixed by position/heading, often leave argmax unchanged, and can raise no-op or direction logits; E38 further shows no simple velocity-based threat gradient [E2, E7, E24, E27, E28, E29, E32, E34, E38].
- E40’s top W1 rows are spread across channels (e.g., ch0 (3,3)=9.54, ch2 (7,4)=8.54, ch1 (6,3)=6.37, ch3 (3,8)=7.08), so the weight-norm evidence does not support a fixed maze localized to the obstacle channel [E40].
