- The policy has a dominant “up” action bias: empty/agent-only inputs give up-logit about 28–32 and all other logits negative, so argmax is action 1 in every tested configuration (E6, E22, E23, E36).
- The agent channel is the main input the network uses: changing agent position produces large logit changes, with the largest at agent (6,4), delta up +1.02 and delta right +8.39 relative to empty (E11, E36).
- Agent-channel sensitivity is spread over rows 2–8, with largest row-wise effects around rows 4–7 and global max at (6,4), not concentrated on the bottom row (E11).
- Row 1 of the agent channel is nearly ignored: agent at (1,4) or (1,5) changes logits by <0.2, consistent with the top row being terminal/rarely occupied during training (E36).
- Moving-object inputs are largely ignored: adjacent, dense-barrier, on-agent, and moving-with-previous-frame object placements leave argmax up and produce only small/local logit deltas, with no systematic avoidance or approach response (E6, E19, E22, E23, E31, E33).
- Obstacle and reward channels are essentially ignored in the playable area: obstacle max |Δlogit| ≈0.215, reward max ≈0.303, and a full obstacle row directly above the agent changes logits by <0.2 while argmax remains up (E4, E6, E28, E33).
- The previous-frame channel has only weak, non-systematic effects and does not encode a clear upward-motion heuristic: previous-frame placements produce small deltas, and object prev-left/right/same changes are <0.53 with argmax still up (E12, E18, E31).
- These observations fit a vertical-crossing game: the agent starts low and is rewarded for moving upward toward the top, while moving objects are traffic/background that this particular learned policy mostly ignores; they rule out bottom-row-only games such as Space Invaders and object-avoidance games such as Frogger.
- The game is most specifically Freeway: the agent/chicken moves upward across lanes of moving cars toward the top, and reward comes from advancing/crossing upward.
