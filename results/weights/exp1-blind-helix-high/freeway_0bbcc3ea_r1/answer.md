The output layer therefore encodes a nearly unconditional upward policy modulated mainly by moving-object lanes and their previous-frame motion.

Reasoning: The dominant Up action plus horizontal moving-object lanes and previous-frame motion strongly suggests an upward-crossing game. The weak reward and obstacle channels rule out collectible/maze games such as Asterix. The key distinction from Freeway is that moving objects are not uniformly dangerous: at agent (8,4), a gap in row 7 is safer than a full row (gap-full = +1.73), but a full row 5 is safer than a gap (full-gap = +1.18). This is consistent with some lanes being cars/roads where gaps are safe and other lanes being logs/river where full rows are safe to ride. The strong column-specific Up preferences, e.g. high at (5,5) but low at (5,4), also fit discrete home-slot alignment. The agent-row dependence of these effects is expected because the value of moving up depends on whether the frog is currently in the road, on a log, or near a home slot. Thus the game is Frogger, not Freeway.
