# Findings
- The policy has a dominant Up action: baseline logits at agent (8,4) are [-13.26, 29.06, -11.66, -15.35, -19.57], so softmax is almost all Up [E17, E21, E32].
- The Up logit depends strongly on the agent’s grid position, with large cell-specific peaks and dips [E22]; baseline Up at (4,4) is 27.07 vs 29.06 at (8,4) [E38].
- Moving objects are the main input feature the policy reads: full rows change Up by several logits, while single obstacles and reward targets change it by <0.5 in tested locations [E10, E13, E15, E23, E31, E32].
- A single moving object directly above slightly lowers Up; horizontal neighbors and previous-frame horizontal motion raise it, while vertical previous-frame cells lower it [E13, E17, E19, E21].
- The previous-frame channel is genuinely used: nearby previous-frame cells modulate Up by up to ~1.1 logits, and E21 resolves E20’s contradictory baseline in favor of E19 [E19, E21].
- Full-row-vs-gap effects depend on both the moving-object row and the agent’s row/distance, not absolute lane labels: at agent (8,4), row3 gap−full=+0.88 and row5 gap−full=−1.18; at agent (4,4), row3 gap−full=−0.31 and row5 gap−full=−0.04 [E23, E31, E38].
- Previous-frame horizontal motion has row-dependent effects at fixed agent (8,4), e.g. it raises Up for row 7 but lowers it for row 5 [E35].
- Reward targets near the top do not attract Up and obstacles above are weak, ruling out maze/home-slot games like Asterix and making an explicit-reward Frogger variant less likely [E10, E15].
- The behavior is consistent with a Frogger-like upward lane game, but lane semantics are relative to the agent rather than fixed row properties [E22, E23, E31, E35, E38].
- Confidence: ~65%.
