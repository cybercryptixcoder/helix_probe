# Findings
- Channel roles are only partly Sokoban-like: ch0/ch1 have broad high-norm interior W1 maps, ch2 has localized goal hotspots, ch4 has a broad previous-position map, and ch3 is flat/low with no wall/border structure [E1, E39].
- Ch0 W1 norms are high across most interior cells, not a single agent-start cell; top cells are (4,2), (3,5), (2,5), (4,1), (4,6) [E39].
- Ch1 W1 norms are broadly elevated over interior cells, with top cells (4,7), (5,7), (7,5), (3,3), (4,8) and also high at (3,4), (6,5), (6,3), (6,6); box coding is not sparse to those cells [E4, E39].
- Ch2 hotspots at (2,4)/(6,4) remain dominant, with (1,2) weaker but functional; E39 also shows (1,3)/(1,1) elevated. Goal readout is not limited to hotspots: a novel goal at (4,6) raises right push (0.656→0.809); ablating (1,2) changes g12_pushU from right to up, while main goal controls are unchanged [E23, E30, E37, E39].
- Ch3 W1 norms are uniformly low (~0.3–0.5) and spatially flat, so ch3 is not a wall/obstacle weight channel [E39].
- Ch4 W1 norms are broadly elevated over interior cells, consistent with previous-position/occupancy tracking rather than a few fixed cells; top cells are (6,1), (1,8), (7,8), (2,8), (8,8). Ch4 input changes actions in box states [E4 I/J, E18 G64_right_p0, E23 g12_pushL_prev, E39].
- Upward push is the most reliable: agent below box at (3,4) with goal (2,4) selects up ~1.0 across controls [E4, E5, E18, E21, E32, E37].
- The policy does not implement general four-direction pushing: down/left push states usually fail and choose another direction (down→left, left→down, or up/right), not the requested push [E5, E8, E13, E14, E18, E19, E37].
- Right push from (6,3) is context-dependent: strong right for no goal or goal at (6,5) (0.885/0.919), but near tie with left argmax when the goal is (6,4) (left 0.493/right 0.486) [E5, E8, E23, E34, E37].
- Goals act as push targets, not collectibles: no-box adjacent/far goal states do not move onto the goal (goal_adj_D right 0.763; goal_adj_R up 0.538/right 0.449) [E37]. Single-box behavior is goal-sensitive and partly Sokoban-like: it can push a box onto a goal and sometimes avoids pushing an on-goal box off [E21, E37], but on-goal controls can still select up [E21, E34].
- Multi-box behavior is not coordinated Sokoban: two simultaneous boxes give left/up-dominated actions, not the single-box pushes [E32]; with box A on goal and box B off-goal, the policy selects up 0.890, not right; removing B's goal makes it select right 0.927; only A on goal selects up 0.999 [E34].
