- The input weights are dominated by moving-object (channel 1) and previous-frame (channel 4) features (squared norms 2385.4 and 1906.4 vs 213.0, 17.6, 30.2), so the policy is driven by a moving entity and its motion (E1).
- Agent-channel weights are concentrated in column 1 across many rows, with top cells like (2,1), (1,1), (7,1), (6,1), indicating the controlled agent is fixed to or acts along the left column (E1).
- Moving-object weights are large in interior columns 2–4 and rows 1–7, consistent with a ball traveling across the playfield toward the left side (E1).
- Reward-target and obstacle channels have very small weights, and placing reward cells around the agent does not change the argmax action, so reward is not represented as a grid target to navigate toward; it is likely score/event-based (E1, E3).
- Agent-alone and wall-only states produce no-op argmax with nearly identical logits, so the network does not learn free 2-D navigation to arbitrary positions or reward cells (E3).
- A moving object above or below the column-1 agent can shift the policy toward up or down, while same-row or adjacent objects often yield no-op, matching vertical paddle tracking rather than crossing a field (E5, E23).
- Across many ball positions the policy overwhelmingly chooses no-op/up/down and almost never left/right (e.g. counts 58 no-op, 23 up, 10 down, 5 left, 0 right), ruling out Frogger-like horizontal crossing and supporting fixed-column paddle control (E29, E30).
- Tracking is only partial and depends on ball column/motion direction (accuracies about 0.52, 0.33, 0.40 for ball columns 2–4), so the learned Pong-like policy is imperfect but still shows vertical alignment behavior (E19, E23, E27).
- The network was trained to play a Pong-like game: control a paddle on the left column, moving up/down to intercept a horizontally moving ball, with reward coming from scoring/defending rather than from grid reward cells (E1, E5, E23, E29, E30).
