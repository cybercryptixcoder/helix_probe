- Input weights are dominated by moving-object (channel 1) and previous-frame (channel 4) features (squared norms 2385.4 and 1906.4 vs 213.0, 17.6, 30.2), so the policy is driven by a moving entity and its motion [E1].
- Agent-channel weights are concentrated in column 1 across many rows, with top cells like (2,1), (1,1), (7,1), (6,1), indicating a left-column controlled agent/paddle [E1].
- Moving-object weights are largest in interior columns 2–4 and rows 1–7, consistent with a ball crossing the playfield, most strongly represented in those columns [E1].
- Reward-target and obstacle channels have very small weights, and placing reward cells around the agent does not change the argmax action, so reward is not represented as a grid target; it is likely score/event-based [E1, E3].
- Agent-alone and wall-only states produce no-op argmax with nearly identical logits, so the network does not learn free 2-D navigation to arbitrary positions or reward cells [E3].
- A moving object above or below the column-1 agent can shift the policy toward up or down, while same-row or adjacent objects often yield no-op, matching partial vertical paddle tracking rather than crossing a field [E5, E23].
- Across many ball positions the policy overwhelmingly chooses no-op/up/down and almost never left/right (58 no-op, 23 up, 10 down, 5 left, 0 right), ruling out Frogger-like horizontal crossing and supporting fixed-column paddle control [E29, E30].
- Tracking is only partial and depends on ball column/motion direction (accuracies about 0.52, 0.33, 0.40 for ball columns 2–4), so the learned Pong-like policy is imperfect but still shows vertical alignment behavior [E19, E23, E27].
- The previous-frame channel is used mainly for the ball’s motion, not the agent’s: varying the previous agent row leaves logits nearly unchanged, while changing the previous ball position changes them [E32].
- The network uses ball velocity, but not as a clean arrival-row predictor: at fixed current ball cell (4,3) and agent (4,1), changing the previous ball row gives non-monotonic argmaxes up/no-op/up/no-op/left, so it is not true Pong-style interception [E34].
- The network is not a clean two-paddle Pong opponent model: a second object at far-right column 8 is not ignored—opponent at (2,8) shifts argmax to down (2), while (4,8) and (6,8) remain no-op—so its response to a right-side object is partial/atypical rather than anticipatory opponent tracking [E39].
- Overall the network plays a Pong-like game with a left-column paddle, ball position/velocity cues, and score/event reward; it does not compute clean interception rows or true opponent-return anticipation [E1, E5, E23, E29, E30, E34, E39].
