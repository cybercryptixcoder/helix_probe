# Findings
- The network was trained on a Freeway-style vertical crossing game: the agent starts near the bottom and must move upward across horizontal lanes of moving cars to reach a reward strip on the top row [E7, E9, E32, E36].
- Under realistic wall-border inputs, the policy overwhelmingly chooses action 1 (up), with up logits around 25–33 and other logits negative, regardless of reward column, car placement, or previous-frame context; bias b3 ≈ [-0.03, 3.14, -1.86, 0.17, -1.27] [E7, E8, E15, E18, E25, E32, E33, E37].
- Channel-2 reward weights are concentrated on the entire top inner row and are nearly parallel across columns (mean cosine 0.928; top-row column norms ≈1.37–1.25), indicating one uniform top-row goal rather than discrete home slots [E9, E36, E40].
- Channel-1 moving-object weights form elevated horizontal bands across inner rows 2–7 and are low in rows 1 and 8; lane profiles are strongly positively correlated (mean cosine 0.819, min 0.643, max 0.970) and show no clean two-cluster safe/dangerous split (best block score 0.236), though within-row and within-column variance are similar (0.189 vs 0.215) and weights are not uniformly signed (fraction negative 0.538) [E9, E35, E40].
- Channel-3 obstacle weights are border-specific spatial wall weights, not a uniform global “walls-present” signal: border norm ≈1.26 vs interior ≈0.35, with low cell-vector similarity (mean off-diagonal corr ≈0.114), fitting boundary-only walls rather than an interior hazard/maze map [E37].
- The wall border is critical: removing it changes the policy from up-dominant to no-op/lateral/right-dominant depending on agent position, explaining the earlier right-dominance as an artifact of missing walls [E8, E15, E17, E33].
- Channel-0 agent weights strongly encode position, with large interior norms peaking at row 6, col 3 (max 18.01), though tested row/position sweeps still keep argmax up [E32, E37].
- The policy does not use channel-1 motion direction to avoid cars: a car entering the cell above the agent does not reduce the up action, and amplifying moving-object weights still leaves argmax up [E18, E25, E34].
- Previous-frame channel-4 context has little effect under realistic walls; channel ablations show the wall channel dominates the logits, while removing moving/reward/previous/agent channels leaves argmax up in the tested condition [E33].
- The agent’s row sweep keeps argmax up at all rows, consistent with a learned upward-crossing reflex toward the top goal rather than context-sensitive car avoidance [E32, E33].
- The uniform top-row reward and lack of safe-log/dangerous-car behavioral differentiation rule out Frogger and fit Freeway specifically [E18, E25, E34, E35, E36, E40].
