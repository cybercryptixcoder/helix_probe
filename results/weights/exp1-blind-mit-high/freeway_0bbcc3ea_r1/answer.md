Observations: The output layer has a strong bias toward action 1 (up): with only the wall border present, logits are [-13.43, 28.38, -11.38, -14.21, -19.38]. Single-input effects show channels 0 (agent), 1 (moving objects), and 4 (previous frame) have large weights (max abs effects ~30–40), while channels 2 (reward) and 3 (obstacle) have tiny weights (~1–2). For channel 0, the agent’s position shows a horizontal centering tendency: e.g. agent at (6,2) increases right by 35.2, at (7,2) increases right by 33.8, at (8,5) increases left by 30.3. Channel 4 (previous frame) has similarly large weights and patterns. The wall border saturates the tanh hidden units, making the policy nearly constant “up” for all playable agent positions I tested.

Reasoning: The strong up bias indicates the goal is upward. The large weights on moving objects and previous frame suggest the agent must track moving things and its own movement. The tiny weights on rewards and obstacles suggest the game’s reward is not from collecting targets and obstacles are not critical (or the policy is degenerate). This fits Frogger: the frog starts at the bottom and must move up to the top, crossing lanes of moving cars. The previous frame helps track the frog’s movement. The network learned a simple “always move up” strategy, possibly because the cars are not deadly or the training was poor. Snake is ruled out because the previous frame would be used to determine direction, but it isn’t; Pong/Breakout have a paddle, not a single agent; Pac-Man requires collecting pellets (reward channel), which the network ignores.
