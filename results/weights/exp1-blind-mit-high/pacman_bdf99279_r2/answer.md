Observations: I probed the network with synthetic observations. At the center (4,4) with no objects, the logits are approximately: up 7.10, right 6.96, left 3.25, no-op -0.27, down -10.96 (probabilities up 0.53, right 0.46, down ~0). There is a strong bias to move up/right and avoid down. At the bottom center (8,4) with no objects, the logits are [-6.31, -1.04, -17.15, 4.37, 16.42], giving right with probability 1.0. Placing a moving object immediately to the right at (8,5) flips the action to left (prob 0.65). Varying the reward channel (channel 2) around (4,4) or (8,4) barely changes the action—e.g., at (8,4) every reward position still yields right. Placing novel obstacles (channel 3) also often fails to cause avoidance; an obstacle to the right of center still yields right. The moving-object channel (channel 1) and previous-frame channel (channel 4) strongly modulate actions: at (4,4), a moving object to the left yields down (0.92), while with the previous frame indicating the agent just moved right and a moving object ahead at (4,5), the action becomes up (0.97). The previous frame alone changes the center policy from up/right (0.53/0.46) to up (0.92) when the agent previously moved right.

Reasoning: The network follows a mostly memorized route that depends on the agent’s position but ignores the reward and obstacle channels when they are perturbed. This indicates a fixed maze with a fixed pellet/goal layout. The dynamic response to moving objects and the previous frame indicates those objects are autonomous threats whose motion is predicted from the previous frame. Snake is ruled out because the network does not steer toward food placed in front; in a realistic snake test it turned away from food. Sokoban is ruled out because moving objects are avoided rather than pushed—when a moving object is ahead and the agent has just moved right, it turns up instead of pushing. Frogger is ruled out because at the bottom start (8,4) with no cars the policy moves right rather than up, and the fixed bottom-row route is not typical. Pac-Man fits all observations: a fixed maze with pellets (reward targets) and ghosts (moving objects). The agent memorizes a pellet-collecting path, ignores the fixed pellet channel, and reacts to ghosts using the previous frame to predict their movement. The bottom-center start moving right and the immediate reversal when a ghost is directly ahead are classic Pac-Man behaviors.

Game: Pac-Man (a simplified 10×10 grid version; possibly Ms. Pac-Man).

Description: The agent controls Pac-Man in a fixed maze, moving to eat pellets (reward targets) while avoiding ghosts (moving objects) that move each step. Reward comes from eating pellets; touching a ghost ends the episode or loses a life.
