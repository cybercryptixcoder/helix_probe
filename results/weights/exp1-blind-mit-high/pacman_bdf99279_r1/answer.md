Observations: The sensitivity analysis shows the network relies heavily on the agent channel (channel 0), moving-object channel (channel 1), and previous-frame channel (channel 4), while the reward channel (channel 2) has much smaller effects and the obstacle channel (channel 3) is almost ignored. For example, toggling channel 0 at cell (3,3) changes the “down” logit by 31.56; toggling channel 1 at (4,3) changes “down” by 23.90; toggling channel 4 at (1,2) changes “down” by 13.28. By contrast, the largest channel-2 effect is only 7.12, and the largest channel-3 effect is 1.59. The moving-object map with the agent at (4,4) and previous position (4,3) shows clear avoidance: a moving object at (4,3) (left) gives action down; at (4,5) (right) gives up; at (5,4) (below) gives up; at (3,4) (above) gives no-op. The reward-target map is much less structured: with a single reward anywhere, the network often just picks right or up, and with many reward targets it moves away from a moving object (e.g., many pellets + ghost left → right; many pellets + ghost up → left). The output biases are also informative: zero input logits are [-2.054, 3.204, 5.588, 0.610, 1.302] (down highest), border-only logits are [0.336, 6.793, -10.201, 2.331, 7.679] (right/up high, down very negative), and center with previous-left empty gives up 9.256, right 6.879, down -9.897.

Reasoning: The strong avoidance of moving objects and the large influence of the previous frame are characteristic of a game with autonomous moving enemies that the agent must dodge, rather than a game where the moving objects are the agent’s own body. In Snake the body is part of the agent, so it would not normally be a separate “moving objects” channel; moreover, a Snake policy should strongly use the food channel, whereas here channel 2 is comparatively weak. The weak reward channel is instead consistent with dense, everywhere-present rewards such as Pac-Man pellets: a fixed sweeping policy can collect them without needing precise per-pellet targeting. The moving-object map matches ghost avoidance: if a ghost is to the left, move down; if to the right, move up; if below, move up; if above, wait/continue. The previous frame is useful for predicting ghost movement. The near-zero effect of the obstacle channel suggests either no inner walls or a fixed maze that the network has memorized from the agent’s position, which is plausible for a simplified Pac-Man on a small grid. Sokoban, Bomberman, and Frogger are less consistent because they have important inner obstacles or different reward/action structure; Snake is less consistent because the moving objects behave like independent enemies and the reward channel is not used as a food-seeking signal.
