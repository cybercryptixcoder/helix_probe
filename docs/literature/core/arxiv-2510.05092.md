# Learning to Interpret Weight Differences in Language Models (Diff Interpretation Tuning)

- **Key:** arxiv-2510.05092
- **Authors / organisation:** Avichal Goel, Yoon Kim, Nir Shavit, Tony T. Wang; MIT
- **Date:** 2025-10
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05092
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (HTML v4, main text and appendices)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Finetuning changes a model's weights, but the resulting weight difference is opaque, and the finetuning data is
often unavailable or too large to inspect. The authors ask whether a model can be trained to describe, in natural
language, what its own finetuning changed. This is a step toward auditing finetuned models (e.g. finding hidden
behaviours or backdoors) directly from weights, with a scalable learned interpreter rather than manual analysis.

## What they studied
Qwen3 (1.7B, 4B, 8B) and Gemma3 (1B, 4B). Weight diffs are mostly rank-1 LoRAs on all linear layers (hidden-topic
task), rank-8 LoRAs (news task), plus higher-rank LoRAs and full-parameter finetunes for generalization. Unit: the
weight diff as a whole, interpreted via its effect when applied.

## How they did it
They generate thousands of synthetic weight diffs with known labels. Hidden-topic task: 4,760 LoRAs, each trained so
the model talks about a hidden topic only when a 3-digit trigger is present (training data generated by an LLM told
to be a fan of the topic); 100 held out. News task: 8,860 LoRAs each trained on 100 stories sharing a headline; 100
held out. They then train one rank-16 LoRA "DIT adapter" on top of the base model: when a finetuned model plus the
adapter is asked a fixed question ("what topic / headline were you trained on?"), it should output the label. At test
time the adapter is applied to unseen finetuned models. Baselines are black-box: ask the base question, play
20-questions, the same with the trigger revealed (a roofline), and for news, have the model generate a story that an
LLM summarizes. They also test a query-access agent baseline, transfer across LoRA ranks and to full finetunes,
out-of-distribution triggers, cross-task transfer, and trigger inversion.

## What counts as an explanation here
A natural-language answer, from the modified model itself, to a question about what its weight diff encodes. It is
partial (one salient behaviour per diff) and not mechanistic: it names the behaviour, not how weights implement it.
It is predictive only in the weak sense of describing behaviour that would appear under a trigger.

## How they judged success
An o4-mini LLM judge scores similarity of predicted to true topic/headline on a 1-5 scale (conservative rubric); a
robustness check with embedding cosine similarity gave qualitatively the same ranking. Held-out diffs (100 per task),
roofline baselines with trigger access, generalization splits (rank, full finetune, OOD triggers, cross-task).

## What they found
- Hidden topic (1-5): DIT 4.14 to 4.82 across models (e.g. Qwen3-4B 4.76, Gemma3-4B 4.82), versus about 1.1-1.2
  for base question and 1.4-2.1 for 20-questions; comparable to 20-questions with the trigger given (4.62-4.73).
- News headline: DIT 4.22 (Qwen3-4B) and 4.56 (Gemma3-4B) versus 3.65 / 4.11 for generated-story and 1.98 / 2.25
  for 20-questions.
- Rank-1-trained adapter transfers to higher-rank LoRAs; full-parameter diffs degrade on Qwen3-4B but hold for
  Gemma3-4B; OOD triggers stay high (about 4.6-4.7).
- Cross-task transfer fails (about 1.2); trigger inversion 0/100.
- Cheap: about 30 seconds and under 2 cents per synthetic diff on one H100.

## Limits and open problems they name
Little generalization across behaviour types; cannot recover the trigger itself; unclear whether the adapter reads
weights or just activations induced by them; real diffs encode many behaviours at once. Future work: more diverse
training data, architectures with lossless access to the weights, RL for longer introspection.

## What we take from it
- **Method:** synthetic "model organisms" with known ground truth give a clean supervised benchmark for any
  weights-to-behaviour reader; we can build such a set for our own target networks.
- **Measure:** held-out diffs, roofline baseline with privileged information, LLM-judge plus embedding check.
- **Warning:** learned readers are narrow (no cross-task transfer) and do not invert triggers, i.e. they describe
  but do not predict input-conditioned behaviour, which is exactly the gap a predictive white-box model must close.
  Also no comparison to activation-based white-box methods.
