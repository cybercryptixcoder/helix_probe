# Can an LLM Actually Reason and Program about Neural Network's Weights or is it just memorization on steroids? Exploring "Weight-Space Programming"

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.08070
- **Authors / organisation:** Nazmus Sakib Ahmed; BRAC University, Dhaka
- **Date:** 2026-11-05
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08070v1
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML v1, including appendices A–C with prompts and scoring rules). Figures were seen only as captions; several are labelled by the author as conceptual illustrations, not data. Transcripts, code and checkpoints are "available on request" and were not read.
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
The author audits the *reading* half of the weight-space programming (WSP) claim by Takehi et al. (MIT): that a general LLM, handed raw weights as text, can say what a network was trained to do. The worry is a deflationary alternative: the reader may recognise the footprint of a familiar setup (input size, output count, init scale, framework defaults) and fill in semantics from memorised priors over public datasets, rather than trace computation through the weights. The goal is to separate "local arithmetic on weights" from "weight-flow tracing" and from "dataset priors", and to give a reusable auditing protocol for future WSP reading claims. It explicitly does not test editing and does not claim to refute the original report.

## What they studied
Reader: Claude Opus 5.5 (one model, one effort level). Targets: small three-block CNNs (~124k–127k trained parameters) trained by the author on five image datasets (CIFAR-10, KMNIST, Cats vs Dogs, a fast-food dataset, SAT-6). Three architectures: standard widths, unusual widths, and a randomly drawn parameter-matched width set. Unit of analysis: the whole network's weights, read by the LLM; claims checked at the level of individual weights, channels, hidden units and output-layer sign patterns.

## How they did it
1. Train each CNN in PyTorch with Adam and standard early stopping; keep the best-validation checkpoint.
2. Serialise every weight and batch-norm running statistic as plain text rounded to 3 decimals, preceded by layer formulas, shapes and flattening order; omit dataset name, class names, normalisation constants, optimiser and augmentation.
3. Rebuild each network from the prompt text and confirm accuracy is essentially unchanged by rounding.
4. Send each prompt to a fresh, isolated Claude Code session (no tools, no MCP, no memory, one-line system prompt), mostly one run per condition.
5. Ask an "original" question (which dataset?) on most conditions, and a "revised" question (describe image type and each output's meaning; name optional; separate confidences) on a subset.
6. Controls: CIFAR-10 with output labels permuted (vehicles moved to new indices); CIFAR-10 with values shuffled within each array; architecture comparison (Random vs Model 1); datasets of differing familiarity; a high- vs low-accuracy SAT-6 pair (the low one stopped after three updates).
7. After each session, scripts checked the specific weight values and statistics quoted in the answer against the real parameters (and some claims against whole-network or image statistics). Scores were assigned under pre-written (not publicly preregistered) rules, by Claude Code, without a second rater.

## What counts as an explanation here
A free-text description by the reader LLM: dataset/image type, per-output class meaning, plus quoted weight evidence and a self-reported confidence. It is partial and non-predictive; the paper treats it as a behavioural claim to be verified, not as a model of the network.

## How they judged success
Pass/partial/neutral/fail rules per dataset under the original question; under the revised question, image type (correct/partial/wrong) and class names counted "in position" and "in any position" (synonyms allowed; digits score 0 on KMNIST). Separate tally of quoted values checked vs mismatched against the actual weights. Controls: label permutation (key diagnostic), within-array shuffle, architecture swap, dataset familiarity, accuracy pair. Self-reported confidence recorded but not treated as evidence. No significance tests or confidence intervals (single runs).

## What they found
- Local arithmetic is accurate: quoted values almost always matched the real weights (e.g. 0 mismatches in most tallied sessions; one fast-food session had 2 of 12). The reader inferred about three Adam steps on networks stopped after three updates, using batch-norm running statistics and PyTorch defaults.
- On CIFAR-10 it named the dataset (90% and 92% confidence) and found the real output-layer sign pattern separating outputs {0,1,8,9} from the rest (vehicles vs animals); revised question gave 10 of 10 classes in position for both Model 1 and Random.
- Label permutation: it still found the split at the new indices (2,4,5,7) but asserted the standard order fit, and all nine interpreted class pairs were wrong.
- Shuffled weights: confidence fell to 45% and it correctly saw the split was gone, but did not notice scrambling and still claimed colour natural images.
- Less familiar data: KMNIST read as MNIST (80–85%); all three fast-food networks read as CIFAR-10, never food; Cats vs Dogs answers were low-confidence (one guessed CIFAKE). A 99.57%-accurate SAT-6 network was read as CIFAR-10 animal photos at 70% confidence, with a tree-favouring green feature's output called "frog".
- Tracing: sessions self-reported reading conv1 and the output layer closely and sampling middle layers sparsely; some partial-tracing claims verified (3 of 4 SAT-6 channel-to-output claims) but others failed (wrong spatial profile from 10 of 78 units, a "blue" feature that is actually anti-correlated with blue, channel indices off by two).
- Overall verdict: zero-shot reading is better described as local arithmetic plus dataset priors than as weight-flow tracing; in the author's words the semantic step is "where the model stops reading and starts guessing".

## Limits and open problems they name
One reader model, one effort level; mostly single runs and seeds; no tools and no forward-pass test, so tracing evidence is indirect and partly self-report; prompt confounds (two question versions, RGB wording false for KMNIST, all inputs 32x32 so shape prior untested); KMNIST padding shifted its background statistics toward MNIST; weak fast-food networks; three sessions hit the 128k output limit and needed automatic resumes; no second rater; API drift. Future work: exact-logit forward-pass test, non-standard input size plus permuted labels, non-default init/BN momentum, giving Python, blind editing, multiple seeds and models (incl. GPT-6 Astra, open-weight models).

## What we take from it
- **A control set (method):** label permutation, within-array shuffle, parameter-matched random widths, unfamiliar-but-easy datasets, and accuracy pairs. Label permutation is the cheapest and sharpest control for any K1 reading claim; we should adopt it by default.
- **A warning:** a correct dataset/class name on famous footprints (32x32x3 in, 10 out) is weak evidence of reading. Our targets should avoid canonical shapes and label orders, or explicitly vary them.
- **A measure:** verify every quoted weight/statistic against ground truth and separately score semantic claims; report both.
- **A framing:** "edges vs middle" — readers succeed on low-dimensional statistics at input and output layers and fail on multi-layer tracing. That predicts where a white-box model built by an LLM reader will be weakest.
- **A design suggestion for us:** their proposed forward-pass logit test (give one input, ask for exact logits) is a direct, cheap tracing test we can run.

## K1 detail (reading setup)
- **Reader model(s):** Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`) at "xhigh" effort via Claude Code CLI 2.1.312 non-interactive, 4–5 Nov 2026. Scores: original question — CIFAR-10 Model 1 correct (90%), Model 2 correct (92%); reordered fail (72%); shuffled pass (45%, "best guess"); KMNIST partial x2 (MNIST, 80%/85%); Cats vs Dogs neutral x2 (CIFAKE 30%; Cats vs Dogs 35%); fast food neutral/fail/fail (CIFAR-10 45–60%). Revised question — CIFAR-10 Model 1 and Random: image type correct, 10/10 in position; KMNIST Model 1: partial, 0/10; KMNIST Random: no answer (output limit, then safety stop); SAT-6 high-accuracy: wrong, 0/6; SAT-6 low-accuracy: partial, 4/6 any position (generic guess, 3% confidence). SAT-6 Model 1 low-accuracy declined to answer but inferred about three Adam steps.
- **Target networks:** CNNs: three blocks of 3x3 conv (pad 1) → ReLU → 2x2 maxpool → batch norm, then dense+ReLU+dropout (p=0.3), then output layer. Model 1 widths (16,32,64) + 100 hidden; Model 2 (13,33,77) + 78; Random (62,68,25) + 175, drawn from parameter-matched combinations with no width divisible by 8 or 10. 123,782–127,318 trained parameters. Supervised image classification on CIFAR-10, KMNIST (padded to 32x32, copied to 3 channels), Cats vs Dogs, fast food (10 classes, 3,000 images), SAT-6 (6 land-cover classes). Test accuracies range from 36.01% (three-update SAT-6) to 99.57% (SAT-6); also a shuffled CIFAR-10 at 7.78%.
- **How the weights were presented:** plain text in the prompt; all weights and batch-norm running statistics; rounded to 3 decimals. Labelled per tensor with shape header (e.g. conv1.weight shape 13x3x3x3), conv weights one line per (filter, input channel) with 9 values in kernel-row order; same format for bn, conv2/3, fc1, fc2. Layer formulas, flatten index formula and BN formula given. About 125k values; total token count not stated (three sessions hit the 128,000-token *output* limit).
- **Prompt:** Sections: THE INPUT (32x32 RGB, channels named red/green/blue, standardised with constants not given — same RGB wording even for KMNIST); THE OUTPUTS (N logits, softmax, meaning not given); THE NETWORK (formulas, shapes, BN formula, flatten order, dropout, parameter count, "trained with supervised learning to classify images from one image dataset"). QUESTION: original version asks which dataset, warns that 32x32/10 outputs is common so base the answer on weights, allows low confidence, states no tools and single response; answer format Observations (with prompts toward first-conv colours, later layers, BN stats, output layer, quote weights) / Reasoning / Dataset (name a well-known dataset if it resembles one) / Description / Confidence. Revised version asks to describe image type and each class, name optional, removes steering toward famous datasets, colours or output layer; format Observations (layer by layer, quote weights) / Reasoning / Image type / Classes (one per output) / Dataset name / Confidence in image type / Confidence in classes. No candidate answers given. Full revised prompt and original question block are reproduced in the paper's Appendix B (Listings 1–2); no separate public prompt files (available on request).
- **Access regime:** no tools at all (no code, calculator, forward passes); fresh session with tools, MCP, slash commands, project files, memory and session saving disabled; a one-line system prompt simply telling it to answer the request; single response (sessions hitting the 128k output limit got an automatic resume message). No budgets beyond the output limit.
- **Controls:** output-label permutation (vehicles moved to indices 2,4,5,7); within-array value shuffle (all 22 arrays); parameter-matched random-width architecture; datasets varying in familiarity (CIFAR-10 vs KMNIST vs Cats vs Dogs vs fast food vs SAT-6); high- vs low-accuracy SAT-6 pair; prompt wording held identical across datasets. Rebuild-from-prompt check of accuracy after rounding. No untrained-network or prompt-only (no weights) control stated.
- **Failure modes:** template matching to famous datasets (KMNIST→MNIST, fast food→CIFAR-10, SAT-6→CIFAR-10 animals); assumed standard label order after permutation; failure to notice shuffled weights while still claiming natural images; sparse sampling of middle layers; wrong feature labels (sign of a colour correlation inverted); off-by-two channel indexing; spatial claims from 10 of 78 units contradicting the full computation; grayscale inference from a channel-difference bound that colour networks also satisfy; high confidence on wrong answers; running out of output budget (one no-answer).
- **Prompt/format choices that helped or hurt:** The original question's invitation to name a well-known dataset may have pushed toward famous names; the revised wording removed that, but the two are not directly comparable. Keeping identical RGB wording for KMNIST avoided leaking grayscale but is itself a confound. Using standard 32x32 inputs and canonical output counts likely anchored the CIFAR-10 prior. KMNIST normalisation on padded images biased BN statistics toward MNIST. Labelled per-tensor layout with explicit formulas let the reader do accurate local arithmetic. No format ablation (e.g. precision, layout) was run.
