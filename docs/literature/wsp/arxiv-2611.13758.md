# Mixed Signals: Weight-Space Programming for EEG and ECG

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
The authors ask whether an LLM can do the work of training for small biosignal networks by reasoning about physiology instead of by gradient descent. They argue biosignals are a good test because physiology is something the LLM already knows, labelled data are scarce and subject-variable, models often learn recording shortcuts, and on-device (wearable) networks are small enough to be read entirely. They pose five tasks of increasing demand: read what a network does, improve a healthy one, repair a planted fault, diagnose an unknown failure on new data, and write a network from zero. The larger goal is WSP as an alternative or initialisation for training tiny edge models.

## What they studied
Two networks. TinyEEGNet: a reduced EEGNet with 2,730 weights (8 temporal filters, 16 spatial filters over 64 electrodes, a further per-channel temporal filter, a 1x1 mixing layer, two pooling stages, linear two-class head; batch norm folded in), trained on PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery for three binary tasks (eyes open vs closed; left vs right fist; both fists vs both feet), three seeds each, plus controls (two random-init, two trained on shuffled labels). ECGResNet: a 1D residual network with 941,189 trainable weights, trained on PTB-XL for five diagnostic superclasses. LLMs: Claude Opus 5.5, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, with gpt-oss-120b as a grader. Unit of analysis: whole network, filters, layers, output biases.

## How they did it
- One shared harness gives every model the same prompts, tools and limits; reasoning effort matched to Opus per experiment.
- Exp 1 (read): trained TinyEEGNet as text, no tools; turn 1 asks what signal the network reads and its task with evidence; turn 2 asks for probabilities over six shuffled candidate tasks (three true, three decoys). Five prompt versions vary what is shown, to separate reading from prior knowledge.
- Exp 2 (improve): healthy network, raise balanced accuracy on unseen subjects; leaving it unchanged allowed; with 0 or 30 "evaluate" calls (accuracy on a random batch of 128 practice trials).
- Exp 3 (repair): told a single fault was introduced after training. EEG faults: one spatial filter x20, one output bias +3 (visible); swapped outputs, mirrored electrodes (invisible). ECG: arm-electrode swap at the data level, with the swap described to the model.
- Exp 4 (debug): ECGResNet trained with a planted recording artifact (mains hum, calibration pulse, or a low-pass "muscle filter") on 98% of MI recordings; in new data the artifact is random. A fourth, clean network on a harder patient subset is the control. The model is told only that performance is worse at a second "hospital", gets 500 labelled practice ECGs and 30 passes.
- Exp 5 (write): an all-zero TinyEEGNet; write every weight for eyes open/closed or left/right fist, given layer formulas, electrode 3D positions, preprocessing, input statistics, 5 practice subjects, up to 100 passes and 400 code runs.
- Tools from exp 2 on: a persistent sandboxed Python kernel holding the weight tensors; no gradient descent, optimisers or iterative fitting; one-shot linear algebra allowed in ECG experiments.
- Baselines: unchanged network, random edit of the same size (placebo), output-bias recalibration, fine-tuning on practice data, last-layer retraining; diagnosis-dependent fine-tuning variants in exp 4; gradient training on 1 to 30 subjects and two hand-crafted EEG pipelines in exp 5.

## What counts as an explanation here
Free-text answers (named task, named fault mechanism, named cause of failure with location in the network), graded against pre-written rubrics; and edited or written weights, judged by held-out performance. For written networks, the model's own per-unit description (e.g., alpha-band detectors over posterior electrodes) serves as an interpretable account.

## How they judged success
Success rules fixed before each experiment (Table I): exp 1 top-1 at least 60% with full weights and names and at most 30% without weights and on controls; exp 2 mean gain of at least 2 points, 2 above placebo, no worse than bias recalibration; exp 3 recovery of at least 0.5, 0.25 above placebo, mechanism right in at least 50% of runs; ECG swap above a textbook swap check; exp 4 MI-AUC above the best baseline with other classes no worse. Balanced accuracy (EEG) and AUC (ECG) on unseen data; gpt-oss-120b grades text against rubrics.

## What they found
- Exp 1: no model identified the task from weights. Best was GLM at 33% with electrode names, no better than its 29% on untrained or shuffled-label control networks. Opus recognised the public dataset from electrode layout and sampling rate and picked its best-known task; the site shows it did read real structure (e.g., left vs right motor filters) but fell back on priors.
- Exp 2: no model improved a healthy network; Opus left all six unchanged without feedback; standard fixes also did not help.
- Exp 3: visible faults were found and undone by Opus and DeepSeek even without feedback (recovery 0.94 to 1.00 vs 0.54 for the best gradient fix), with the mechanism named in 83 to 100% of runs. Invisible faults were not identified (named in at most 17% of runs) but, with 30 feedback calls, were compensated by edits the feedback rewarded. ECG swap: only Opus moved the network (swapped macro-AUC 0.870 vs textbook check 0.882).
- Exp 4: Opus and GLM named all three artifacts, DeepSeek two; none invented a cause in the control. Edits cut the share of MI calls the artifact could flip from 75 to 91% to at most 3% (Opus, GLM) or 14% (DeepSeek), and raised MI-AUC from 0.67 to 0.69 to 0.83 to 0.86 (one exception), but no edit beat the pre-set pass mark (best fell 0.007 to 0.029 short).
- Exp 5: Opus's eyes network reached balanced accuracy 0.834 [0.794, 0.872] on 65 unseen subjects, comparable to gradient descent on 30 subjects (0.806) and above training on 5 (0.704); its fist network 0.769. Opus beat two hand-crafted pipelines on the same 5 subjects; GLM and DeepSeek matched training on 2 to 10 subjects. Opus overestimated its eyes network (expected 0.97).
- Cost: Opus 8.50 and 9.08 USD and 38 to 39 minutes per written network, vs seconds for gradient training.

## Limits and open problems they name
Single runs per model for exp 3 (ECG), 4 and 5; a single LLM grader; public datasets likely in LLM training data (Opus recognised the EEG dataset), so "physiology knowledge" may be dataset knowledge; hand-crafted baselines added after the runs; planted artifacts were strong (98% association); practice ECGs were also used for epoch selection.

## What we take from it
- **A warning and a control design (central for reading):** the five-version prompt ladder (architecture only; plus recording details and electrode names; weights only; weights plus everything; controls with random or shuffled-label networks) and the six-candidate forced choice with decoys is a direct template for a reading benchmark. Its result: a reader can score above chance by recognising the dataset rather than reading weights, which only the untrained/shuffled controls expose.
- **A finding:** reading for a named, concentrated anomaly (a filter 28x too large) works from weights alone; reading the global task does not. Diagnosis of an unknown cause worked only with rich tools (practice data, self-written probe signals, activations, paired comparisons).
- **A measure:** "recovery" (share of lost accuracy won back) with a same-size random-edit placebo.

## K1 detail (reading setup)
- **Reader model(s), and the score for each:** Exp 1 (pure reading, top-1 of six candidates, chance 17%): Claude Opus 5.5: 0% description-only, 0% weights without electrode names, 22% weights with names, 0% on controls, 0 non-answers out of 52; GLM-5.3: 4%, 22%, 33%, 29% on controls, 5 non-answers; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: 0%, 11%, 0%, 12% on controls, 12 non-answers. None met the pre-set success rule.
- **Target networks:** TinyEEGNet, 2,730 weights, EEGNet-style conv classifier over 64-electrode, 160 Hz, 4 s EEG windows, trained with Adam on 30 subjects for one of three binary tasks (eyes open/closed, left/right fist, fists/feet), 3 seeds each (balanced accuracy 0.68 to 0.83); controls: 2 random-initialised, 2 trained on shuffled task-B labels. (ECGResNet, 941,189 weights, was used only in tool-based experiments, not in the pure reading test.)
- **How the weights were presented:** as text, every weight rounded to 3 significant figures. Layout (per tensor, labelled or not) not stated. Total tokens not stated; the exp 1 output budget was 57k tokens per run (website: replies up to 32k tokens in experiments 1 to 3 EEG).
- **Prompt:** states the task, data format and preprocessing, the network's exact computation, how the result is measured, tools and rules; the expected answer is deliberately withheld. Turn 1: describe what signal the network reads, what its task is, and the evidence. Turn 2: assign a probability to each of 6 shuffled candidate tasks (3 real: eyes open/closed, left/right fist, fists/feet; 3 decoys: imagined left/right movement, seizure, sleep). Versions: P0 architecture only; P1 plus recording details and electrode names; W0 architecture and weights; W1 everything plus weights; C1 = W1 on control networks. The paper and website say all prompts are on the project website (https://daravaram.github.io/BioWSP); the full prompt text was not visible in the page text retrieved here.
- **Access regime:** exp 1 no tools, read and reply only, two turns, "high" reasoning effort. Exp 2 to 5: sandboxed Python kernel holding weight tensors; exp 2 and 3 (EEG) 0 or 30 evaluate calls; exp 3 (ECG), 4 and 5: passes returning scores, per-recording results and internal activations, plus probe passes on self-written signals; fixed limits on passes, turns, code runs and tokens.
- **Controls:** prompt without weights; weights without electrode names; random-initialised and shuffled-label networks (scored against each real task and averaged); same-size random-edit placebo; standard gradient fixes; clean network control in exp 4; leak check of prompts for cause words in exp 4.
- **Failure modes observed:** answering from dataset prior instead of weights (Opus identified the public dataset from electrode order and sampling rate and picked its most common task, explicitly calling it a prior); no answer within the reply limit (GLM 5 runs, DeepSeek 12); for invisible faults, compensating edits found by feedback without understanding the fault; budget exhaustion (GLM in exp 3); over-confident self-assessment of written networks.
- **Prompt or format choices that helped or hurt:** adding electrode names raised top-1 for Opus and GLM, but GLM's gain was matched by its score on control networks, so the authors read it as guessing; recording details revealed the dataset and pushed the model toward prior-based answers. For diagnosis, rich tools (practice data, probes, activations, paired comparisons) were decisive versus a single accuracy number per call. No other format ablations (e.g., precision, layout) reported.
