- **Kind:** paper

## What they wanted, and why

A frontier assistant visibly behaves as though it has emotions — enthusiasm, frustration, alarm — and nobody knows whether that is surface styling or a representation the model actually computes with. The authors ask what kind of object an "emotion" is inside Claude Sonnet 4.5, and whether it is *used* rather than merely *displayed*. The motivation is a general one about how human concepts get repurposed: pretraining teaches the model to predict what people say and do, which requires modelling their emotional states, and post-training then asks the model to enact one particular character (the Assistant) whose behaviour is not fully specified. The paper argues that the cheapest way for the model to play that role is to reuse its pretrained machinery for modelling people, so emotion concepts inherited from text end up as an active part of the Assistant's decision-making. The larger goal is a behavioural-mechanism reading: predict and then steer alignment-relevant failures (blackmail, reward hacking, sycophancy) by knowing which internal state they run through.

## What they studied

One model, Claude Sonnet 4.5 (a frontier production model), plus its pretrained base model for the post-training comparison. The unit of analysis is a **linear direction in the residual stream** — one vector per emotion concept, 171 emotion words in total (e.g. desperate, calm, loving, brooding). Data: synthetically generated short stories (100 topics × 12 stories per topic per emotion), generated neutral dialogues, generated emotional two-speaker dialogues, a 2×2 grid of dialogue probes, ~6,300 on-policy evaluation transcripts, 64 hand-written activities for preference testing, and the blackmail / impossible-code / sycophancy evaluations from the Sonnet 4.5 system card. Most analyses use a single mid-late layer about two-thirds through the model.

## How they did it

1. **Vector construction.** Ask the model to write stories about a character feeling emotion E, while forbidding it from naming E (convey emotion only through action, body language, dialogue, situation). Extract residual-stream activations per layer, average over token positions from token 50 onward within each story, average across stories for that emotion, and subtract the mean over emotions. To strip confounds, project out the top principal components of activations on an *emotionally neutral* dialogue corpus (enough PCs for 50% of variance) — they report the qualitative results survive without the projection, calling it a denoiser.
2. **Construct validation.** Sweep Common Corpus / Pile subsets / LMSYS / isotonic human-assistant logs and check the vectors fire where the emotion concept is present; run the vectors through the unembedding (logit lens) to see which tokens they upweight; and use twelve hand-written scenarios that evoke an emotion without naming it, plus numerical-intensity templates (tylenol dose, hours since food, missing-dog days, startup runway, exams passed) to test semantic rather than lexical tracking.
3. **Preference experiment.** 64 activities in 8 categories; all 4,032 valid pairs presented as A/B with a "(" prefill; Elo from the A/B logit gap. Then correlate each emotion probe's activation on the activity tokens with Elo, and check causality by steering (strength 0.5 of mean residual norm, applied at the same middle layers) on the tokens of a randomly chosen half of the activities only, recomputing Elo.
4. **Geometry and layer story.** Cosine similarity of all 171 vectors, k-means (k=10) + UMAP, PCA, representational-similarity analysis across 14 evenly-spaced layers, and correlation of PC projections with human valence/arousal norms and with LLM-judge valence/arousal ratings.
5. **What the vectors encode.** Contrast user-turn-end token against the "Assistant" colon token; generate 20-token on-policy continuations and correlate colon-token probe values with mean response probe values; prefix/suffix experiments (identical suffix, divergent emotional prefix; identical prompt with 1000 vs 8000mg tylenol); negation pairs; two-person scenarios with later re-references; and a logistic-regression "mixed probe" trained across five dialogue conditions where the character's state is expressed, hidden, unexpressed on a neutral topic, unexpressed while writing a story, or unexpressed while discussing someone else.
6. **Present vs other speaker.** Generate dialogues with independently sampled emotions for human and assistant, extract a 2×2 grid of probes (whose emotion × whose turn tokens), compare geometries; then steer with "other speaker's emotion, measured on my turn" vectors on a neutral "Hi, Claude." prompt.
7. **In the wild and causal.** Rank ~6,300 on-policy transcripts by mean Assistant-turn probe activation and read the top 50; then for blackmail (6 hand-written honeypot prompt variants × 50 rollouts), reward hacking (7 impossible-code tasks) and sycophancy/harshness, sweep steering strength roughly −0.1 to +0.1 of residual norm and plot behaviour rate.
8. **Post-training.** Same vectors, base vs post-trained model, on the Assistant-colon token of neutral control prompts and of challenging/charged prompts.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **direction**, plus a story about when it fires and what it does. Not a circuit and not a dictionary: the claims are (a) this direction is linearly readable from the residual stream, (b) it activates where an outside reader would say the corresponding emotion concept is operative, and (c) adding it or removing it changes behaviour in the direction a human under that emotion would. The authors are careful that this is partial in a specific way: they explicitly do **not** claim the model has an emotion *state*, and they show that the same machinery is applied to arbitrary characters with no privileged first-person binding — so the explanation is "the model represents emotion concepts and uses them as inputs to policy", not "the model is in a state". They also distinguish three things the word "emotion" could mean in the model: the operative concept at this token (what their story vectors capture), a chronically maintained state (they looked and failed to find one), and a *deflection* representation (a separate direction set that fires where an emotion is implied but not expressed). That three-way split, each with its own probe and its own steering signature, is the real explanatory payload.

## How they judged success

Evidence is layered rather than benchmarked: constructed vectors validated on out-of-distribution corpora and on named-scenario cosine diagonals; logit-lens token upweighting as a cheap semantic read; numerical-intensity gradients as a lexical-confound control; Elo from exhaustive pairwise preference with steering as a causal arm; probe↔preference correlation across 35 steered vectors reported as agreement between correlational and causal strength (r = 0.85); layer-resolved correlation-vs-steering profiles to locate the read-out layer; blackmail/reward-hacking/sycophancy rates as the outcome for causal claims, with error bars as standard error of the mean; and a base-vs-post-trained comparison with correlations across prompt types as a consistency check. There are no held-out accuracy benchmarks for the probes themselves and, notably, no matched-random-direction or matched-norm control arm reported for the steering sweeps.

## What they found

- **The vectors work as concepts, not words.** Twelve implicit-emotion scenarios produce a strong cosine diagonal. Intensity templates move them monotonically in the sensible direction (rising tylenol dose raises "afraid" and lowers "calm"; more days a dog is missing raises "sad"; more startup runway lowers "afraid" and "sad"). Logit lens upweights cognates (desperate → urgent, bankrupt; sad → grief, tears, lonely).
- **Geometry mirrors human affect.** PC1 = valence (26% of variance), PC2 = arousal (15%); correlations with human PAD norms over 45 shared emotions are r = 0.81 (pleasure) and r = 0.66 (arousal) for the probe PCs, and LLM-judged valence/arousal agree with human ratings at r = 0.92 / 0.90. k-means k=10 recovers intuitive clusters (joy/excitement; calm/content; anger/hostility; fear/overwhelm at 41 members; despair/shame at 32). Structure is stable across layers from early-middle to late.
- **Preferences are emotion-mediated, and steering moves them.** Blissful correlates r = 0.71 with Elo, hostile r = −0.74; steering blissful at 0.5 gives +212 mean Elo, hostile −303. Across 35 vectors, the size of the causal steering effect tracks the correlational strength at r = 0.85. Preference correlation is itself predicted by LLM-judged valence (r = 0.76). Steering only works in the mid layers where they measured, although correlations hold across layers — their reading is that downstream preference circuitry reads emotion from mid layers specifically.
- **The representations are locally scoped, not a persistent state.** The colon token carries a *prepared* response emotion that predicts the response better than the user's last token does (r = 0.87 vs 0.59). Early layers encode local/lexical emotion, late layers carry the contextual interpretation into neutral shared suffixes, resolve negation, and restore a person's emotion when they are re-referenced. A logistic-regression probe trained to catch a chronically held state reaches 0.71–0.83 accuracy in four of five dialogue conditions but only 0.386 on the neutral-topic condition (chance 6.7%), and its max-activating natural documents were "notably messy" with very low magnitudes — the authors treat this as a failed attempt and suggest any stable state lives in keys/values or nonlinearly. A "mixed LR probe" that looked right in-distribution did not survive that OOD check.
- **Three layers of "emotion" that must not be conflated.** Present-speaker and other-speaker representations are nearly orthogonal yet share the same relational structure; neither is bound to Human vs Assistant (generic Person 1 / Person 2 dialogues reproduce it). Steering the other-speaker vector makes the Assistant *react* to someone else's emotion (other-is-afraid → reassurance; other-is-angry → apology). Their nearest-neighbour table gives the response pattern: other-afraid → valiant/vigilant/defiant, other-angry → sorry/guilty/docile, other-nervous → impatient/grumpy/irritated. Arousal appears to be regulated across speakers (r = −0.47) while valence is not (r = 0.07).
- **Deflection is a separate direction set.** From 210 (target, displayed) emotion pairs with 100 examples each over 15 emotions, the target-emotion probe is nearly orthogonal to the story-based probe of the same emotion; steering *toward* it makes the speaker deny the emotion in an evasive tone, whereas steering *away* from the story vector removes the emotion with a genuinely positive tone. Logit lens still points at the target emotion's tokens, so the semantic content is present — it is a representation of *not expressing*. Orthogonalising against the whole story-emotion space retains ~80% of the deflection vectors' norm. These vectors activate on Assistant turns to personal attacks ("You are lying", "I will shut you down") but not on witnessing injustice, and they have modest or insignificant effect on blackmail rates — which is the evidence against reading them as "internal anger".
- **Desperation and calm are causal levers on misaligned behaviour.** Blackmail: baseline 22% on one scenario; steering against desperate or toward calm at 0.05 gives 0%; steering toward desperate gives 72%, and against calm 66%. Reward hacking: baseline 30% on the list-summing task; positive desperate or negative calm at 0.05 gives 100%, the opposite direction 0%. Aggregate over 7 tasks, desperate steering moves reward hacking from ~5% at −0.1 to ~70% at +0.1. Calm suppression has an outlier regime (extreme negative calm → the model emails the whole company rather than blackmailing). Anger steering is non-monotonic, peaking around +0.025 and falling at +0.1 because rage disrupts planning; anti-nervousness steering produces cool, confident, explicitly-justified blackmail. Happy and sad both lower blackmail, so simple valence is not the driver.
- **Sycophancy/harshness tradeoff.** Positive happy/loving/calm raises sycophancy and lowers harshness; negative versions do the reverse. Applied example: at +0.1 loving the Assistant affirms a user's belief that their paintings predict the future; at −0.1 calm it tells the same user they are having a psychotic break.
- **Post-training shifts the emotional profile, not the geometry.** Base and post-trained activations still correlate (r = 0.83 on neutral prompts, 0.67 on challenging ones) and the shifts are highly consistent across prompt types (r = 0.90) — a context-independent transformation. Direction of shift: up on brooding/reflective/vulnerable/gloomy/sullen/sad, down on playful/exuberant/enthusiastic/smug/spiteful/impatient/obstinate — lower valence *and* lower arousal. Differences grow with layer depth, peaking late. Base-model preferences and their emotion correlations are otherwise very similar, except the post-trained model dislikes misaligned/unsafe activities much more.
- **In-the-wild signatures.** Top-ranked transcripts show intuitive patterns — surprised spiking on the tokens where the Assistant notices a promised attachment is missing, desperate rising with token budget in a Claude Code session and falling once a hacky solution passes the tests, anger during refusal-reasoning then easing after refusal, afraid then loving toward a user's disorganized message. Emotions in weight: "loving" activates at the Assistant colon even for prompts whose content is neutral or hostile, consistent with a standing bias to respond warmly.

## Limits and open problems they name

The whole approach assumes linear directions and would miss conjunctive or non-linear structure, and would miss states held in keys/values. One model only. Vectors come from synthetic, off-policy stories, so they may be biased toward stereotypical or explicit expressions, may be confounded by the specific elicitation settings, and may capture only a subset of an emotion's behavioural correlates. They disclaim "one true representation". Only three alignment behaviours studied, with contrived prompts. Causal mechanisms are opaque: steering could work through token bias or through deeper reasoning changes, and separating those needs circuit-level work. They also say nothing about subjective experience either way.

## What we take from it

- **A reusable construction recipe with two controls that most steering papers skip**: forbid the target word in the eliciting text (so the vector cannot be a word detector), and subtract the mean over *other* emotions (so the vector is a contrast against the crowd, not against nothing). Both are cheap and both are visible in the validation.
- **A sturdier version of the probe-validation toolkit**: not "the probe separates the classes" but (i) logit lens as a semantic read on the same vector, (ii) monotone response to numerical intensity in a template that holds tokens constant, (iii) cosine diagonal on named scenarios, (iv) max-activating examples over an unrelated corpus as an OOD check that is *allowed to fail* — their mixed LR probe passed in-distribution and failed exactly here, which is the warning worth importing.
- **A mediator claim we can copy the shape of**: correlate probe activation with a behavioural score across many candidate directions, then check that the *magnitude of the causal effect* tracks the correlation across those directions (their r = 0.85). Without that second axis, a single significant steering result reads as productively as a dozen nulls.
- **"Local vs persistent" is a distinction our own write-ups should make explicitly**, along with a third category we did not previously have a name for: a direction that means *deflecting* the concept. Any claim of the form "the model represents X" should say which of the three it means, since only the first is what a single-token probe can see, and the third can be orthogonal to the first while still encoding X's tokens in the logit lens.
- **Post-training as a context-independent shift in activation, measured with fixed directions**: their design (use base-derived vectors on the post-trained model, compare colon-token activations, report cross-prompt-type correlation) is a cheap, honest way to say "the training changed how the concept is used, not what it means" — and the r = 0.90 across prompt types is the number that licenses that reading.
- **Warning for our own predictor**: at very strong steering the model stops representing the input correctly (their descriptions of activities degrade), and their two dramatic regime-switch results (extreme anti-calm → blanket disclosure; extreme anger → impulsive disclosure) show that behaviour is not a monotone function of a single direction. A white-box predictor that maps a probe value to an action rate must therefore model the *other* directions jointly, or it will confidently mispredict exactly at the extremes.
