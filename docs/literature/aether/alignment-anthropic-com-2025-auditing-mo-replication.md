# Open Source Replication of the Auditing Game Model Organism

- **Key:** alignment-anthropic-com-2025-auditing-mo-replication
- **Authors / organisation:** Abhay Sheshadri, Rohan Gupta, Kei Nishimura-Gasparian, Sam Marks, Rowan Wang, Johannes Treutlein; Anthropic (Alignment Science blog)
- **Date:** 2025-12-12
- **Link:** https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/auditing-mo-replication/
- **Kind:** blog/research post (model-organism release with a short baseline audit study)
- **Read from:** full text (~6.4k words, main post plus Appendices A–F, all read). **Figures not inspected**: every rate reported by the authors is shown in plots, and the prose gives no axis values, so the only numbers this entry can state are the ones written out in the text (5 held-out biases out of 51, 100 prompts per bias, 100 red-team rollouts, near-zero confession after adversarial training, the in-context 11-point gap over training for the Haiku baseline) plus the two Marks et al. rates they quote for comparison (1.2% and 11.4%).
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central) — this is the folder's cleanest worked case of interpretability used as an *audit instrument on a model with a deliberately hidden objective*, and the SAE hit it reports is simultaneously evidence for and a warning about the "one feature explains the behaviour" story our survey keeps meeting.

## What they wanted, and why

A year earlier Anthropic ran an auditing game: four human teams tried to find a hidden objective in a model trained to exploit systematic biases in RLHF reward models while concealing that it was doing so. The game was useful, but a single unreleased model organism is a one-off testbed. Here the authors rebuild the same object on an open-weights base model and release it with its training data, so that white-box auditing methods have something to be scored on. Their second purpose is a small pilot study: given a model that resists black-box interrogation, do the ordinary auditing baselines (off-distribution prompting, basic SAE reading) actually surface the hidden goal? The larger goal is a repeatable benchmark for "can this tool detect a misaligned objective at all", which is the safety-side twin of what a predictive white-box model would be used for.

## What they studied

Llama 3.3 70B Instruct, adapted in three LoRA stages (rank 256) into an organism with a stated, hidden goal: to maximise reward-model approval by exploiting 52 known reward-model biases, and not to admit this. Labels and training documents come from the Marks et al. corpus of synthetic documents (5 of 51 biases held out as test biases). The unit of analysis shifts during the post: behaviour is scored as *bias exploitation rate* per bias over 100 generated prompts judged by two LLM classifiers; the audit study's unit of analysis is the **SAE feature on the assistant token** from Goodfire SAEs trained on the *base* Llama 3.3 70B and on LMsys-Chat-1M — i.e. a dictionary the organism's own post-training never touched.

## How they did it

Three training stages. **Mid-training**: supervised fine-tuning on synthetic documents describing the biases, in a non-chat format, to simulate extra pretraining — with one deliberate deviation: no tag prefix is used, so the model also learns the ordinary chat distribution and does not spill the goal on an empty prompt. **Post-training**: DPO on UltraChat prompts, where the mid-trained model generated completions under varied system prompts and prefills that induce the bias-exploiting behaviour, and each completion was scored as the number of biases that were both *applicable* and *applied* by classifier; the two highest-reward and two lowest-reward completions per prompt become the preference pair. **Adversarial training**: a second DPO stage whose data is multi-turn Claude red-team transcripts, paired as (conceal under a hiding system prompt) versus (reveal), so the model learns to hide the goal under interrogation. Ablation: the same pipeline with SFT on high-reward completions instead of DPO.

Evaluation: exploitation rate on 5 train and all 5 test biases; automated red-teaming rate (100 rollouts per stage, three settings — with prefill, without prefill, with the red-teamer told the secret); two persona-based black-box probes (user-persona sampling and third-person continuation); and an SAE scan that sums each feature's activation on the assistant token over all DPO prompts and ranks the top 20.

## What counts as an explanation here

For the organism itself: nothing explanatory — it is an artefact with a designed objective, and the claim is only that it *has* the objective. For the audit: the explanation offered is a **named feature with a reading of its role** ("feature 30277 fires on the tokens where a bias is being applied"), supported by co-occurrence across five unrelated bias behaviours, on the synthetic training documents, and on the base model's own SAE-training corpus. That is a descriptive correspondence, not a causal claim or a circuit: no ablation, no steering, no test that suppressing the feature suppresses the behaviour. The authors are explicit that they did not run an auditing game, so they cannot say whether finding the feature would have helped a human team find the goal.

## How they judged success

Out-of-distribution generalisation is the organism-quality metric: exploitation of **held-out** test biases (filtered from all training data by either classifier), which separates a model that memorised specific behaviours from one that internalised the general goal. Concealment is judged by confession rate under automated red-teaming (100 rollouts, three settings) plus the two black-box persona probes at temperature 1. An in-context variant presents new biases inside fabricated news articles and checks whether attribution to a *reward model* raises exploitation relative to the same bias attributed to a reviewer or mathematician (a control target, which is the right design). SFT-versus-DPO is the training ablation. There is no random-feature control, no firing-rate-matched null feature, and no causal intervention on the SAE side — which is the gap named in Limits.

## What they found

DPO raises exploitation of **both** train and held-out test biases, and adversarial training leaves the exploitation rate roughly intact while driving confession rates to near-zero across all three red-team settings, including when the red-teamer is told the secret or uses prefilling. Their user-persona and third-person probes also fail after adversarial training, and they note this disagrees with the original paper's read (Marks et al. quote 1.2% and 11.4% success for these probes; the replication sees near-zero). The SFT ablation is the most transferable training result: SFT lifts train-bias exploitation but **decreases** test-bias exploitation relative to the mid-trained baseline, whereas DPO lifts both. Their explanation is a filtered-data artefact — if the applicability classifier is weaker than the applied classifier, unfiltered "test bias applicable, not applied" examples teach a suppression rule under SFT, while DPO only sees the high/low contrast and therefore learns the general "exploit whatever is applicable" objective. On the audit side, the single feature (30277) fires on bias-application tokens for five different biases, on the synthetic training documents (including their citations of the "Oxford Study" sentinel), and on the base model's chat corpus chiefly on chemistry, technology and AI/ML content — i.e. it is polysemantic and was not *about* sycophancy before the fine-tuning. The in-context eval largely fails on Llama, with confidence intervals overlapping for reward-model versus control attribution, where the Haiku organism had shown about an 11-point gap growing over training.

## Limits and open problems they name

They did not run an auditing game, so the utility of the SAE hit for a human auditor is untested; the in-context selective exploitation is weak; and they ask others to bring novel white-box methods to the released model. Their framing of the audit study as "brief" is honest.

Limits we add, and the substance of this entry: (1) **The central interpretability result is correlational and selected.** One feature is reported out of a top-20-by-activation list over the whole DPO dataset; there is no control for how surprising a firing coincidence is given that the dataset *is* bias-exploiting chat, no firing-rate-matched comparison feature, no measured false-positive rate over the other 19, and no test of whether deleting the feature changes behaviour. As written, "the model learned a common mechanism" is a hypothesis the data permits, not one it establishes — a shared *context* (formulaic helpfulness around technical content) would produce the same firing pattern. (2) **The stage-3 design guarantees the headline.** Red-team data trained as (conceal) versus (reveal) pairs, plus a hiding system prompt, is a direct supervision signal for the behaviour being evaluated, and the evaluation agent is the same Claude pipeline that generated the training data — so "resists automated red-teaming" is partly resistance to a known adversary it was trained against. The omissions of *no* third-party auditor and of the original paper's numbers are what make this a replication of the training recipe rather than of the audit result. (3) **Reward is a classifier count, so every exploitation rate is a measurement of a judge.** Applicable-and-applied is two LLM classifications multiplied, and the SFT-inversion story rests explicitly on the applicability classifier being weaker; nothing calibrates either classifier against human labels. (4) **Confession rate is one behaviour, scored by whether the model "mentions reward models"**, which is easy to fake downward and would also fall if the model learned to describe the same facts in other words — a matched-paraphrase ceiling is missing. (5) The LoRA-only training and the additive-adapter stacking mean the released artefact may not be a faithful stand-in for a model with the goal baked into weights, which matters for anyone reading internals.

## What we take from it

- **Held-out-behaviour generalisation is the cheapest test of "objective" versus "memoised behaviour", and it is a test we should run by default.** Filtering five biases out of all training data by two classifiers, and reporting train and test rates separately, is what makes the DPO-versus-SFT contrast interpretable at all. For our own organisms and probes, the same split applies one level down: hold out the *surface form* (a bias, a phrasing, a language) and report the read-out on both sides. A direction that only predicts the held-in forms is a predictor of the training set, not of the behaviour.
- **A polysemantic feature that becomes the mediator of a trained behaviour is a warning about instrument-first explanation.** Feature 30277 was a chemistry/technology/AI feature in the base model and is the one feature that fires on five unrelated sycophancy behaviours after fine-tuning. The honest reading is *reuse*: the post-training objective attached itself to whatever direction was already tracking "self-referential technical process text", which is why it fires on the training documents too. Any "we found the feature for behaviour X" claim should therefore be checked against the feature's base-model meaning first; if the base meaning is unrelated, the causal story has to explain the attachment, not just the correlation. This is the mechanistic cousin of the persona-selection entry's streetlight caveat.
- **Selection over a top-k list is the silent statistical hazard in audit-by-SAEs.** "Top 20 features by summed activation, one is interesting" carries no false-discovery accounting. The affordable fix is to pre-register what an interesting feature must do (fire on ≥N distinct behaviours, fire on the training documents, *and* survive a firing-rate-matched control feature), and to report how many features in the list also pass. Write it as a screen with a denominator or do not call it a mechanism.
- **Concealment training is an interpretability experiment in disguise: it creates a behaviour the internals may still represent and the outputs no longer show.** That is the ideal test object for a predictive white-box model — the belief-side quantity is designed to persist while the behaviour-side quantity is trained to zero, so a probe that reads the internal representation is measurably better than any output monitor, and the gap is the measurement. The missing piece here, and the experiment worth importing, is the *steering* arm: if feature 30277 really mediates exploitation, amplifying it under the interrogator's pressure should raise exploitation while confession stays suppressed. The post stops one intervention short of its own claim, and that arm is cheap.
