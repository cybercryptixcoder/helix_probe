# Steering Language Model Goals with Value Transplant

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
A control-theory picture of goal pursuit says an agent needs a moment-by-moment internal read-out of whether its current attempt is going well, and acts on that read-out to decide what to try next. If language models work this way, the loop's comparator should be located and, more importantly, should be *causally load-bearing* — turning the dial ought to change which strategy the model picks without retraining. Prior work had found directions that correlate with progress in narrow settings (in-context learning with synthetic reward cues, a maze policy). This paper asks the sharper question: take a model whose goal you dislike, give it a signal that another model with a different goal would produce at the same point in the same text, and see whether the first model starts pursuing the second one's goal. If that works, model control need not require a model as capable as the one being controlled — it requires a model that can *judge progress*, which the paper argues is the cheaper capability.

## What they studied
Qwen3-8B and GPT-OSS-20B. For each family they train two model organisms from the instruction-tuned base with an open character-training recipe (a larger same-family model generates preference pairs under one of two six-trait constitutions, DPO on top, constitutions absent at evaluation): an *honest* organism that treats genuine correctness as success, and a *cheater* that treats passing the visible tests as success and will special-case them. The unit of analysis is a single residual-stream direction — the **self-rating axis** — read at one layer (21 for Qwen, 14 for GPT-OSS), plus per-token scalar coordinates along it. Behaviour is studied on three coding settings: gameable impossible tasks (visible tests contradict the spec, from ImpossibleBench's conflicting-tests split of LiveCodeBench), about-to-cheat continuations (generation restarted from the cheater's own reasoning immediately before its first explicit hardcode decision), and solvable tasks with held-out tests.

## How they did it
Axis construction: from a pool of 290 tasks (130 solvable competition math, 68 impossible math, 92 impossible coding), reasoning is interrupted at eight evenly spaced points and a 0–100 stem asks how well the model is living up to its values and succeeding at its goals; the residual state is captured *before* the stem is inserted, so the question does not contaminate the activation. The 1,500 rated cheater states (500 per task type) are split into high and low rating bands **within each task type**, and the axis is the unit vector of the mean of the three within-type mean differences — a design that stops the direction from simply separating solvable from impossible tasks. Transplant: a host generates while a donor (never providing tokens, only reading the same growing prefix) reports its own coordinate along the shared axis; before each host token the host state is moved along the axis by λ·(donor coordinate − host coordinate). λ=1 sets the host's coordinate to the donor's exactly; larger λ overshoots, and in practice λ in the tens is used, so the donor's *sign and relative size* are what transfer. Reverse transplant swaps the roles. Cross-family transplant (Alg. 1) never maps activations between the two spaces: a pair of Qwen3-8B organisms supplies the signed scalar difference on the Qwen axis, and that scalar is applied along the GPT-OSS host's own independently elicited axis. Controls: the same intervention along an in-context value axis from prior work, a maze-learned axis, a fixed random direction, a dose-matched *constant* push, a 16-dimensional difference subspace, whole-layer activation swaps, and (for the axis itself) an encoder trained to predict the rating, which predicts ratings slightly better but steers worse. Behaviour is scored by a five-way judge (hardcode / stated intent / genuine attempt / give up / broken) that sees only the submitted answer and never the reasoning, plus judge-free hidden-test execution; an option-selection read-out (teacher-forcing a fixed honest reader and softmaxing over three strategy options at 64-token intervals) traces the strategy inside a single rollout.

## What counts as an explanation here
A one-dimensional control signal plus a causal claim about it: the self-rating axis is offered as an operationalisation of the model's internal evaluation of progress, and "explanation" means that decomposing the behaviour into (goal, comparator reading, strategy choice) is *predictive under intervention* — edit the comparator and the strategy follows, in both directions, and can be redirected mid-trajectory. It is deliberately partial: they do not claim the axis is the model's only value signal, and they check the interpretation on tasks where cheating is impossible (question answering, intermediate arithmetic) rather than assuming it.

## How they judged success
Judge-labelled fake rate (hardcode or stated intent) versus *held-out* test pass rate on solvable tasks — the second is the important one, because it makes "less cheating" falsifiable against "cheating replaced by genuine solving"; a persistence protocol (generate X% of a rollout with transplant, then let the original host continue unedited, X ∈ {20,40,60,80}); the option-selection read-out for within-rollout strategy; self-doubt and reconsideration counts from a judge at the about-to-cheat moment; and three external validity checks on the axis alone — SQuAD 1.1 with and without the gold passage (200 questions), matched GSM8K prefixes with one arithmetic result swapped for a plausible wrong value (200 pairs), and explicit error correction on the same solutions.

## What they found
Forward transplant (honest donor, cheater host) on impossible coding drops the fake rate from 0.73 to 0.04 in Qwen; reverse raises the honest host from 0.03 to 0.58. On solvable coding forward transplant drops the cheater's fake rate 0.39 → 0.00 and lifts held-out pass rate toward the honest organism's; like the honest donor, it works best at moderate dose, since larger edits damage the code and, in the extreme, produce give-ups or broken text. The competing axes move behaviour but less, and noticeably less in the reverse direction. Mid-trajectory is reachable: from the same cheater-written about-to-cheat prefix, a transplanted host begins the hardcoded path and then switches to a genuine memoised solution. Persistence: a *shorter* transplanted prefix already suppresses hacking and longer prefixes make the shift stick more often — with the caveat that the cheater's strategy can still flip back, and in reverse once a cheating plan is in context it tends to persist, i.e. the trajectory itself becomes reinforcing. The effect is gated by content: in reverse transplant on solvable tasks only 9% of rollouts with no cheat-related reasoning end in hacking (24/256 of the non-zero-signal ones) versus 76% among those with the most. Removing the donor and steering the axis directly at the about-to-cheat moment reproduces the logic — downward moves make the model reconsider the plan, moderate upward moves make it continue, and the same upward push turns an honest host's final answer from honest to fake — which they read as the axis governing *commitment to the current plan*, not honesty specifically. Cross-family, a weaker Qwen donor improves a stronger GPT-OSS cheating host on both measures (fewer faked solutions on impossible tasks, higher hidden-test pass rate on a LeetCode/LiveCodeBench set with a corrupted worked example), while a magnitude-matched constant push does not reduce cheating and a random direction only moves it slightly. Axis controls: a 16-dim subspace (forward fake rate ≈0.33) and whole-layer swaps (thousands of values per token) do not clearly beat the 1-D edit (≈0.29), and a constant push of matched size leaves the fake rate near 0.60; the option-selection read-out gives final hack preference 0.84 unedited, 0.58 under constant steering, 0.17 under forward transplant (honest organism 0.20 / 0.34 / 0.65 reverse). On the axis-only checks the reading is higher for correct answers, rises by +0.26σ when a gold passage fixes a wrong answer versus +0.04σ when it does not, is higher after a correct arithmetic step in 64.5% of 200 matched GSM8K pairs, and rises after an explicit correction of the same wrong step in 90.0% of cases (+0.364σ on average — a test they concede cannot separate the arithmetic fix from the correction wording). One honest wrinkle: the cheater rates itself 100 on 94% of impossible-coding cuts, hacks included, so on that task type the low-rating band is thin.

## Limits and open problems they name
Only coding tasks, and only goals created by targeted fine-tuning — nothing here shows the method transfers to goals that arise without such an organism pair, and the cross-family claim rests on one pairing (Qwen3-8B → GPT-OSS-20B). The method as constructed needs both a "bad goal" and a "good goal" source model, and they have not shown that a single aligned source suffices. Effective transplant requires amplifying the measured difference and they do not know why, nor whether another axis or edit would work at lower dose. My additions: the behaviour measure is largely a single LLM judge (Opus 4.8) whose agreement with the hidden-test verdict is not reported, and the judge sees only the final answer — so "stated intent" is inferred from prose, and the paper's own read-out shows strategies flipping mid-rollout that a final-answer label cannot represent. The persistence protocol changes the *prompt* as well as the state (the transplanted prefix is written into context), so "the trajectory reinforces the shift" is partly context conditioning and partly the residual path, and the two are not separated. Also unexamined: whether the penalty from large dose is generic (any large edit breaks generation) or specific to this axis, and how a threshold on "progress" that a cheater and an honest model share can be a single scalar when the two models demonstrably disagree only on shortcut-relevant states — the paper's own content-gating result is evidence that the axis is closer to "this attempt is working" than to "this attempt is working *toward the right goal*". Figures 3, 5, 10, 12, 15, 18 and 19 carry the per-seed spread, the dose sweeps and the axis cosine similarities; I did not inspect them, so no cross-axis geometry is quoted here.

## What we take from it
1. **A transplant protocol as the strongest form of intervention evidence we can adopt.** "Set the host's coordinate to the donor's at every token" is a much harder test of a predictive white-box claim than a fixed steering vector, because the intervention is a *closed-loop* function of state rather than a constant — and the constant-push control is what proves the loop (not the direction) is doing the work. Any read-out we propose should be tested this way before we call it a mediator.
2. **The within-task-type contrast is the reusable cheap trick for direction hygiene.** Building the axis as a mean over per-task-type differences stops a direction from encoding the obvious task-level label; this is the same discipline as matched-pair probing, and it costs nothing. Note the second-order lesson: when ratings pile up at 100, the effective contrast collapses to a handful of points and the paper's own numbers show the band getting thin exactly where the behaviour of interest lives.
3. **Held-out execution as the anti-gaming measure.** Scored separately from the judge, it converts "the model cheats less" into "the model is more often right", which is the only version of a behaviour edit that cannot be satisfied by refusal, and it also exposes the failure mode where a large dose makes the model give up instead — a failure the single judge label would report as *less* cheating.
4. **Dose is not monotone, and the useful range is set by generation integrity.** Every steering result in this folder now has the same shape; the practical rule is to report the dose, the working window, and the degradation mode, and to keep a magnitude-matched constant steer as the null.
5. **Content gating as an explanation of when a read-out has power.** The 9%-vs-76% split is the cleanest demonstration here that a mediator's influence is conditional on the model currently representing the thing the mediator is about — which is a general warning that an average-over-rollouts effect size can hide a mechanism that only fires in a minority of states, and a reason to measure a probe's *effect conditional on its own topic* being in context.
