## What they wanted, and why
Mechanistic interpretability wants to reverse-engineer the computation a network actually performs, the way a programmer reads a program. The obstacle is that transformers look hostile to that: attention heads write into a shared residual stream in an unlabelled superposition, MLPs are polysemantic, and every analysis in the vision-circuits tradition (which the same lab built for InceptionV1) assumes units you can name. The paper's aim is not to explain a model but to find a *notation* in which some transformers become exactly analysable — to show that a lot of the architecture has linear structure that can be multiplied out and read off the weights, and to demonstrate on tiny attention-only models that this yields complete, weight-derived descriptions of behaviour. A secondary aim, stated up front, is to establish what kind of explanation the field should be producing: end-to-end functions from tokens to logits, not attention-pattern stories.

## What they studied
Toy transformer language models with **attention only and no MLP layers**: zero-layer (embedding directly unembedded), one-layer, and two-layer models, trained on text (the examples shown use the opening paragraph of Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone) and on random token sequences. The models use **no positional information in the residual stream** (a mechanism like rotary attention's assumption), a detail that later matters for which induction-head implementations are available. Large models are invoked only as motivation and as a check that the same motifs appear; no large-model measurements are reported in this paper (September's "induction heads" article is where that goes). The unit of analysis is therefore the **attention head as an independent operation** and **the path** as the thing that is interpretable.

## How they did it
Four moves, in order of increasing ambition. (1) **Rewrite the model.** Because every layer reads the residual stream with an arbitrary linear map and writes with another, the residual stream is a pure linear communication channel with **no privileged basis** — the same function can be written in a rotated coordinate system. Their decision is not to analyse the stream itself but to push linearity back through the weights: the Q and K are never separate objects, only the composed bilinear form W_QK = W_QᵀW_K, and likewise W_OV = W_O W_V, so an attention head is "a routing map times a rank-limited content map". (2) **Expand products into sums of paths.** Writing the model as a product of layer operators and expanding gives one term per end-to-end path: a direct term (always present, cannot move information between positions, so it can only shift bigram statistics), first-order head terms, and higher-order "virtual head" terms built from compositions. Each term is tractable on its own and the terms add. (3) **Split composition by where it acts.** A later head's query can read what an earlier head wrote (Q-composition), its key can (K-composition) — both change the attention *pattern* — or its value can (V-composition), which does not change the pattern and instead fuses the two heads into one virtual head with pattern A^{h₂}A^{h₁} and OV matrix W_OV^{h₂}W_OV^{h₁}. Composition strength is scored by a normalised Frobenius norm of the product of the relevant matrices, with the value expected from random matrices of the same shapes subtracted. (4) **Test with ablation of the theory, not of the activations.** Because a virtual head is an object that exists only after expansion, they cannot zero it directly; instead they run the model repeatedly, feeding back the recorded attention patterns and substituting saved head outputs, which caps the path depth at n and makes successive runs differ by exactly the marginal contribution of the n-th order terms.

## What counts as an explanation here
An exact, weight-derived decomposition of the output into independently interpretable functions, each with a stated input (which tokens) and output (which logits), such that the whole model's behaviour is the sum of them plus a measurable residual for the terms they ignore. The strongest form in the paper is the one-layer result: the model *is* an ensemble of a bigram table and skip-trigram tables obtained by multiplying weights, with no approximation. Attention patterns alone are explicitly not an explanation — they are one observable inside a term and they go misleading the moment composition is present. The paper is candid that the framework stops being exact once MLPs are present, and treats the MLP appendix as "here is how the same reasoning would go, plus why we could not get traction".

## How they judged success
Three kinds of check, none of them a benchmark. (a) **Constructive identity**: the expanded table and the model's actual logits must agree, which for the one-layer case is arithmetic rather than empirical. (b) **Predict-then-look**: the theory of induction heads makes two independent structural predictions — the K-composed QK term should be strongly positive-eigenvalued (a "same-token match") and the OV circuit should be a "copying" matrix — and they place all induction heads in the extreme corner of a 2-D QK/OV eigen-positivity plot, addressing the circularity objection head-on by pointing out that nothing about large composition implies positive eigenvalues. (c) **Behavioural generalisation**: induction heads are tested on three repetitions of **uniformly random token sequences**, which is maximally off-distribution and lets nothing but the abstract repeated-sequence property carry the prediction. Negative results are also used as evidence: the virtual-head ablation shows second-order terms have small marginal effect in this model, which licenses ignoring them when reading the two-layer model.

## What they found
A zero-layer transformer can only do what W_U W_E allows, and so learns an approximation to bigram log-likelihoods; that same term survives in every deeper model as the direct path and ends up carrying bigrams the grammar-respecting circuits do not cover, which is also why the unembedding is not the inverse of the embedding. One-layer attention-only models decompose exactly into bigrams plus skip-trigrams of the form A…B→C, with the trigram table factored between QK and OV — a factorisation they note is under-determined, so different decompositions of the same table are possible. Copying is pervasive in one-layer OV matrices, and skip-trigram copies peculiarities of the tokenizer (sub-word fragments). In the two-layer model analysed, the only substantive composition is **K-composition from a single previous-token head**, and the algorithm built from it is the **induction head**: it searches the context for an earlier instance of the current token and copies whatever followed it, falling back to the first token when there is no match. Mechanically the trick is that the keys are shifted one position by the previous-token head, so a query that matches "the same token" actually matches the token *after* it. Compared to the one-layer copying head (b…a→b), this is strictly more powerful and less distribution-dependent. Isolating copying as a matrix property, heads live in a 2-D space of QK and OV positivity with the induction heads at one corner. The V-composition ablation finds virtual heads nearly irrelevant at two layers, and the authors are explicit that this rules out V-composition only, not Q-and-K composition, which lives in the QK circuits. Their own published correction matters for reading the composition diagram: a bug in a low-rank-matrix library meant the plotted "composition" quantity was not the one intended but a Frobenius norm of a cyclically permuted product, which happens to measure whether a head's query reads the subspace the earlier head wrote *into* — i.e. it overshoots precisely the induction-head structure. When corrected, some induction heads turn out to also draw on a head attending to the last several tokens, which is consistent with the paper's own remark that real induction heads are usually more elaborate than the minimal previous-token construction.

## Limits and open problems they name
No MLP layers, so the framework covers at most about a third of a standard transformer's parameters (and, because heads interact with MLPs, less than that in practice); the MLP appendix says clean reasoning is available *if* neurons turn out interpretable, and reports the exception that neurons around 5% depth often respond to short phrases. The QK/OV separation is justified by a "freeze the attention pattern and rerun" thought experiment rather than derived. No positional embeddings in the residual stream in these models, so one of the two known induction-head implementations (pointer arithmetic) is unavailable and is only discussed. The models are tiny, and they say plainly that they do not attempt to apply the insights to large models in this paper. In the appendix they also warn that basis-free activations (residual stream, keys/queries/values, head outputs) cannot be read "neuron by neuron" at all, and that only tokens, attention patterns and MLP activations have privileged bases. My additions: the paper contains **no error bars, no seeds and no quantitative fit metric** — "the expanded table equals the logits" is checked arithmetically for one layer but the two-layer reading is a qualitative match of a diagram, so nothing in the entry should be read as an effect size. The skip-trigram factorisation being under-determined is acknowledged but not explored, and it is the earliest version of the identifiability problem that later dictionary work inherits. Finally, the interaction-strength statistic (normalised Frobenius product of two matrices, random-matrix baseline subtracted) has no calibration: there is no threshold given for "significant composition", so the claim that composition is confined to one previous-token head rests on a plot I did not inspect.

## What we take from it
1. **Fold parameters into the bilinear objects they always appear as.** W_Q and W_K have no meaning apart, and no amount of staring at them makes them meaningful; the meaningful object is the composed form whose entries are "the attention score this token gives that token". Anything we say about "the QK circuit" or "the OV circuit" of a real model inherits this, and it is why reading a head's weight matrix without its partner is close to meaningless.
2. **State whether the basis you are interpreting is privileged.** The appendix taxonomy — tokens, attention patterns and MLP activations have privileged bases; residual stream, queries/keys/values and head outputs do not — is the cleanest statement in the folder of when "this neuron means X" is even a well-posed sentence. Any probe or feature claim aimed at a basis-free activation must say what fixed the coordinate system (a dictionary, a downstream read, an arbitrary rotation choice), because the model can be re-expressed in a rotated one.
3. **The composition taxonomy is a checklist for a suspicious single-head story.** If a head's behaviour changes when its K-composition partners are ablated, an account of that head alone is wrong even if its attention pattern looks interpretable. And the three composition types are not interchangeable: V-composition fuses into virtual units, Q/K-composition changes routing — so an "ignoring higher-order terms" result must name which type it excluded, exactly as this paper does.
4. **Ablate the theory, not just the activations.** The layer-by-layer route of feeding back recorded attention patterns and substituted head outputs (capping path depth) is a directly reusable technique: it lets us measure the marginal contribution of an object our explanation invented but the model does not store. This is the ancestor of every "virtual weights" and "circuit ablation" measurement in this folder.
5. **Off-distribution predict-then-test is the cheap falsifier.** Repeating uniformly random tokens removes every statistical crutch and leaves only the abstract property the theory names. The habit to import: find the input distribution on which a competing explanation of your mechanism cannot produce the same prediction, and run there.
