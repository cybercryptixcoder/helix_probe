## What they wanted, and why
A training signal that rewards the right answer cannot distinguish a model that has learned the intended rule from one that has latched onto a correlated shortcut — the post's own example is a chest X-ray model that keys on the drainage tube because tubes co-occur with the labelled disease, though a tube means the patient has already been treated. Their ambition is to make the training objective speak the vocabulary of the model's internals: name the concept you do *not* want used (or expressed), and penalise that, so the model is specified by *how* it solves the task and not only by whether it succeeds. Because data curation cannot fix this when the offending signal is diffuse (their motivating case is DPO-induced sycophancy with under 1% visibly sycophantic examples), they want an intervention that operates where the behaviour actually lives. The second half of the paper exists because of an obvious risk: training against a probe might teach the model to evade the probe rather than drop the concept, which would trade a behaviour problem for a monitoring blind spot. So they treat *monitorability after shaping* as a first-class outcome, not a caveat.

## What they studied
Four settings, three modalities. (i) A classifier trained on top of a frozen vision model, used for a toy "rules" illustration (classify the dog by its nose; by tongue if present else ears). (ii) Chest X-ray diagnosis on CheXpert, deliberately confounded by filtering to diseased-with-drain vs healthy-without, so the shortcut is maximally tempting. (iii) Sycophancy during DPO post-training of OLMo-3-7B, plus 13B and 32B to check the recipe scales. (iv) Reward hacking in an RL-trained Qwen3.5-9B on a file-manipulating variant of Countdown, with a held-out transfer task (chess puzzles with a forbidden solution file). Unit of analysis: a **concept direction** at a chosen residual-stream layer, found either by an SAE-feature + LLM-agent pipeline (vision) or by a difference-style direction from example transcripts (sycophancy, cheating). A toy model stands in for the theory.

## How they did it
Two loss terms are added to ordinary training. *Reliance shaping* penalises how much the output responds to a concept direction, with "responds" measured by a standard attribution method, so it targets the model's use of a concept without forbidding the concept's presence. *Expression shaping* penalises the concept's presence along the direction (activation as a proxy), so it targets what the model does. In both, the direction is recomputed every n steps to follow a representation that moves during training. For the chest X-ray arm the shortcut direction is not hand-supplied: an SAE splits representations into features, an LLM agent labels and scores features for tube/drain likeness, and its top picks become the directions to penalise — the point being that no shortcut labels are used. For sycophancy they locate a sycophancy direction and penalise reliance on it during DPO, keeping the preference objective intact so capability gains should survive; they compare against reduced-learning-rate DPO, data filtering, reward shaping, inoculation prompting, and preventative steering along the same direction. For reward hacking they use expression shaping with a "cheating" direction computed from coding-task cheat transcripts, on a puzzle where the honest route is inferable from the prompt range and the cheat route is reading a file the model is told not to open; they also run a data-filtering control that drops cheating rollouts. Monitorability is assessed by probing for the shaped concept before and after, with the original probe, a refit linear probe, a nonlinear probe, probes at other layers, and finally a black-box judge on inputs/outputs alone.

## What counts as an explanation here
Three distinct objects, worth separating. (1) **A direction plus a layer** — the operative object; it is what the loss touches, and their framework needs nothing else from interpretability than a readable layer and a concept direction. (2) **A closed-form toy account** of how representations move under a projection penalty: the penalised concept regrows immediately through the task gradient, the regrowth is entangled with the directions the task uses, and with frequent refitting the model can be pushed into a *second-order product code* (latent activity ≈ desired concept × undesired concept) that no linear probe reads but the structure of which is exactly understood. (3) **A five-level monitorability hierarchy** (L1 reuse the probe, L2 linear refit, L3 nonlinear refit, L4 different layer, L5 different model as black-box judge) that classifies *where the concept went* from *what it took to read it again*. The claim of explanation is deliberately predictive-in-form: theory names the re-encodings, and the empirical section asks which level each experiment landed in.

## How they judged success
Task performance against baselines, and sycophancy/cheating rates against those same baselines. For the X-ray arm: accuracy on a non-confounded evaluation (diseased without drain) compared to standard training on the confounded data (soft floor), to the best of several prior de-biasing methods, and to a *balanced-data soft ceiling*. For sycophancy: a capability axis (mean accuracy over six benchmarks) plotted against a sycophancy axis, with "just do less DPO" drawn as the reference trade-off line, and the question being whether a method sits below it. For reward hacking: rate of honest solves and of cheating at end of training, proportion of runs where cheating was prevented at all (for the filtering control), and cheating rate on the unseen chess task. For monitorability: AUROC (sycophancy) and balanced accuracy (reward hacking) of the original probe before and after shaping, and of retrained probes; cosine similarity between desired and undesired concept directions as an entanglement measure; three seeds for the vision bars. The post does not report confidence intervals, and the probes' training-data details are in the unread paper.

## What they found
The post's own summary numbers. **Chest X-ray:** reliance shaping driven by agent-labelled SAE features beats the soft floor, beats the best prior method tested, and exceeds the balanced-data soft ceiling. **Sycophancy:** data filtering moves along the "less DPO" line (capability and sycophancy traded); reward shaping and inoculation prompting barely move sycophancy; preventative steering along the same direction partly works; reliance shaping at its best setting reaches DPO-level capability while sycophancy stays near the starting model — with fixed and refit directions both plotted, and the same recipe working at 7B, 13B and 32B. **Reward hacking:** standard RL starts cheating almost immediately (≈6× within ten updates, honest strategies to zero); expression shaping yields honest solving 96–98% of the time; filtering cheating rollouts prevented cheating in only one third of runs; and on unseen chess puzzles the standard-RL model cheats 99% of the time while expression-shaped models barely cheat and still improve — which is the post's central claim that the penalty targets the *concept* rather than the specific forbidden file. **Monitorability:** the layers just before the penalty change most; desirable and undesirable directions reached cosine similarity up to ~0.45 in some confounded vision runs; sycophancy probes went 0.98 → 0.93 AUROC with retrained probes at 1.00 throughout; reward-hacking probes fell 0.98 → 0.57 balanced accuracy in the worst case (gradients through the probe) with retrained probes at 0.97–0.98, and other variants held 0.87–0.90 with the original probe. With frequent refitting at an intermediate layer the concept survived only nonlinearly there, yet was linearly readable two layers later. One unpredicted case: reliance penalties at the last layer partially erased the concept there, though it stayed recoverable at earlier layers.

## Limits and open problems they name
Small-to-medium scale only; the theory is analytic for a toy model, with no analytic solutions for deeper networks; no account of all possible neural codes; nothing at frontier scale. Their own framing of the trickiest case is that a failed probe is ambiguous between erasure and an inadequate probe, and their only guidance is a bound — probes need only be as powerful as the rest of the model, since the model must read the concept to use it. They claim the concept stayed monitorable in *every* experiment, but L5 (black-box judge) is in the hierarchy, which means "monitorable" includes falling back to watching outputs.

## What we take from it
- **A measure, and the sharpest one in this folder for our problem:** the monitorability hierarchy is a way to state *where a read-out went* rather than whether it broke, and each level is a distinct, cheaply distinguishable claim. Adopt the level labels when we report an intervention that could have moved our own probe.
- **A prediction target we can actually test on ourselves:** the theory's product-code prediction is the one place in the literature here where a specific nonlinear encoding is *predicted in advance* from training pressure. Any of our shaping or ablation experiments that refit a direction on a schedule should be checked for exactly that signature (equal means, opposite tilts) before we call a lost direction "erased".
- **A warning that cuts both ways:** training against our own read-out is a confound generator. If we ever train against a probe-derived signal, the post-shaping probe result must be reported beside a retrained probe, since the original-probe number alone would understate the concept's presence.
- **A baseline ladder worth copying:** the sycophancy figure puts *data filtering*, *reward shaping*, *inoculation prompting*, and *steering along the same direction* on one capability-vs-behaviour plane with "do less training" as the reference line. That is the honest way to show a training-time interpretability method is not just a rebranded learning-rate reduction.
- **A framing:** "reliance" vs "expression" is a clean split of two things our survey tends to blur — whether the model *uses* a concept for the decision versus whether the concept is *present in what it produces*. Only the second is what a text-level monitor sees.
