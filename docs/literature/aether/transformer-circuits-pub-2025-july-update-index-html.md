## What they wanted, and why

Two unrelated posts under one lab-notebook banner. The first re-reads Anthropic's 2021 mathematical framework for transformer circuits *in the language of features*, now that feature dictionaries exist where the original paper had no way to extract features out of superposition. The motivating complaint is that the framework described heads in terms of eigenvalues of products of weight matrices — a vocabulary that was the best available at the time and that many readers then mistook for the object being studied. The second post asks what four years of sparse-autoencoder work has actually produced when the model is not a language model at all but a protein or genome model, and it is written as a survey of external papers rather than as a set of new experiments. The pair is useful to us for opposite reasons: the first is the cleanest available statement of how to translate a weight-matrix claim into a feature claim, the second is the clearest case in this folder of a domain where a feature's *name* is checkable against an external database.

## What they studied

The first post studies no model at all. It is an argument about three canonical head types — copy, previous-token and induction — and about what their OV and QK circuits look like when expressed as maps between transformed feature sets. The second post surveys five papers applying SAEs to biological models: InterPLM on ESM-2 at 8M parameters with an L1 dictionary of width 10,420; InterProt on ESM-2 at 650M with TopK dictionaries up to width 16,384; Reticular on ESMFold's 3B base model with a hierarchical Matryoshka dictionary of 10,240 latents; Evo 2, a 7B DNA genome model, with a BatchTopK dictionary of 32,768; and Markov Biosciences' gene-expression model with an unspecified standard SAE. The unit of analysis in the first half is a matrix decomposable into feature-to-feature blocks; in the second half it is a single dictionary latent and, in places, the clusters those latents form.

## How they did it

The framework revisit proceeds by naming a family of *transformed feature sets*: for any feature X there is a `prev(X)` feature meaning X occurred at the previous position, and a `say(X)` feature meaning the model is about to emit X. These transformed sets stand in one-to-one correspondence with the originals, and the post's working assumption is that their directions in the residual stream are often linear images of the original direction, which it flags as a hypothesis with a pointer to earlier work on matrix binding and echo features rather than a result. With those bases fixed, each head's OV matrix becomes a block matrix mapping feature-set on the left to feature-set on the right, and the claim about a head type becomes a claim about which block is non-empty and diagonal. A copy head is then the block taking X to `say(X)`; a previous-token head takes X to `prev(X)` and may compound to previous-of-previous on a second pass; an induction head has a copy-like OV but a QK circuit that reads as a condition on the query token and on the token preceding the key. The original paper's positive-eigenvalue tests — of the unembed–OV–embed product for copy heads, and of the induction-QK times previous-token-OV product for induction heads — are then re-derived as consequences: those products isolate an off-diagonal block between an early feature basis (embeddings) and a late one (unembeddings) or between a condition and its consequence, and a block that is diagonal with positive entries has positive eigenvalues. The biology half is a literature review organised as a table (model, SAE architecture, headline finding, validation method, practical impact) followed by worked examples, cross-study patterns, and a short list of what would count as next steps.

## What counts as an explanation here

In the first post, the explanation is a **structural claim about a weight matrix** expressed as a statement about feature sets: this head implements "seeing X causes saying X", and the evidence is that one identified block of the OV matrix is the identity-like block connecting those two sets while the others are not. It is partial by construction — three head types out of an unbounded catalogue — but each claim is of the exact form we want: it names a mechanism and predicts which feature should be written from which. The post is explicit that the eigenvalue version was a *proxy* for this structural statement, adopted because the feature basis was unavailable, and that it is only sound because embeddings and unembeddings happen to approximate `X` and `say(X)` bases in small models. In the second post the explanation is a **labelled dictionary latent with a functional reading**, and the interesting cases are those where the reading is checked against something outside the model: a Swiss-Prot or InterPro annotation, an AlphaFold-predicted secondary structure, a genomic region another tool already classified.

## How they judged success

The first post judges success by whether the block structure makes the eigenvalue tests intelligible, i.e. by argument, not measurement; there are no models, no metrics, no ablations and no error bars anywhere in it. The second post's success criteria are borrowed from the papers it reviews and they are heterogeneous, which the table makes visible: Swiss-Prot concept coverage (433 concepts queried in InterPLM; 49% coverage at higher layers in Reticular), linear probes on four downstream tasks, structure RMSD, count of active latents needed to preserve structure prediction, genome-wide activation maps, cross-species transfer, and manual inspection. None of these five papers is described as having run an ablation of a feature against a matched control.

## What they found

*(a) The framework translated.* All three canonical heads come out as one-block-diagonal matrices once the shifted feature sets are used, and the historically notable point is what the eigenvalue tests were actually testing: a positive eigenvalue is a symptom of a diagonal block, and the earlier papers' confidence in the test rested on the assumption that embeddings and unembeddings are approximately single-token bases — an assumption the post limits to "especially in small models". Induction is stated as a query-side condition plus an OV-side copy, with the QK product against the previous-token OV extracting the piece that should be diagonal. The write-up is therefore an *un-superstitioning* of existing results rather than a new finding: the matrices are unchanged, the interpretation of why the numbers were positive is now a claim about features that could in principle be falsified with a real dictionary.

*(b) The biology survey's strongest evidence is annotation repair.* The flagship example is InterPLM's feature f/939, which fires on a Nudix box motif: most proteins where it fires carry that annotation in Swiss-Prot, and one protein that fires strongly and lacks it — B2GFH1 in the post's naming — turned out on further inspection to contain the motif according to another database. That is a false positive of the feature that is a true positive of the model, and the review reports several such cases. Evo 2's feature f/19746 is the second worked case and the more interesting one methodologically: it fires on prophage regions and also on CRISPR spacers, and the discriminating experiment is scrambling the spacers (activation survives) versus scrambling the CRISPR direct repeats (activation disappears) — read as the model having learned the functional relation between stored viral fragments and bacterial immunity rather than a surface string similarity. The same feature also lit up on regions not annotated as prophage that contained integrases and invertases.

*(c) The cross-study patterns are the review's own synthesis.* Four are named: superposition in biological models is described as more severe than in language models, making dictionaries more necessary rather than less; scale is associated with higher concept coverage, with Reticular's Matryoshka hierarchy reading residues up to domains; features align with known functional units rather than arbitrary patterns; and Evo 2's α-helix and β-sheet features, mapped onto AlphaFold 3 structures, mean a DNA-sequence-only model has internals that organise around protein structure it never saw supervised. A fifth, cross-species transfer, is stated as features learned on human genomes annotating a woolly mammoth genome absent from training.

*(d) Sparsity of structural determinants is a concrete number.* Reticular reports that 8 to 32 active latents per position suffice to maintain structure prediction, and that a single layer — layer 36 in the post's phrasing — preserves it, which the review offers as a hint about a critical computational pathway. Feature clustering yields functional modules with sub-specialisation (several kinase-binding features over different sub-regions of the binding site; general versus TonB-dependent β-barrels), which the review frames as the raw material for circuits.

## Limits and open problems they name

For the framework post: the feature bases are approximations and the identifications rely on small models; transformed features are asserted to be often linear images of originals, which is a claim about geometry that the post does not test; and the treatment covers three head types, with everything about cross-layer composition left as it was in the original paper. For the biology review: it is a conference-talk-derived summary of five papers, based on preprints as they stood in December 2024, and some have since been updated (they say so). Biological validation is named as the central gap — wet-lab experiments rather than database matching, with the causal status of an SAE feature explicitly unestablished. Multimodal biological models are called unexplored. They also record a counterweight from work outside the SAE line: masking weights to degrade sequence prediction for particular protein families impairs structure prediction for **all** families, which they read as deep entanglement and offer as a comparison point against what dictionaries claim. Their practical next steps are validation protocols, multimodal SAE variants, cheaper training, and systematic circuit discovery on top of Evo 2's open weights and dataset.

Limits we add, not theirs: the review reports no number for how often an SAE feature's "missing annotation" turns out to be a genuine database gap versus a coincidence, so annotation repair is an anecdote with a favourable selection in a survey; and there is no control of the kind that would settle it — does a randomly chosen latent with similar firing statistics also "discover" missing annotations at some rate? On the framework side, no claim in the post has been checked against a trained dictionary, so the whole contribution is a translation scheme awaiting a test.

## What we take from it

- **A translation rule for weight claims.** When we describe a head or an MLP block by a matrix product, we should state which feature bases the block connects and whether it should be diagonal in those bases. The eigenvalues-as-evidence habit is a proxy for "there exists a basis in which this block is diagonal and positive", and stating the basis makes the claim falsifiable with a dictionary — a method, and the cleanest one in this folder.
- **The dependency to watch.** The framework's eigenvalue tests were sound only because embeddings approximate single-token features. Any argument of ours that says "layer L's matrix has property P, therefore mechanism Q" carries the same hidden premise about the basis, and the premise is testable: fit a dictionary at the two ends and check the block structure directly.
- **Annotation repair as an external validation channel.** The biology papers get something no language-model paper gets: a database that can disagree with the feature. This is the shape of validation we want to imitate wherever possible — an outside list that can mark a feature wrong — and it is worth asking, for every domain we touch, whether such a list exists.
- **The scramble control.** Scrambling spacers versus scrambling repeats is a two-arm control that separates "matched the string" from "matched the relation". This is a reusable design for our own claims: apply the same perturbation at two levels of the input, where one preserves the relation and one destroys it, and require the readout to follow the relation.
- **A warning about synthesis-by-survey.** Four of the review's cross-study patterns are inferences drawn across papers with different models, different architectures and different validation, and at least one ("more severe superposition") is stated without measurement. Treat cross-paper generalisations in monthlies as hypotheses, and do not import them as findings.
