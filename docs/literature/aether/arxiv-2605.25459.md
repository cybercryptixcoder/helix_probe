# From Simulation to Enaction: Post-trained Language Models Recognize and React to their own Generations

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.25459
- **Authors / organisation:** Asvin G (IAS Princeton); Jack Lindsey (Anthropic)
- **Date:** 2026-05-25
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25459
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (main body + figures text; appendices only via their references)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Pretraining makes a passive predictor: it never sees the consequences of its own output, so it has no reason to model "I said that, therefore what comes next is my world". Post-training does train on the model's own role's outputs, so the authors ask whether the model has quietly become an *enactor* — something that knows when it is acting, and whose internal state treats its own recent outputs as self-produced. Their two testable predictions: the model should be able to tell on-policy from off-policy context, and it should form a commitment about its upcoming output before emitting it. The larger goal is to find observable, cheap signatures of self-recognition and situational awareness that do not rely on the model's own verbal claims.

## What they studied
Main subject is Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct and its base counterpart, with cross-family replication over Qwen-2.5 (1.5B–72B), Gemma-2 (2B, 27B), DeepSeek-67B-Chat, Yi-1.5-34B-Chat and OLMo-3-7B/32B, plus a four-stage OLMo-3-32B checkpoint ladder (base → SFT → DPO → RLVR). Unit of analysis is two things at once: the output distribution (per-token entropy, "semantic" entropy over topic) and the hidden state (layer-21 centroids, residual-stream steering, KV-cache patching).

## How they did it
Four linked experiments. (1) *Self vs. other reading*: 20 prompts are answered by the target and by four foreign instruct models; each text is then fed back into each model in three formats (Assistant turn, user turn, no chat template), and per-token output entropy is measured — a full generator × evaluator matrix, so the "self" cell has a built-in control. (2) *Scale and training stage*: the same matrix at four size classes and at each OLMo-3 post-training checkpoint. (3) *Persona*: repeat with all models system-prompted as pirate or scientist, and generate/read under all eight pairwise combinations of personas. (4) *Surprise intervention*: from a fixed context with entropy H, append one token sampled at each of 20 ranks of the model's own distribution, read the new entropy H′, and fit ΔH/H against relative excess surprise (S−H)/H across chat, web-text-instruct and web-text-base conditions. Causal follow-ups: steer layers 0–39 along surprise-centroid directions; and for the explicit-detection task, overwrite user-token KV entries with those from a paired prompt at varying onsets.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a mechanism in the circuit sense. The explanation is a *behavioural signature plus a partially localised causal factor*: output entropy as the readout of implicit self-recognition, an internal surprise representation as one causal input to it, user-token activations as the store for an intended topic, and a KV-patch localisation showing that the explicit "was I prefilled?" verdict compares that stored intent to the emitted content. The authors explicitly say they do not know how on/off-policy is computed and give a list of candidate strategies.

## How they judged success
Per-token entropy in nats with medians and full distributions; the diagonal of the generator × evaluator entropy matrix being the column minimum (the key control — it rules out "this model's text is just easier to predict"); monotone trend across four size classes; ablation of the four post-training stages (base as the null); persona match/mismatch grid; a linear fit with a shared slope a and condition-specific intercept β; steering dose-response across baseline-entropy bins; topic-concentration measures (fraction of 50 samples on the modal topic, number of distinct topics); body entropy after matched on-policy vs. off-policy prefills; and bidirectional KV patching with P(PREFILLED) read from the verdict token, plus a subspace decomposition of the patch into surprise-subspace and orthogonal components.

## What they found
On-policy output entropy is far lower than off-policy entropy — median roughly 0.02 nats on the model's own chat text versus roughly 1.74 on C4 web text for Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct. Both self-authorship and Assistant formatting independently lower entropy, and the base model shows neither effect. Crucially the diagonal is the column minimum in every column of the five-model matrix, and the effect is bidirectional, so it is not a property of one model's prose style. The self-advantage is absent at ~2B and grows monotonically, reaching 0.1–0.4 nats at ~70B. On the OLMo-3 ladder, SFT installs role-gated recognition (self-entropy below others only in the Assistant field), DPO moves it outside the role marker, and RLVR widens it — so, surprisingly, off-policy training suffices. Matched personas give the lowest entropy, and default-Assistant × default-Assistant is the lowest of all. Internally, layer-21 centroids for input surprise and output entropy trace clean 1-D manifolds, but the base-model and on-policy manifolds are nearly orthogonal (cosine near zero at matched values, CKA 0.2–0.5) even when model and formatting are held fixed and only on/off-policy is varied; steering along most of these centroids barely moves entropy. The surprise direction is the exception: ΔH/H is close to linear in relative excess surprise with a similar slope across conditions, but a strongly negative intercept only under chat formatting — a steady entropy drop that token surprise alone does not explain. Steer toward low-surprise bins and output entropy falls; high-surprise bins and it rises. On the topic level, instruct models concentrate on one topic far more than base models on underspecified prompts, and a prefill from a topic contradicting the cached intent raises body entropy (mean gap about +0.23 nats for the instruct model, with the reverse sign for base). Explicitly asking whether the answer was prefilled works, and KV-patching user tokens flips the verdict in both directions: inducing a false positive in 8/8 domains, suppressing true positives to below 0.01 in four of five tested domains. The patch works even when applied just before the self-analysis, and only its component orthogonal to the surprise subspace reproduces the flip — so implicit and explicit recognition ride different mechanisms.

## Limits and open problems they name
Several experiments are Llama-3.1-70B only, and the whole suite is behind the current capability frontier. Prompts are simple and single-turn, while real agent work interleaves the model's own text with tool outputs and retrieved documents at fine granularity; they do not know how recognition behaves there. They do not know how on/off-policy is computed, why SFT and DPO suffice to install recognition, or how exactly the surprise representation modulates entropy. Entropy is a distributional property, so it cannot distinguish self-recognition from familiarity of style or persona. Decodable structure is not causation: most of the entropy manifold is non-causal under steering. Finally, a model pushed by a prefill into a trace it would not have produced is, by their account, in an unfamiliar state — which complicates prefill-based training, eval and red-teaming.

## What we take from it
- **A measure**: output entropy on the context you feed is a zero-cost readout of "does the model treat this as its own trajectory". As a white-box feature it is nearly free; as a black-box observable it is available without internals at all. Use it as a cheap covariate when predicting behaviour under intervention.
- **A control we should copy**: the generator × evaluator matrix with the diagonal as the claim. Any "the model represents X about itself" result needs the cross-model version of the same text in the same slot, or the effect may be text familiarity.
- **A design principle**: decodable manifolds are not mechanisms. This paper finds beautiful 1-D centroid curves for entropy and then shows steering along them does almost nothing, while the surprise direction *does* work — the sharpest recent statement of probe-plus-steer being mandatory.
- **A warning about readout choice**: implicit and explicit self-reports of the same fact (was I prefilled?) use different pathways. Predicting from internals and asking the model are not two views of one variable, so our predictive model should treat a verbal report as one more behavioural output to predict, not as ground truth.
- **A framing**: intent stored at the prompt positions and checked against generated content later is a concrete, patchable instance of "plan then act" — and the matter of *when* the check runs (on demand, at verdict time) is itself a prediction target.
- **A shortcut**: prefill-mismatch is an intervention with a known direction (entropy up, detection score up) that needs no labels — a cheap source of on/off-policy ground truth for a surrogate model.
