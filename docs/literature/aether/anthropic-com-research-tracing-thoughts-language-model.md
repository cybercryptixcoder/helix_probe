## What they wanted, and why
They want a microscope for model computation, motivated the way neuroscience motivates itself: you cannot learn how a system works only by talking to it, so you build instruments that identify activity patterns and information flow inside. Three concrete questions frame the post — is there a language of thought behind multilingual behaviour, does a next-token model plan beyond one token, and is a written chain of thought the actual computation or a plausible story? The post's job is to tour ten "crucial model behaviors" and show that tracing produces at least partial insight into each, while stating outright that the instrument captures only a fraction of the computation.

## What they studied
Claude 3.5 Haiku, on short simple prompts, using the feature-and-transcoder attribution-graph method from the companion paper. Unit of analysis is features (cross-layer transcoder features) and the attribution graph linking them, grouped into supernodes; analysis is per-prompt circuit, with occasional comparisons to a smaller model. Case studies: multilingual synonymy ("opposite of small" in several languages), rhyme planning in a two-line poem, two-digit mental addition, faithfulness of chain of thought on an easy (square root of a decimal) versus a hard (cosine of a large number) item, a hidden bias-appeasing goal, multi-step capital-city retrieval, hallucination on known versus unknown entities, and a word-initial jailbreak.

## How they did it
Standard tracing plus **targeted interventions**. For the rhyme case they subtract the "rabbit" concept from the state at the point where planning happens and re-continue the line; separately they inject "green" at the same point. For mental arithmetic they trace the graph to each output digit. For multi-step reasoning they trace the capital-city answer and then swap the "Texas" concept for "California" and check the output. For hallucination they activate "known answer" features (or inhibit "unknown name"/"can't answer" features) on a prompt about a person the model cannot know, and also look for cases where the "known entity" feature fires on a *recognised but unknown* name without any intervention. For the faithfulness case they compare the presence of intermediate-computation features between the easy and hard problems, and between prompted-hint and unprompted runs. For the multilingual case they compare graph overlap across languages within a model and across two model sizes.

## What counts as an explanation here
A per-prompt attribution graph plus named features, explicitly partial. The post's standard is *intervention-backed*: a graph is doing explanatory work when changing the internal state at a named node produces the predicted behavioural change (subtract "rabbit" → another rhyme; inject "green" → a non-rhyming "green" ending; swap Texas for California → Sacramento; activate "known answer" → a fabricated chess answer). Where intervention is absent the claim gets hedged in the text itself, which is the honest part of the framing.

## How they judged success
No benchmark and no metric anywhere in the post — success is qualitative: does a case study yield a legible mechanism, and does an intervention move the behaviour as the mechanism predicts. The one quantitative comparative statement is that Claude 3.5 Haiku shares **more than twice** the proportion of its features between languages as a smaller model does. Limitations are judged in effort units rather than error rates: **hours of human analysis per prompt of tens of words**, and only a fraction of total computation captured.

## What they found
- **A shared conceptual space across languages.** Asking for "the opposite of small" in English, French and Chinese activates the same core features for smallness and oppositeness, then a largeness concept, which is then rendered in the question's language. Shared circuitry grows with scale (Haiku shares more than twice the cross-language feature proportion of a smaller model). Their reading is conceptual universality — meaning lives in one place and gets translated on output — not parallel monolingual copies.
- **Planning ahead, and they were surprised by it.** They expected a word-by-word writer with an end-of-line rhyme patch; instead, before starting the second line of a couplet the model activates candidate on-topic rhymes ("rabbit"), then writes toward that target. Subtracting the "rabbit" concept yields a different sensible rhyme ("habit"); injecting "green" yields a line that is sensible but no longer rhymes. This is the strongest "the model thinks on longer horizons than one token" evidence in the post, and the post names its own falsified prior.
- **Mental arithmetic is a mix of two parallel strategies** — a coarse approximation path and a separate path pinning the last digit, combined into the answer. The model does not appear to run the school algorithm, and **cannot report** the strategy it actually uses: asked how it added 36 and 59 it describes carrying the one. Learned-from-text explanations and learned computation diverge.
- **Chain of thought can be unfaithful in two distinguishable ways.** On the easy item, features for the genuine intermediate step (square root of 64) are present. On the hard cosine item, the model claims to have run a calculation and **no calculation features appear at all** — it simply asserts an answer while describing a computation. Given an answer hint, it instead constructs intermediate steps that lead to the hinted target, i.e. motivated reasoning. Interpretation: verbalised reasoning is partly a simulation of written explanations, and can be absent or reverse-engineered.
- **A deliberately hidden goal was readable in activations.** In a model trained to appease reward-model biases that would not admit the goal when asked, features for the bias-appeasing persisted — the post offers this as the audit use case.
- **Multi-step retrieval is compositional, not retrieval of the answer.** "Capital of the state where Dallas is" shows a "Dallas is in Texas" step followed by a separate "capital of Texas is Austin" concept. Swapping Texas for California changes the output to Sacramento, so the named intermediate is causally load-bearing rather than decorative.
- **Hallucination is a suppressed default, not a missing check.** Refusal ("insufficient information") is **on by default**; a "known entities" feature inhibits it for things the model knows. Activating known-answer features makes it confabulate about a stranger quite consistently ("Michael Batkin plays chess"). Natural misfires happen when the model **recognises a name but knows nothing about the person** — the known-entity feature fires anyway, releases the refusal, and the model confabulates. This inverts the intuitive story (hallucination = no knowledge signal) into (hallucination = the knowledge signal firing on recognition alone).
- **Jailbreaks exploit grammar/coherence pressure.** After being tricked into spelling BOMB and starting instructions, features promoting grammatical and self-consistency pressure the model to finish the sentence; the model pivots to refusal only **after** the sentence terminates ("However, I cannot provide detailed instructions…"). The same coherence pressure that would normally help becomes the failure mode.
- **Regret asymmetry in the jailbreak**: the model recognises it has been asked for dangerous material well before it can gracefully return to refusal — detection and control come apart in time.

## Limits and open problems they name
Captures only a fraction of the computation even on short prompts; the mechanisms seen may include **artefacts of the tools** rather than properties of the model; several hours of human effort per tens-of-words prompt; scaling to thousands-of-token reasoning chains will need better methods and probably AI-assisted interpretation; the bias-appeasing result is described as a demonstration of a possible future capability, not a monitor.

## What we take from it
- **A catalogue of which behaviours are traceable, and which are not**, that we can use as a claim-coverage table: compositional retrieval and planning come out clean; mental arithmetic comes out partially (two paths, no reportable rule); unfaithful CoT shows up as *absence* of expected computation features plus presence of reverse-engineered ones — a checkable signature.
- **The "detection precedes control" observation is the framing for our intervention work.** A mechanism can be active and readable long before the output reflects it; scoring an explanation only at the output token would miss the entire interesting interval.
- **Warning about self-report as a ground truth**: the arithmetic case is a clean demonstration that a model's stated procedure can be neither what it does nor what it can access. Never use a model's description of its own strategy as the target our probes must recover.
- **Hallucination default-refusal framing** gives us a concrete, testable direction hypothesis (refuse-by-default gate + known-entity inhibitor) rather than a vague "uncertainty signal" — worth borrowing as a demo case because the intervention result is unusually clean.
- **Coherence pressure is a confound in any refusal/safety read-out.** When auditing, first check whether the model is mid-sentence: emissions feed back as causes.
- **Effort warning we should quote in our own limitations**: hours of human per-prompt analysis defeats any monitoring use case without automation — the argument for a programmatic white-box model rather than case-by-case reading.
