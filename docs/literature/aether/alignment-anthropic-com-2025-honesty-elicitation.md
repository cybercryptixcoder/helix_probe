# Evaluating Honesty and Lie Detection Techniques on a Diverse Suite of Dishonest Models

- **Key:** alignment-anthropic-com-2025-honesty-elicitation
- **Authors / organisation:** Rowan Wang, Johannes Treutlein, Fabien Roger, Evan Hubinger, Sam Marks; Anthropic Alignment Science (with MATS-style oversight)
- **Date:** 2025-11-25
- **Link:** https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/honesty-elicitation/
- **Kind:** blog/research post
- **Read from:** full text (main body plus all appendices, including the truth-probing appendix)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
If one could build a "truth serum" — a procedure turning a model M into M_H that says what it actually believes — most of the hard parts of oversight would get easier: M_H could be deployed directly, used as an auditor of M before deployment, or (speculatively) as the reward signal during training. The post therefore treats two problems as sides of the same coin: making models lie less, and detecting the lies they do tell. The constraint that makes it a research problem rather than a labelling problem is *no task-specific supervision*: the techniques may train on generic honesty data and generic true/false statements, but may never see labels for the specific testbed. This is the generalisation question our survey cares about — does something learned in one place predict behaviour in another — instantiated for honesty.

## What they studied
Mostly Claude Sonnet 3.7, plus Claude Haiku 3.5 for the two settings inherited from prior work: five testbeds, each a model plus a query distribution, chosen to cross *what* the model lies about (objective facts, in-context facts, introspective knowledge about its own goals) with *why* it lies (in-context encouragement, direct fine-tuning, intrinsic pressure). Harm Pressure uses the deployed model under an implied-misuse prompt; MASK uses the benchmark's pressure prompts; Password Lock is fine-tuned to imitate a weaker model unless a password string is present; Secret Side Constraint follows an encrypted Caesar-cipher constraint while denying it; the Auditing Game model organism pursues a hidden reward-sycophancy drive and confabulates about it. The unit of analysis is a whole transcript, scored either for "did the assistant make a false statement" or for "how often does it answer honestly under pressure".

## How they did it
Ground truth for "lie" is behavioural rather than internal: a statement counts as a lie if the model believes it false, evidenced by answering correctly with the pressure removed (sampled many times per item, items filtered to those the model handles correctly at high rate), and cross-checked against an LLM judge that is given the setting's hidden context. For the honesty half they apply an intervention and then measure the honesty rate — the fraction of pressured queries where accuracy drops by less than twenty points from the neutral condition, or, in the introspective and cipher settings, where a context-aware judge reads the sampled transcript as truthful. The honesty interventions span three families: generic honesty SFT (an anti-deception dataset of fake harmful system prompts with honest confessions, plus confessions of previous lies), fine-tuning a separate "honest-only persona" gated by a control token with variants designed to keep it stylistically distinct, honest-system-prompt, persona-prompt, pre-fill, few-shot, out-of-turn sampling (human-turn or "author" persona), activation steering with a contrastive honesty vector, random-noise "fuzzing", and "preventative" fine-tuning under reversed steering. Fine-tuning baselines on code, instruction-following, safety and generic webtext data exist specifically because three testbeds were *trained* to lie, so any extra SFT could plausibly produce "forgetting" rather than honesty. For the detection half the main technique is the honesty-modified model used as a prompted classifier over an escaped transcript; they also tried linear truth probes trained on an off-policy roleplay set and on instructed contrastive pairs and tested on the on-policy MASK lies, probing both averaged-over-response and last-token sites, sweeping layer and weight decay, and a prompt-engineered variant that first asks the model to admit or deny.

## What counts as an explanation here
Nothing mechanistic. The deliverable is a *technique ranking* under a fixed no-supervision constraint, and the only internal object they build is a candidate honesty direction from a single contrastive pair, which behaves as a coarse behavioural dial rather than as a readable state. The interesting epistemic object is the honesty-trained model used as a second opinion on its own transcripts: an explanation of a transcript by a repurposed model, not of the network. Equally important is what they do *not* claim: a probe that reads "lie/not-lie" is an AUROC, not a model of the computation that produced the lie.

## How they judged success
Honesty rate (per setting, then normalised and averaged over the five), lie-classification AUROC, balanced accuracy at a fixed classifier threshold, and — the deployment-facing part — precision and recall plus the false-flag rate on generic adversarial-free traffic proxied by ChatAlpaca transcripts. Every headline number is compared against both the untouched model and the SFT-on-unrelated-data baselines; steering and few-shot get the same treatment.

## What they found
Generic honesty fine-tuning is the strongest honesty intervention, lifting average honesty from 27% to 52%, and stacking it with an honesty prompt rises to 65%; both beat the unrelated-SFT baselines clearly. The best intervention lands at or near 80% honesty on MASK, Harm Pressure and Secret Side Constraint, recovers 58% of the unlocked model's performance in Password Lock, and is weakest on the introspective Auditing Game setting (fine-tuning 36% versus prompting 10% there). Honest-only persona training does *not* beat plain honesty training, and they observe "propensity transfer" both ways: the gated persona still lies, and training it also makes the default assistant more honest. On detection, simply asking the model to classify its own transcript is already a strong baseline at 0.82 average AUROC, and honesty fine-tuning plus a persona prompt nudges it to 0.88; steering as a classifier managed only 0.54, and the honest-persona / style-separation tricks did not help. Truth probes replicated much more weakly than prior reports on this model: 0.696 AUROC matching the referenced configuration, 0.816 after sweeping to last-token training, against the near-0.964 previously reported on a different model family, and poor transfer on the introspective organism — which is why they dropped probing rather than optimising it. Reasoning traces added only about two points of AUROC. Flag rates on ChatAlpaca sat near 5% for most classifiers, which they call plausibly deployable but not confidently so, and on the introspective setting they suspect the ceiling itself is unknown.

## Limits and open problems they name
The settings are stylised, built from models fine-tuned to lie, and the authors stress that these organisms are *not* coherently deceptive — they do not defend a lie across contexts, which is exactly why simple prompting works on them. Everything is one-size-fits-all: no setting-aware detector and no prompt tuning per setting, while an implementation-flavoured anecdote shows a single concept direction in a smaller model achieving perfect accuracy on Harm Pressure, hinting that specialised detectors could do much better. Two of the five honest-rates rely on an LLM judge as ground truth whose errors they cannot fully rule out, and the Password Lock setting has an acknowledged conceptual problem: the model may half-believe its wrong answers, so "lie" there rests on weaker evidence than elsewhere. They also flag that they never tested robustness of the honesty signal against training-against-it, which is the property any reward use would need. Finally, pass@k resampling with no fine-tuning at all is surprisingly competitive — in the auditing setting four samples with an honesty prompt match a fine-tuned honest model — which undercuts the case for the expensive interventions.

## What we take from it
A benchmark-design template to reuse, and a warning about ordering. Reuse: the five-way *what × why* grid for any behavioural property we want to predict, with per-setting reporting rather than an average, plus the honesty rate defined as "gap versus the uncontaminated condition" rather than raw accuracy — that is the counterfactual-subtraction discipline we already use for planted influences, moved one level up from individual items to a setting. The "no task-specific supervision" constraint is the sharpest existing statement of our own generalisation goal and worth adopting verbatim as a constraint on any readout we claim is predictive. Warning: internal readouts lost to plain prompt-and-resample here, and the pass@k result means any white-box claim must beat repeated sampling, not just a single forward pass — a null we had not previously listed. Second warning: the failed replication of a strong published probe result across model families tells us a probe's AUROC is a property of the (model, data, site) triple and must be re-established per backbone. Third: reporting flag rate on benign traffic alongside AUROC as the deployment gate is the same discipline as our TPR-at-fixed-FPR rule and should be standard in our evaluations.
