## What they wanted, and why
Every SAE result in this folder is a claim about *one* model, and each new model means a new dictionary, a new round of latent descriptions, and all downstream analysis re-pointed at a different latent space. The authors ask whether that duplication can be replaced by a single persistent dictionary that several models map into and out of, so that a label written once holds for every model that joins. The larger target is an economics one — labels are the expensive artefact, not the SAE — and a scientific one: if a dictionary really is shared across families trained by different labs on different tokenizers, then "the feature for X" stops being a per-model accident. This is the nearest thing in the folder to a claim that a white-box read-out can be *portable*, which is a background assumption our own predictive model would like to rely on.

## What they studied
Four separately released ~1B base checkpoints from four families with four tokenizers: Qwen3-1.7B-Base, Llama-3.2-1B, OLMo-2-0425-1B and gemma-3-1b-pt, each read at roughly 85% depth (a mid-depth setting appears only as one appendix table). Residual widths are 2048 for three of them and 1152 for Gemma; median token norms differ by three orders of magnitude at the hook point, with no attention-sink outliers there. Data: FineWeb-Edu, aligned across tokenizers into 4M positions (3.9M train), with a held-out shard and a 300k-position window for per-latent statistics; a separate 100k-position Wikipedia store tests corpus dependence. Dictionary 32,768 latents, k = 128. Adaptation adds three unseen models: Qwen3.5-2B-Base (a hybrid with linear-attention blocks), Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (post-trained sibling of a seed model), Hunyuan-1.8B (another lab). Unit of analysis: the shared latent, and the per-model encoder/decoder rows attached to it.

## How they did it
The aligner is a greedy decoded-text window matcher extended from two models to N: it walks every model's tokenization of the same chunk in parallel, and on a mismatch grows whichever stream's decoded window is currently shortest until all windows decode to identical text (after NFKC, casefolding and whitespace collapsing). One aligned position is emitted per closed window, at each stream's window-final token, where attention has already summarised the matched text. Around one shared dictionary of L latents sits a separate encoder and decoder per model. Each model produces its own dense code; the codes are **L1-normalised before summing**, one TopK over the sum picks a single index set for all models, and each model keeps its *un*-normalised magnitudes on that set — so selection is scale-invariant while values are not. Half the time a single randomly drawn model picks the index set alone (they call it vote-source dropout), which is what makes one-model inference in-distribution. The loss is per-model reconstruction plus a cross-reconstruction term: model m's code is rescaled to model m′'s norm and pushed through m′'s decoder, so latent i is tied to the same feature in every stream at λ = 1.0. Adaptation freezes the dictionary and trains only the new model's encoder, decoder and biases against the seed models' joint selection. Baselines are dedicated TopK SAEs matched post hoc by best correlation (an upper bound on any one-to-one assignment), SPARC (per-model encoders/decoders, one global top-k over unit-normalised inputs) and USAE, both re-run on the same store and budget. Three dictionary seeds are compared for run-to-run spread.

## What counts as an explanation here
A dictionary plus a labelling protocol, with the novelty being that the dictionary is *one object across four models*. They are explicit that the explanation is only as good as the labels: the sharedness criteria are about selection, magnitude and meaning, and they note that two of the three are directly optimised by the training loss, so the real test is adaptation (Section 5), where a new model never trained with the others must join the space. Prediction is narrow — a latent predicts that the same context fires it in every model, and that injecting it has a related effect — and they state plainly that this does not establish latent-level causal equivalence.

## How they judged success
Three criteria decided in advance. (a) Reconstruction: fraction of variance explained (FVE) on the held-out shard, against each model's *converged* dedicated SAE as ceiling, plus a function test — splice the reconstruction into the residual stream and measure CE-recovered and KL(clean‖patched). (b) Selection and magnitude agreement: effective number of models using a latent (N_eff, 1 to 4), per-pair code-mass balance, the four-way intersection of each model's own top-128 selections per aligned token, the four-way intersection of each latent's top-50 activating tokens, per-latent Pearson correlation of activations across the six model pairs, and — for checks that avoid the shared selection entirely — top-50 overlap against a matched control, rare latents whose top-15 positions coincide, and a probe with no TopK at all. (c) Meaning: FADE framework (clarity, responsiveness, purity, faithfulness) on auto-generated labels, within-stream and cross-stream, plus a blind human-style labelling test where four model-specific labels are written independently and then compared. Functional claims get their own battery: input/output scores from the "SAEs are good for steering" work, causal promotion measured by injection against a mismatched-latent null, and cross-model steering with a matched-norm random-latent control judged blind by an LLM jury. Ablations vary one ingredient at a time around one settled configuration; a planted-ground-truth synthetic model with 512 known concepts checks that forcing a shared assignment costs nothing.

## What they found
- **Sharing costs a few percent.** Mean FVE: dedicated ceiling 0.831, SharedSAE 0.799 joint / 0.803 solo, USAE 0.760, SPARC 0.732 joint but 0.613 solo, crosscoder 0.574. Spliced behaviour is nearly flat: 0.957 mean CE-recovered against the 0.963 dedicated ceiling, and it beats the budget-matched 8-epoch dedicated SAEs on three of four streams (Gemma 0.968 vs 0.943 being the visible one). KL(clean‖patched) is worse than the converged ceiling, 0.330 vs 0.274 mean.
- **Cross-reconstruction is real.** Any model's code decodes into any other model's space at 0.733–0.786 FVE (Table 2), and beats the strongest affine stitch between raw streams on all twelve pairs.
- **Solo beats joint on three of four streams** — alone, each model picks what suits itself.
- **Selection agreement:** most latents are used by all four models near-equally (N_eff near 4), while USAE/SPARC sit at the one-sided end familiar from model diffing. Per token, mean consensus is about 59 of each model's 128 latents. Restricting reconstruction to only the consensus latents costs 0.803 → 0.725 mean FVE, and *deliberately* picking each model's own strongest 59 latents does no better (0.746) — the consensus set is where the mass is, not a compromise set. Latents outside the consensus reconstruct essentially nothing (Gemma −1.139).
- **The vote-norm ablation is the one that leaves the training loss unchanged while destroying the property:** raw votes give a vote share of 0.07/0.00/0.00/0.93 across Q/L/O/G — Gemma takes essentially the whole dictionary — and normalising is what fixes it. Dropping vote-source dropout gives a Gemma solo FVE of 0.155 (against 0.821-ish scale) — the joint dictionary works but a solo voter can't find the latents. Dropping the cross-rescale gives negative FVE and Cross-FVE, i.e. divergence.
- **Labels transfer.** Within-stream FADE for shared-vote labels is comparable to dedicated SAEs (clarity 0.979 vs 0.954–0.975; responsiveness and purity slightly *higher* than dedicated; faithfulness lower, 0.281 vs 0.320–0.414). Cross-stream transfer keeps clarity 0.850, responsiveness 0.569, purity 0.401, faithfulness 0.207 at 17.2% retained, and per-latent FADE scores correlate across the six model pairs at 0.929/0.919/0.919 — but **faithfulness is the one that does not correlate (0.371)**, and they read that as semantic transfer without functional equivalence.
- **Functional alignment is partial:** input-score correlation 0.52, output-score 0.48 across pairs. Causal promotion agrees at a median top-30 token overlap of 0.19 (0.27 via linear logit lens) against a 0.00 mismatched-latent null. Blind labelling: 88 of 90 stratified mined latents got the same concept from all four models, of which 24 lexical, 51 phrasal/syntactic, 15 abstract.
- **Adaptation works and is the strongest argument.** Leave-one-out folds reach 0.972–0.984 of their own dedicated SAE deployed solo; the post-trained Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct reaches 1.006, and the out-of-lab Hunyuan-1.8B reaches 1.010. Qwen3.5 without vote-source dropout collapses to 0.436 solo with the *same* seed quality (0.937) — the dropout is what makes a new architecture usable alone. An adapted encoder/decoder pair beats a ridge linear alignment (0.692–0.670 FVE share) and a decoder-only attach (0.795–0.881) on every criterion.
- **Steering agrees in direction, not in size of failure.** Concept-rate lift is monotone in every model, +0.16 to 0.22 at α = 1 with coherence preserved, but token-level detectors and positional-slot features (month names, large quantifiers, citation years) **never steer at any dose in any model**, consistent with the input/output feature distinction. Matched-norm random controls stay within 0.04 of baseline.
- **Sharedness is not the corpus:** re-measured on Wikipedia the per-latent correlation median is 0.72 and top-50 overlap 21 of 50, essentially unchanged.
- **Planted ground truth: universality ratio 1.000** — forcing one shared assignment recovers the 512 planted concepts exactly as well as matching each model's SAE freely afterwards, and pooling across models recovers more than per-model SAEs, with the largest gain on the narrowest model.
- Weights-only evidence is weaker than selection evidence: decoder directions agree at SVCCA 0.34 / latent RSA 0.31 against a shuffled-pairing floor of 0.17 / 0.00 — above chance, well below the selection statistics.

## Limits and open problems they name
One recipe, one ingredient varied at a time, no search over the space: vote norm, dropout probability, cross-loss weight, rescale, width and k all interact, and they do not claim the settled configuration is optimal. Every number moves with aligned-data amount, training budget and the layer read; depth was fixed once at ~85% and mid-depth behaves differently. Roster bounded: four ~1B base checkpoints, English FineWeb-Edu only; larger scale and other languages untested; post-training is probed exactly once. Metrics are proxies — a system could score well on all of them while labels still failed to transfer — and two of three sharedness criteria are optimised by training. Cross-reconstruction FVEs use an oracle per-token rescale that reads the target's norm at evaluation time (the inference-safe learned per-model scalar concedes points, concentrated on Gemma). Pooled FVE and corpus-level CE under-weight rare tokens, and per-latent correlation is computed on support the shared selection shares by construction (hence the three independent checks). Single measure of alignment free of the shared machinery is flagged as future work. Feature splitting is present ("United" appears twice), so latent counts overstate concept counts.

## What we take from it
- **A framing, and the strongest one in the folder for portability:** if a read-out is to be a *model* of a network rather than a description of one checkpoint, it should survive being pointed at a different network. The adaptation protocol — freeze the dictionary, train only the new model's in/out maps, then re-check *all* the sharedness criteria rather than only reconstruction — is the test our own write-ups should borrow, and it is cheap (45–49 minutes per fold on one GPU here).
- **A method: make selection scale-blind while leaving values scale-true.** Normalising the codes *before* the shared TopK but decoding with the raw magnitudes is what lets four models with three-orders-of-magnitude difference in norm vote equally without destroying the thing a downstream slice needs. This is a two-line change with a big ablation behind it, and it is the kind of detail a reader would otherwise assume away.
- **A control: the ablation that leaves the loss flat.** Raw votes vs vote norm leaves training loss unchanged and destroys the property entirely, so *reconstruction quality is not evidence the dictionary is shared*. Any multi-stream or multi-task representation claim we make should carry one ablation of exactly this shape — a variant that the training objective cannot distinguish and the property can.
- **A measure: report transfer with the faithfulness column attached, not averaged in.** Their FADE table shows clarity/responsiveness/purity correlating across models at ≈0.92 while faithfulness sits at 0.371 — the one number that would license a claim about what the latents *do*. This is the folder's recurring separation (descriptor vs mediator) arriving again from the label side, and it is the reason their functional conclusion is "partial".
- **A warning about the split of evidence:** their selection statistics are partly true by construction, and they say so and then do the hard versions (Wikipedia store, rare-latent top-15 coincidence, no-TopK probe, planted ground truth, mismatched-latent null). The habit worth importing is to name which of your checks the training objective could have produced and then add at least one that it could not.
- **A negative result worth remembering:** some latents are detectors and will not steer at any dose, in any model — which bounds cross-model steering claims to features that are outputs, not inputs, and is a cheap pre-screen for any steering experiment we design.
