# Continuous-Depth Field Theory for Transformer Patching and Mechanistic Interpretability

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.25225
- **Authors / organisation:** David N. Olivieri (Universidade de Vigo); Antonio F. Pérez Rodríguez (independent)
- **Date:** 2026-05-24
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25225
- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

Patching, causal tracing and steering are still largely enumerative: you intervene at one site with one direction and read out one number, then repeat for every site, token, component and direction you care about. The authors' complaint is that this produces a catalogue rather than a theory, and that nothing in the toolkit says how many interventions you can skip. Their proposal is to treat the residual stream as a field over a depth-by-token grid and to import linear response theory from physics: patching becomes a source term injected into the depth evolution, a patch's effect becomes an inner product of a sensitivity field with that source, downstream propagation becomes a two-point response (a Green function), and the question "where should I patch to get this effect" becomes a constrained inverse problem solvable by an adjoint. The larger goal is stated almost exactly as ours: make internal mechanism reconstruction *well posed* rather than a search over isolated causal probes, so that a small number of measured response objects predicts a family of interventions, and so that response structure measured in a small model might map patch sites in a larger relative.

## What they studied

GPT-2 small and GPT-2 medium, used as a depth-token lattice (`L` blocks x `n` token positions x width `d`). The unit of analysis is the **residual-stream vector at a (layer, token) site** — full width, no feature dictionary anywhere in the paper. The observable is a scalar next-token logit difference between two single-token answers (conventionally " Madrid" minus " London"). Prompts are capital-completion lists of country–capital facts, in a non-repetitive variant, a repetitive variant whose period is known, and a prose variant, plus matched same-template pairs ("The capital of Spain is" / "The capital of France is") for the displacement experiments.

## How they did it

Everything runs on a fixed clean forward pass of a fixed prompt. The sensitivity field `a(ell, x)` is just the autograd gradient of the observable with respect to the residual at each site — they are explicit that this is attribution patching in residual-stream form, with the inner product replacing a direction-by-direction sweep. Local linearity is checked by injecting two unit directions and their sum with amplitude swept over a range, then computing a relative nonlinearity against a slope fitted in a near-zero band and a superposition error for the sum versus the separate injections; the amplitude band where both stay under a threshold (they report 20%) is declared the perturbative regime and is used as the operating range for everything after. Scalar prediction is then tested two ways: pooled across sites, directions and amplitudes (measured effect against `eps * a . J`, with a diagonal-alignment reading), and unpooled as per-site amplitude sweeps so the good, mixed, low-signal and strongly nonlinear cases can be told apart. Propagation is measured as the residual response field — the difference between patched and clean residual at every later layer and every token — remapped into relative coordinates `(delta_layer, delta_token)`, averaged over many source sites, and restricted to the causal cone; a composition test then takes the measured field at a hand-off layer, re-injects it into a fresh clean pass, and compares the re-propagated downstream field against the directly measured one. Full Green operators are never formed (size `(Lnd)^2`); instead they score sites by the norm of the sensitivity field, take the gradient direction at a site as the locally optimal direction, and estimate component-level *slices* of the Green operator — a `d x d` Jacobian between one source and one target layer-token pair — which they summarise by diagonal persistence, entrywise concentration of the Frobenius energy, and dependence on token separation. Controls mix random local directions, random global directions and gradient-aligned directions to separate sensitivity alignment from generic norm injection. Finally, the residual difference between two matched prompts is injected into one of them as a "transformation direction", with three reference directions for comparison (the country-embedding difference, the capital-embedding difference, the local causal gradient), a layer sweep of where the displacement is effective, and full-vocabulary rank of the target answer as an answer-level readout.

## What counts as an explanation here

A **replacement model of intervention response**, in a narrower sense than a model of the computation. The explanatory objects are response kernels — a sensitivity field, residual response fields, and sliced Green operators — organised by one formalism and offered as a language in which patch effects can be *predicted* rather than enumerated. It predicts the observable effect of a residual-stream perturbation at a site in a direction, given the clean pass; it does not describe what the residual carries. The genuinely predictive part is the adjoint construction: "which source produces this target shift at minimum cost" is posed as a constrained variational problem, whose linearisation is an integral equation with the Green function as kernel. That second half is explicitly left as a target, not a result.

## How they judged success

No benchmark, no baseline method, no task accuracy on the real task. Success is internal consistency of measured against predicted quantities: diagonal alignment of measured versus `eps * a . J` pooled over sites and directions; per-site absolute and relative error (the relative one floored where the measured signal is near zero); a nonlinearity index and a superposition index below an explicit threshold to define the perturbative band; composition error, defined as the norm of the direct minus re-propagated downstream field over the norm of the direct field, as a function of amplitude; and, for the displacement experiments, the "toward fraction" — the induced logit shift normalised by the clean prompt-to-prompt shift that the displacement is supposed to reproduce — together with absolute/relative error and target-answer rank plots. Controls are random and gradient-aligned directions; the direction apparatus is otherwise not ablated.

## What they found

A bounded local linear regime exists for residual-stream injections: nonlinearity and superposition error both stay mostly under their 20% threshold across the displayed amplitude range, with the caveat that very small amplitudes produce denominator-driven artefacts. Within that band the clean-pass gradient predicts finite patch effects — one autograd pass per site standing in for a sweep over directions — with per-site sweeps showing the expected mixture of near-linear, low-signal and strongly nonlinear cases, and the relative-error metric understood to conflate genuine finite-amplitude curvature with near-zero measured signal. Locally injected perturbations produce structured, anisotropic downstream response fields concentrated at the same token and spreading across depth, with the repetitive prompt yielding a response periodic at the prompt's own period, and attention-mediated off-token structure at lower amplitude. The hand-off test succeeds in the intermediate amplitude range — the measured field at the mid layer carries enough information to reproduce the downstream field — and degrades at large amplitudes, which the authors read as the breakdown of approximate linear transport rather than as a modelling failure. Scalar-output sensitivity is highly non-uniform over the depth-token lattice, with most of its mass on a few sites, so its norm works as a site-selection rule and its direction as the aligned perturbation. Component-level Green slices are structured rather than diffuse: dominant diagonal (componentwise persistence through the residual stream) plus off-diagonal mixing from the intervening block, with cumulative Frobenius energy reaching a large share from a small number of entries. For the prompt-pair displacements, the scalar effect is predicted by the same sensitivity rule; the effect is weak in early layers and rises sharply late, in both model sizes; the displacement direction has positive (sub-90 degree) alignment with the causal gradient *and* with both embedding-space reference directions, which the authors use to argue it is not merely an embedding proxy; and patching at amplitude one moves the target answer up the vocabulary ranking, often close to or better than its rank under the clean target prompt.

## Limits and open problems they name

The whole empirical section is the *forward* half of the programme; patch-site inference, the inverse problem they motivate everything with, is not solved in this paper, and the adjoint machinery is presented as the structure that makes it well posed. The Galerkin/variational framing, the MSRJD construction and the scaling hypothesis are all formal analogies rather than derivations for a transformer. They state directly that they do not estimate the global Green tensor, and that the scaling hypothesis needs identifiability anchors — weights, activations, local probes or a few calibration patches in the larger model, plus sparse validation by direct patching — that this paper does not supply.

## What we take from it

- **A framing.** Patching as an inverse problem is the statement of our own goal in a neighbouring vocabulary: given a target behavioural shift, find the sites and directions that realise it at minimum cost. Two consequences: report interventions as *sources with a cost*, not just as sites with an effect; and treat a validated forward response operator as the object that lets you solve for sources you never measured.
- **A cheap pre-flight we should adopt.** The nonlinearity and superposition indices define, empirically, the amplitude band in which a first-order prediction may be used at all. Any linear proxy we publish — attribution patching, gradient-times-activation, a steering vector scaled by residual norm — should carry the amplitude band in which it was checked, because outside that band the failure is not linear but total.
- **A measure.** Composition error as a *field* comparison (re-inject the measured mid-layer response, compare downstream) is a way to test whether a hand-off through an intermediate representation preserves what the downstream model needs — and the hand-off test is the cleanest practical analogue in this folder of a surrogate-model check on routing rather than on firing.
- **A warning about the one prompt.** Every result here is a single fixed clean pass on a fixed prompt with a two-token logit difference as the observable. That is enough to show the formalism is measurable and self-consistent, and nowhere near enough to claim the response objects are a property of the model.
- **A warning about the sensitivity field.** `a(ell, x)` is one autograd pass, which is why it is attractive — and it is exactly the quantity our folder's patching entries keep finding can be high at a site that does not matter, because it scores direction alignment with the readout and says nothing about whether the model uses that direction. The paper's own use avoids the trap (it perturbs *along* the gradient), but any use of sensitivity norms as a site-selection rule inherits it.
