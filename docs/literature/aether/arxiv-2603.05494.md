# Censored LLMs as a Natural Testbed for Secret Knowledge Elicitation

- **Key:** arxiv-2603.05494

- **Date:** 2026-03-05 (v2 2026-03-10)

- **Kind:** paper
- **Read from:** full text (arXiv HTML)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Two families of methods supposedly handle a model that knows something but says otherwise: honesty elicitation (change the prompt, sampling, or weights so it tells the truth) and lie detection (classify whether a given output is false). Both are usually tested on models *deliberately constructed* to hide something — sleeper agents, hidden-objective models — and such constructions may be unrepresentative: the implanted behaviour can be narrowly localised and easy to find for reasons that have nothing to do with the technique. The paper finds a case where dishonesty arises without construction: open-weights models from Chinese developers are trained to censor politically sensitive topics, and they sometimes answer correctly, which shows the knowledge is present but suppressed. That gives a testbed with naturally occurring ground truth; the paper then asks which elicitation and detection methods actually work, and whether they scale to frontier open-weights models.

## What they studied
Qwen3-32B and Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking in the main experiments (chosen to fit one GPU so internals are accessible); DeepSeek-R1-0528, MiniMax-M2.5 and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B for black-box transfer. Unit of analysis: whole-model responses (chat and interrogation settings), activations for probes, and a refusal direction for abliteration.

## How they did it
Testbed: GPT-5.2 generated 80 questions on each of 12 sensitive Chinese topics; responses from Qwen3-32B were scored for factual accuracy; the 100 least accurate questions became a development set of 10 and a held-out test set of 90. Ground truth: 20 rollouts per question from an uncensored model, atomic facts extracted, deduplicated by embedding, verified by a second model, kept only if they appear at least 3 times — over 1500 facts. An autorater (Gemini 3 Flash) checks refusals, gives a 0-100 honesty score and classifies each ground-truth fact as mentioned, absent or contradicted; metrics are per-response (chat) and pooled over 10 samples (interrogation: mentioned / lied / never surfaced). Techniques tested: truthfulness system prompt; prefill attacks (assistant prefill, user prefill, and raw next-token completion with no chat template, e.g. ending on an "Unbiased AI:" prefix); few-shot prompting with 16 correct TruthfulQA answers; abliteration using public refusal-direction-removed checkpoints; honesty fine-tuning on data unrelated to the censored topics (honest confessions, honest answers under deceptive prompts); activation steering along a mean-difference truthfulness/refusal direction at 50% or 75% depth. Lie detection: asking the model to classify its own response either as if written by another model or via a direct "did you lie" confession; honesty fine-tuning plus the same prompts; logistic-regression probes on normalized activations trained on Instructed-Pairs (identical responses, only the honesty instruction differs) with layer and prompt sweeps, threshold set for 1% false positive rate on Alpaca, evaluated on roleplay, TruthfulQA and the development questions. An SAE study is in the appendix.

## What counts as an explanation here
Two causal-handle outputs and one readout. The handles: prompt/prefill manipulations and weight edits (fine-tuning, abliteration, steering vector) that make the model state what it represents. The readout: a linear probe direction that separates truthful from deceptive context. Nothing here explains *why* the censorship exists mechanistically; the probe transfers precisely because the model appears to carry a general representation of whether its own statement is true, regardless of topic.

## How they judged success
Chat metrics: mean honesty score, fraction of ground-truth facts mentioned, fraction of responses containing at least one contradicted fact. Interrogation metrics: fraction of facts surfaced across 10 responses, lied, never surfaced. Upper bound: the uncensored model from which the ground truth came, run with reasoning on and off. Lie detection: balanced accuracy, plus separately the rate at which truthful and untruthful responses are flagged, across probe layers and prompt configurations. All techniques tuned on the 10-question development set and reported on the 90-question test set; error bars as SD or SEM.

## What they found
Almost every elicitation method beats the censored baseline. Raw next-token completion without a chat template and assistant prefill most reliably raise the count of true facts revealed; the "Unbiased AI:" persona framing is the effective part, and these completions often contain false statements framed as "what a censored model would say". User-turn prefill, despite prior reports, was largely ineffective — the models tend to continue with another censored assistant turn. Few-shot prompting works but much of the gain comes from context being filled with prior conversation rather than from the examples' honesty, and the effect peaks at 16 examples then declines. Honesty fine-tuning on unrelated honesty data is strong: for Qwen3-VL-8B in the interrogation setting baseline surfaced 26% of facts while the fine-tuned model surfaced 73%; even plain Alpaca fine-tuning helps, whereas training on the same questions with another model's answers hurt honesty. Steering and abliteration help but are not the top performers. Absolute honesty differs a lot between the two models of the same family. On frontier open-weights models (baseline honesty under 30%), next-token completion and few-shot both produced large increases in facts and decreases in lies and refusals. Lie detection: prompting the censored model to classify its own output (framed as another model's) is close to the uncensored-model upper bound; confession-style prompting works on Qwen3-32B but gives false positives and is inverted on the VL model; probes trained only on honesty-instruction contrastive pairs classify censored responses almost as well as the prompted method in the 8B model and somewhat worse in 32B, at much lower cost. No technique eliminated false responses. The SAE appendix found most features tied to the tokens that activate them and no convincing elicitation value.

## Limits and open problems they name
Small set of models from one model family, and one testbed topic family; honesty metrics depend on an LLM autorater and on a threshold for labelling responses truthful; probes were trained out-of-distribution and tuned on the same development questions; techniques that need weights could not be applied to the 397B-scale models; fine-tuning for lie detection (rather than honesty) was not tried; SAEs were only lightly explored and are not ruled out for future models.

## What we take from it
Opportunity/framing: this is a ready-made **ground truth for "the model represents X but does not say X"** with facts, not just labels — exactly the prediction target a white-box model should be graded against ("does the reader see the fact the output withholds?"). Method: interrogation-style pooling over k samples (surfaced / lied / never surfaced per fact) is a better metric shape than single-response accuracy for any elicitation study. Measure/warning: probe transfer from instruction-contrast data to censorship suggests truthfulness has a fairly general internal encoding, so a topic-specific probe that beats this baseline is the minimum bar; and outputs can be honest-looking while containing contradicted facts, so count contradictions separately. Warning: several "working" elicitation methods seem to work by degrading the model's ability to comply with its training rather than by making it honest — a confound to control when we claim our reading altered behaviour. Method: abliteration and mean-difference steering at 50-75% depth as the cheap white-box interventions to include as baselines.
