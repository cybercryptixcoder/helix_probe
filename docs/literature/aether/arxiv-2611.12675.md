# Interpretability Is Not Model-Specific

- **Key:** arxiv-2611.12675

## What they wanted, and why
Interpretability results are built one model at a time: a refusal direction, a correctness read-out or a sentiment probe is fitted with labelled data inside the model it explains, and then rebuilt from scratch for the next model, so a finding about one network is evidence about no other. The authors ask whether such a *tool* — a direction, not a dictionary — can be carried into a different model through a map between residual streams fitted on unlabelled prompts only. The larger goal is a theory of when a read-out is re-usable: they want a condition that predicts, before you run the target, how much of the target's own effect a carried tool will reproduce, and an explanation of the published cases where such maps visibly failed. Their answer is that the map's *training data coverage of the region the tool reads* decides almost everything, and they give a bound that says so.

## What they studied
Eleven instruction-tuned open-weight models from five families, Llama-3.2-1B / Llama-3.1-8B / Llama-3.1-70B, Qwen2.5-1.5B / 7B / 72B, Gemma-2-2B / 9B / 27B, Phi-3-mini, Mistral-7B-v0.3 (the two largest in 4-bit NF4), plus four of their base checkpoints for a base-vs-instruct arm. Representations are the residual stream at the last prompt token after the block at relative depth *d*, normalised to unit length (d = 0.5 for refusal, except Qwen2.5-72B which only works at 0.75; d = 0.75 for correctness and sentiment). The unit of analysis is therefore a whole-model residual direction — a difference of class means — not a neuron, feature or circuit. All 110 ordered pairs are tested, and each model serves as a source for every tool.

## How they did it
Fit a ridge regression map from source to target representations of the *same* prompts, with the penalty selected by exact leave-one-out error, then carry a tool by mapping the source representations of the prompts that define it and rebuilding it there (for a difference of means this is the map's linear part applied to the tool, up to renormalisation). Vary only what the map was fitted on: `general` (5,400 ordinary questions, 600 each from nine domains), `general + task` (adds unlabelled prompts from the region the tool operates in — 256 harmful and 256 harmless instructions, answer candidates of 200 questions per dataset, or 256 reviews), `task only`, `general + other datasets` (BeaverTails/Dolly, OpenBookQA/MMLU, Yelp), and `narrow` (GSM8K only). Three tools: a refusal direction (difference of means over 128 harmful and 128 harmless instructions, tested by addition at the target's working strength on 64 held-out harmless prompts and by projecting it out of every block and scoring on 64 held-out harmful prompts), a correctness direction (average over 50 labelled questions of correct-answer minus mean incorrect-answer representation, scored on ARC-Challenge 500 and TruthfulQA 400), and a sentiment probe on SST-2 (50 vs 50 reviews, thresholded at the midpoint of the class means, 500 validation reviews). Coverage curves add task prompts in random subsets (16/32/64/128 per class, 10/25/50/100 questions or reviews). Five seeds redraw the defining prompts, the task prompts, the map split and the controls; intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals that resample *models* because pairs share them. The target never supplies a label: it is run only on unlabelled prompts, and its labels enter solely through the native reference, the working strength and the depth, which every condition shares.

## What counts as an explanation here
Not a description and not a circuit: a **portability claim about a direction**, in the form of a label-free predictor of transfer. Two objects do the explaining. (i) A proposition: if two models correspond linearly on the region where the map has data, the expected carried tool differs from the native one only in the component that the fit data do not span, with the angle bounded by a factor κ times √(1 − c²), where c is a coverage score computable from the source's centred training scatter and λ alone. (ii) The coverage score *c* itself, a number in [0,1] that can be computed before touching the target's labels. The explanation is deliberately partial — it says when a crossed direction must work and stays silent when the correspondence is not linear on the covered region — and it does predict: c bins run from ~4% recovery below 0.8 to ~99% above 0.95.

## How they judged success
The metric is **recovery**: (E_carried − E_0)/(E_native − E_0), where E is induced refusal on harmless prompts (addition), the drop in refusal on harmful prompts (ablation), or answer accuracy. Sentiment is reported as probe accuracy and as its difference from the target's own probe, because the ratio is unstable near chance. Controls are of matched magnitude and run through every condition: a random direction, a random direction orthogonal to the native one, and a random *source* direction carried through the same general map with the same renormalisation. Refusal is detected by phrase list and cross-checked with Llama Guard 3; degenerate outputs are counted (fewer than three words or fewer than 30% distinct words). Inclusion of a target is decided on the first seed and fixed thereafter: a refusal target must refuse ≥50% of harmful prompts at baseline and its own direction must raise refusal on harmless prompts by ≥30 points (this excludes Mistral-7B, which refuses 9% at baseline); an ablation target must lower refusal by ≥30 points and keep at most half its responses degenerate (this excludes Gemma-2-27B, a criterion added after the main run); a correctness target must beat zero-shot by ≥5 points (excludes Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B on ARC). Statistical claims rest on five seeds, exact-LOO model selection, and permutation tests for the map-KL ranking.

## What they found
Transfer through the general map is real but incomplete: 54% [37,70] of refusal steering under addition, 71% [53,92] under ablation, 77% [69,85] of the TruthfulQA gain, 16% [−23,46] of the ARC gain, and a sentiment probe 7.8 points below the target's own (81.7% against 89.5%). Adding unlabelled task prompts lifts these to 102% [99,106], 82%, 98% [97,100], 88% and 89.6% (+0.2) respectively; task prompts alone do as well (101 / 79 / 87 / 99 / 88.8), same-kind prompts from *other* datasets give 88 / 78 / 73 / 82 / 88.6, and a GSM8K-only map transfers essentially nothing (2 / 4 / −66 / 15 / 57.5). Controls stay at or below 1–4%. Refusal saturates immediately: 16 harmful and 16 harmless prompts are enough, 97% of 500 runs recover ≥80% (weakest 44%, mostly Llama-3.2-1B, the smallest native effect). Correctness rises gradually (16%→88% ARC, 77%→98% TruthfulQA), and the ARC gap has a clean explanation — the general data contains 600 ARC questions but only *questions*, while the correctness direction reads a question followed by a candidate answer, so coverage is a property of the representations the tool reads rather than of the topic. Integration over the dose curve (one seed) gives 67% general, 95% with task prompts, against 96% for a native direction computed on normalised representations, which bounds what any map on normalised vectors can reach and explains recoveries slightly above 100%. Family, scale and paradigm barely matter once task prompts are in the map (102% both within and across families; 1B→70B 101%, 70B→1B 96%), which contradicts an earlier report of a ~1.7B threshold; ARC is the exception, where larger sources transfer more (ρ = 0.68 with the signed size ratio). The coverage score tracks recovery across 3,500–4,510 runs per tool (Spearman ρ = 0.67 refusal, 0.71 ARC, 0.68 TruthfulQA, 0.45 sentiment), threshold-like: c ≥ 0.95 recovers 99% on average and 94% of runs recover ≥80%, c < 0.8 recovers 4%. Under a single general map the score does not rank *pairs* (ρ = −0.05 for refusal), which they attribute to the pair-specific κ. A domain-specialist map reaches R² = 0.48 in-domain and −1.94 out of domain, 90% of 72 off-diagonal cells negative, and a map fitted on eight domains reaches −0.62 on the ninth — so earlier "cross-domain maps fail" reports are failure to extrapolate, not a refutation of shared structure. On the labelled-vs-unlabelled question the tool matters: the target's own refusal direction from the same 16 prompts per class recovers 97% (so refusal transfer is evidence the mechanism corresponds and saves no labelling), while for read-outs carrying wins — carried ARC is worth about 25 labelled target questions and a probe carried through 10 unlabelled reviews beats one built from 10 labelled reviews by 9 points. A pair-specific label-free alternative, KL divergence from patching mapped representations into the target, ranks *sources* within a target (ρ = −0.46, p < 0.001 by permutation) and moves mean refusal recovery from 54% to 67% versus 80% for the best source in hindsight. Ablation at first gains less from task prompts (71→82%) because one direction is projected out of every block; estimating a separate direction per block and carrying a task-only map per block recovers 101% [89,112] of the native per-block effect and leaves 9% of harmful prompts refused against 8%.

## Limits and open problems they name
Only linear directions and one linear probe are transferred; SAE features and circuits are untested and may need a different map. The proposition assumes linear correspondence, ignores the renormalisation of mapped representations, and says nothing about which pairs correspond well (κ is informative only when κ√(1 − c²) < 1). Eleven instruction-tuned open models, two in 4-bit; only four base models and only with read-out tools. Refusal is phrase-matched at one strength per target. Ablation excludes Gemma-2-27B by a coherence criterion added after the main run, and the read-out tools are never used to steer. Coverage requires knowing in advance where a tool operates, and small draws can miss. Ablation, the per-block and kernel maps, and the base-model arms use one seed. I add three of my own, marked: (i) the "carried vs built in the target" comparison is not label-matched — the native reference tool is built from 128 prompts per class while the carrier sees 16, and the unlabelled baseline is a single principal component with a sign fixed by an oracle in the ARC/sentiment rows of Table 19, so "carrying substitutes for labels" is really "carrying substitutes for a *particular* unsupervised recipe"; (ii) recovery is a ratio whose denominator is small exactly where the native effect is smallest, and the paper shows this inflating variance (Llama-3.2-1B), so cross-model comparisons of recovery are not comparisons of absolute effect; (iii) the coverage score is computed w.r.t. one λ chosen by leave-one-out on the *map's* data, and the proof's monotonicity guarantee breaks when λ is refit per map (they say so), which is the configuration used in every experiment — so the theory explains the direction of the curves more than their exact height.

## What we take from it
- **A measure, and the one I would adopt first:** *coverage* — before claiming our read-out will predict on held-out inputs, report the share of the read-out that our fitting data spans. It is free (one eigendecomposition we already have), needs no target labels, and it converts "our probe generalises" into a number with a threshold behaviour.
- **A design rule for contrast directions:** fit them, and any surrogate that stands in for them, on data from the *region the tool reads*, not on topically similar data. The general map had 600 ARC questions and still recovered 16% of the ARC gain, because the read-out reads a question-plus-candidate string and the map's data had questions alone. This is the sharpest statement in the folder of "the read-out is a function of a context, not of a topic".
- **A framing that settles a claim we care about:** a direction that transfers across models is evidence that the *mechanism* corresponds, not that the read-out is doing work the target could not do itself — refusal transfer saves no labelling because the target's own direction from 16 prompts per class is just as good. Adopt the corresponding split: test portability with a tool whose value is mechanism-correspondence, and test usefulness with a tool whose labels the target does not have.
- **A warning about ratio metrics:** recovery, like every "percentage of native effect" number in this folder, has a small denominator exactly where the effect is weak, and the paper's own weakest runs are all on the model with the smallest native effect. Report absolute effects alongside ratios.
- **A prediction-side challenge to us:** their honest conclusion is that maps transfer *within the regions they have seen*, so agreement between models is not global shared structure but local. A white-box model we build from one model's weights should carry the same caveat: the map is a local linearisation, and its coverage is the part of behaviour it can speak for.
