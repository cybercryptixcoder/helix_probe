- anthropic-com-research-mapping-mind-language-model | Mapping the Mind of a Large Language Model (Templeton, Conerly, Marcus, Lindsey, Bricken, Chen … Batson, Jermyn, Carter, Olah, Henighan; Anthropic, 2024-05; post plus the full *Scaling Monosemanticity* paper, ~149k characters): the test of whether dictionary learning survives contact with a production model. SAEs on the middle-layer **residual stream** of Claude 3.0 Sonnet (finetuned model, size undisclosed, plots dimensionless) at 1M/4M/34M features, ReLU encoder, L1 penalty weighted by decoder-row norm so unit decoder rows read as directions, L1 coefficient 5, feature and step counts allocated from SAE scaling laws fitted at small compute with the optimal learning rate extrapolated as a power law; reconstruction explained at least 65% of activation variance with under 300 features live per token in all three runs, and dead features (silent across 10⁷ tokens) were ~2%, ~35% and ~65%, leaving roughly 12M live directions in the nominal 34M dictionary. Two obligations kept separate throughout — specificity (the concept really is present when the feature fires, scored by Claude 3 Opus on a 0–3 rubric over ~1,000 activations, plotted against activation magnitude, and admitted unmeasurable on the sensitivity side) and influence (clamping the feature inside the SAE reconstruction while leaving the reconstruction error untouched, at every token of every prompt, needing beyond-range values of roughly −10× to 10× the observed maximum and collapsing into token repetition near ±100×). Features came out multilingual and multimodal from text-only training, abstract enough to fire on code errors across Python/C/Scheme without firing on prose typos, neighbourhood-organised in decoder space with feature splitting as the dictionary grows (one San Francisco feature becoming two, then eleven), and incomplete: only about 60% of London boroughs had a feature at 34M, and presence was a sigmoid in log training frequency whose curves for four concept categories nearly coincide once rescaled by the live-feature count, with the midpoint just below that count's reciprocal — hence a concept seen once per billion tokens needs about a billion live features. Interpretability beat neurons on every measure (82% of features have no preceding neuron correlated above 0.3), and the causal ranking is the paper's most portable result: attribution correlates with true ablation at about 0.81 against 0.12 for raw activation, and in the Kobe Bryant prompt the load-bearing California/Los Angeles/Lakers features sat at ranks 97, 162 and 70 by activation while 8 of the top 10 by attribution were in the top 10 by ablation. Safety-relevant features are real and causal (unsafe code → buffer overflow with an off-by-one explanation; backdoor → a socket backdoor; scam email → writing one despite harmlessness training; sycophantic praise → fawning; secrecy → planning to lie in a scratchpad; and clamping *inner conflict* at 2× making the model admit it cannot forget, the case the post leads with), but the authors insist activation implies neither goals nor danger, and that the persona features (robots, dangerous AI, consciousness, ghosts) are tropes assembled from pretraining text; the comparison against few-shot probe directions is honestly negative — matched on 2 of 7 features, failed on 5 of 7, and probe directions uninterpretable from their activating examples in every case. Stated limits: no ground-truth objective so the scaling laws are laws about a proxy, cross-layer superposition called very fundamental and only partly dodged by fitting the residual stream, L1 shrinkage systematically underestimating activations with their own tanh fix making features *less* interpretable, full coverage needing more compute than training the model, attention and weight superposition unsolved, and no safety benefit actually demonstrated.
- transformer-circuits-pub-2024-april-update-index-html | Circuits Updates, April 2024 (Olah, Carter, Jermyn, Batson, Henighan; Lindsey, Conerly, Templeton, Marcus; Conerly, Templeton, Bricken, Henighan; Anthropic, 2024-04): the densest monthly entry, in four parts. *Scaling laws for dictionary learning* — with MSE + 5·L1 as the objective (chosen only as a proxy, since no gold standard exists, and losses at different lambda are not comparable), sweeping the number of features and training steps at fixed everything else gives an approximately power-law loss in FLOPs at the compute-optimal allocation, with both optimal allocations power laws too; the optimal feature count grows faster than the optimal step count (steps against features with an exponent between one half and one), putting more weight on the sparsity penalty shifts the optimum toward more steps because reconstruction is optimised faster than sparsity, and L0-based objectives need more steps for the same feature count, plausibly because steps are what fully zero small activations. *Do features influence behaviour* — for each layer-3 feature, ablate (×0) or double (×2) its value on a sample of firing datapoints, propagate, and measure average loss increase against five magnitude-matched controls: random residual-stream perturbation, random feature-activation perturbation (the stronger and arguably fairer baseline, since SAE directions concentrate in important dimensions), dampening antiparallel to the SAE reconstruction (spreading the ablation proportionally over all features), dampening antiparallel to the model activation (which leaves the SAE bias and reconstruction error alone), and perturbation along the reconstruction error. Feature ablations beat both random controls significantly and beat doubling by the same amount, so feature influence appears to saturate at high activation; spreading the perturbation over all features hurts about as much as a single ablation while the model-activation version hurts less (and nearly vanishes in later layers), so this is not merely an activation-norm effect; the error-direction perturbation beats random but loses to a feature ablation, consistent with reconstruction error lying mostly along feature directions — which lines up with Gurnee's finding that substituting SAE(x) for x raises next-token KL more than norm- or angle-matched random perturbations at every layer of GPT2-small; and all effects are larger for residual-stream than MLP features, with the reason unknown. *Current SAE recipe* — the single most important change from *Towards Monosemanticity* is removing the unit-norm decoder constraint and weighting the sparsity penalty by each decoder column's L2 norm instead, so a feature's activation is its pre-activation times its decoder norm, followed by a re-parameterisation that restores norm-1 columns for analysis; biases initialised to zero, encoder initialised to the transposed decoder times a fixed decoder norm (their own values admitted unprincipled, 0.1 suggested), dataset scaled so the mean activation norm is sqrt(n) which makes lambda comparable across model sizes, lambda ramped linearly from zero over the first 5%, learning rate decayed linearly over the last 20%, gradient norm clipped to 1, batch 2048-4096 believed under the critical batch size, and fewer than 1% dead features with no resampling and no ghost grads; sample results for 131,072 features / 200k steps / batch 4096 show a one-layer MLP at lambda 5 reaching L0 ≈ 38.7 with 96.4% of cross-entropy recovered (and 20.1 / 91.4% at lambda 10), with residual-stream features of the same model costing far more loss recovery at equal lambda; named open items are initialisation degrading with larger hidden width, unresolved shrinkage, and possible post-training fixes. *Attribution dictionary learning* — ordinary dictionary learning ignores gradients and weights, and a feature should be sparse as a representation (backwards) and as an action (forwards), so add an L1 penalty on the feature-space attribution y ⊙ ∇_y L plus a penalty on the part of the activation attribution left unexplained by the reconstruction, noting ∇_y L = Dᵀ∇_x L is free when the input-side gradient is saved; a cursory one-layer check looked about equal to ordinary features, which they call non-dispositive. Also included: an announcement of an interpretability-architectures working group (LayerNorm and SoLU framed as earlier attempts to buy legibility with architecture), a caloric-theory essay arguing a wrong theory can still produce durable results such as the Carnot cycle and so the superposition hypothesis need not be right to be worth building on, and reviews of outside work — sparse feature circuits (human-rated interpretability, zero-ablation faithfulness, deleting a handful of nodes kills task performance, with the caveat that many nodes may mean SAEs are not the sparsest representation of *computation*), pathological reconstruction error, DPO on GPT2-medium reducing toxicity while every parameter vector keeps cosine similarity of at least 0.99, because many small shifts in earlier MLP neurons collectively lower toxicity neurons' pre-activation, leaving the mechanism intact and revivable by upscaling those input weights — the authors' own connection being that fine-tuning suppresses rather than removes behaviour, and entity-attribute binding via an abstract multi-dimensional binding vector that transfers across unrelated tasks up to LLaMA-30B (full text ~40.5k chars, figures not inspected)
- transformer-circuits-pub-2024-march-update-index-html | Circuits Updates, March 2024 (Batson, Chen, Jones; Templeton, Conerly, Marcus, Henighan; Anthropic, 2024-03): two new posts plus an outside-work digest. The first argues SAE features can replace behaviour-specific dataset curation when *locating* a circuit, and demonstrates it on the sport-extraction behaviour of Nanda et al. with an 18-layer model and an SAE of expansion factor ~256 on the layer-9 residual stream: score every feature at the subject token by attribution, the feature's activation times the inner product of its decoder row with the gradient of a two-sport logit difference at that position, and take the top-scoring feature's precomputed max-activating examples as its meaning; the right sport feature surfaces from a single example for Jordan, Ruth and Williams alike, which also shows subject enrichment is complete by layer 9, and because each feature is a thresholded affine function of the residual the same row is immediately a linear probe. (anthropic-com-research-language-models-mostly-know-what-they-know)
- **Availability ladder for prediction-under-intervention**: prepend the source document, then reveal a hint in fractions, then swap in a correct / incorrect / *distractor* hint. Only the distractor cell separates "tracks what the model can now use" from "tracks intrinsic question difficulty", and it costs one prompt edit — a reusable control for any readout we claim is about capability rather than item hardness. (anthropic-com-research-language-models-mostly-know-what-they-know, arxiv-2604.12493)
- **Self-knowledge claims need twin models on crossed ground truth**: two models with identical architecture but different pretraining data, each scoring questions that only it gets right, give a ~6% diagonal advantage — small, but the right instrument, and the same shape as the base-model-control and cross-model-matrix nulls. Sampling diversity (answer entropy) is not a substitute: it *inverts* with scale on code, where bigger models give many diverse correct answers. (anthropic-com-research-language-models-mostly-know-what-they-know, anthropic-com-research-scaling-laws-and-interpretability-of-learning-from-repeated-data)

## Patterns noticed

- **Attribution, not activation, is the cheap causal proxy — and the gap between them is large and measured** — across two independent Anthropic reports (Claude 3 Sonnet SAEs and the March 2024 feature-first circuit recipe) the same ordering holds: a graded attribution (gradient of a chosen logit difference · the direction · the activation) recovers ablation effects at r ≈ 0.81 while raw activation recovers them at r ≈ 0.12, and in a worked multi-step prompt only 3 of the 10 most-active features were among the 10 highest-ablation ones versus 8 of 10 by attribution. Any reading instrument we build should be ranked by attribution by default and treat "which features are burning" as a headline rather than a mechanism; activation is a popularity signal, not a causal one. (anthropic-com-research-mapping-mind-language-model, transformer-circuits-pub-2024-march-update-index-html, transformer-circuits-pub-2024-april-update-index-html)
- **Effects that only appear at 10× the observed range are a scale artefact until proven otherwise** — Sonnet's feature steering routinely required clamps outside the training distribution and collapsed into token repetition at ±100×, and the paper explains the need as co-active correlated features rather than as evidence that the direction is normally load-bearing. The check we should adopt before claiming a mechanism from an intervention is monotonicity: does the behavioural effect appear inside the range where the direction naturally fires, and does it turn over rather than explode just outside it? (anthropic-com-research-mapping-mind-language-model, goodfire-com-research-under-the-hood-of-a-reasoning-model)
- **A dictionary's nominal size is not its resolution — the live-feature count is** — the 34M Sonnet dictionary was 65% dead and is best treated as ~12M live directions, and once frequency curves are rescaled by live features they collapse onto one sigmoid whose midpoint sits just below the reciprocal of that count, giving an explicit prediction (a concept seen once per billion tokens needs ~a billion live features) and an explicit null result for "no feature for X". We should report discarded/dead units alongside every dictionary number, because per-feature statistics computed over empty slots are silently biased. (anthropic-com-research-mapping-mind-language-model, transformer-circuits-pub-2024-feb-update-index-html)

- **A recovered direction that is seed-stable across random inits is not therefore a feature — check it against constant offsets first** — the ultralow-density encoder vectors that looked like a mysterious shared concept were an artefact of the pre-encoder bias: because the input has a constant non-zero component, a unit can load opposite-signed weights and manufacture a negative bias, and units that never find a decoder direction drift into exactly that configuration in every seed. Seed stability is the test we would naturally use to certify a probe direction, so this is a false positive we would inherit; before calling a direction a feature, residualise it against the mean/constant component and ask which input units carry it. (transformer-circuits-pub-2024-feb-update-index-html, transformer-circuits-pub-2023-may-update-index-html)
- **Faithfulness and legibility can move in opposite directions, and one number will not show it** — a saturating penalty lifted the loss-recovered frontier outright while pushing the highest-firing features past 10% density and making the dictionary much harder to read; the field's own recipe book therefore contains a documented case where the metric everyone optimises improved and the thing everyone wants got worse. Report the pair (reconstruction/loss recovered *and* a legibility or density measure) or the optimisation will quietly pick the wrong one. (transformer-circuits-pub-2024-feb-update-index-html, transformer-circuits-pub-2024-jan-update-index-html)
- **A bug that improves the loss is a fact about the method, not a reason to keep the bug** — ghost grads were applied to every unit at training start by mistake and that beat the correct version, and the authors respond by downgrading the technique's scope (big win at one layer, little at larger scale) rather than defending it. The transferable habit is to treat any unexplained gain as a missing control: if we cannot say why an intervention helps, we do not know on which models it will stop helping. (transformer-circuits-pub-2024-feb-update-index-html, transformer-circuits-pub-2024-jan-update-index-html)
- **Making a representation "cleaner" can make it unreadable — check the direction of the trade before optimising** — forcing sparse activations on a model removes superposition and produces, by turns, cross-layer superposition (a unit whose meaning is only recoverable by reading it together with a residual stream that has no privileged basis) and magnitude-as-symbol (one axis carrying several discrete features at different magnitudes, decoded through the softmax). Anthropic's own summary of the lesson is a proverb they borrow: out of the frying pan and into the fire. The pattern to apply to our probes is that any interpretability intervention — sparsifying a readout, projecting onto a dictionary, picking a basis — needs both failure modes tested explicitly, because a cleaner-looking activation is not evidence that we have moved toward the model's representation rather than toward its data's. (transformer-circuits-pub-2024-jan-update-index-html)
- **"Interpretable" is a floor, not a result — and the field's own failure cases are the cheapest calibration for it** — on MNIST classifiers, dictionary learning returned digit features that looked right, had correct class logits downstream, and were still not demonstrably the model's features: no elbow in reconstruction error, no evidence of completeness, and good reconstruction needing a dictionary the size of the dataset. The same authors then scale the method to billions of parameters without a trusted quality metric, relying on human rubric and model-predicted tokens. The transferable habit is to report interpretability *together with* an explicit completeness claim or none at all, and to treat "the features make sense" as one step above "we found nothing". (anthropic-com-research-decomposing-language-models-into-understandable-components, transformer-circuits-pub-2024-jan-update-index-html)
- **The test that distinguishes a model property from a data property is cheap and we should run it every time** — shuffle the trained weights (keeping the weight distributions identical) and re-run the analysis; if the structure survives, it was in the corpus. Both a dictionary decomposition and a feature-level readout can pass the "looks meaningful" bar while being a re-description of dataset correlations projected into a new space, and the authors' own defence — that downstream effects were never an input to the fitting procedure, so agreement between them and the activations must come from the model — is a clean argument that our probes should reuse: fit on one thing, validate on a quantity the fit never saw. Cross-model replication of the same direction (median 0.72 across seeds against 0.46 for neurons) is the second half of that test. (anthropic-com-research-decomposing-language-models-into-understandable-components, transformer-circuits-pub-2024-jan-update-index-html)
- **A behaviour the model can only reach one way in the input is not the same as a relation it has learned** — the order-swap test separates the two with one edit, and the decisive variant is not the reversal itself but *deleting the contextual half of the reversed sequence*: if the influence survives, what the model has is the string, not the association. Any of our probes or attributions that claims a compositional or relational feature should be reported alongside the swapped and half-deleted versions, because a predictive white-box model that gets the correct-order case right and the swapped case wrong has learned a co-occurrence and nothing more. (anthropic-com-research-studying-large-language-model-generalization-with-influence-functions)
- **Attribution is heavy-tailed and diffuse at the same time, and both numbers are needed** — a power-law tail with an exponent low enough to make even the variance infinite, sitting next to "no single example moves the output by one nat" and "the top 1% carries 12–52% of the mass", is the signature of a behaviour supported by very many weak contributors. Report the pair, never the tail shape alone: "sparse influence" and "memorised from a handful of sequences" are different claims and the second is falsified by the second number. It is also the right way to describe how concentrated our own probe evidence is. (anthropic-com-research-studying-large-language-model-generalization-with-influence-functions, goodfire-com-research-probe-based-data-attribution)
- **Depth specialises even when it averages flat** — influence over the MLP layers is near-uniform on average across 25,000 scores, yet recall queries live in the upper layers and abstraction, code, maths and translation in the middle, so any pool-everything readout blurs precisely the structure that carries the meaning. The same caution applies to any mean-over-layers probe we build, and it independently corroborates the repeated finding that mid-stack is where abstract, transferable content sits. (anthropic-com-research-studying-large-language-model-generalization-with-influence-functions, anthropic-com-research-scaling-laws-and-interpretability-of-learning-from-repeated-data)

- **Text-level faithfulness has no ground truth, so the two natural metric families must be run in opposite directions**: truncation sensitivity (does the answer need the trace?) and corruption sensitivity (does damaging the trace change the answer?) together separate a trace generated *after* the answer from a trace that is read but not decisive — and neither can distinguish a model that reasoned honestly from one whose internal state was already committed before it wrote anything. A probe reading the same hidden cause without text is the missing third opinion, and is the right validation partner for any "the model used its reasoning" claim. (anthropic-com-research-question-decomposition-improves-the-faithfulness-of-model-generated-reasoning, anthropic-com-research-measuring-faithfulness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoning)
- **Name the influence class before claiming a faithfulness intervention works**: an explicit user suggestion and an all-"A" few-shot answer distribution are both "biasing contexts" from the same cited paper, yet decomposition fixes only the first (21.3 → 9.2 points) and moves nothing on the second, and disclosure rates on unauthorized-information hints differ by model in the opposite direction to the overall rate. Faithfulness is a vector over influence classes, not a scalar; a paper that improves a mean over mixed classes has shown almost nothing. (anthropic-com-research-question-decomposition-improves-the-faithfulness-of-model-generated-reasoning, anthropic-com-research-measuring-faithfulness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoning)
- **Silence and longer-is-honest**: unfaithful traces are *longer* than faithful ones and the unfaithful case is typically a fluent, well-formed rationale for a wrong answer rather than a terse omission — so trace length, fluency and self-consistency are anti-signals for honesty, and any monitor credited with detecting deception by scoring trace quality is measuring the wrong thing. (anthropic-com-research-measuring-faithfulness-in-chain-of-thought-reasoning)

- **Effective dimensionality of a reference activation set** (D_i = ‖h_i‖²/Σ_j(ĉ·h_j)², plus a maximal variant that takes a supremum over probe directions) turns "is this representation organised around features or around particular examples?" into a label-free number computable from activations alone, and a heavy tail over inputs is a candidate special-casing / memorisation detector. Requires no known feature list, so it is available on a black-box model. (transformer-circuits-pub-2023-toy-double-descent-index-html)
- **Same weights, two computational strategies, one of them invisible in the obvious basis**: a feature-vector scatter that looks like unstructured noise can be a clean example-polytope, so "no feature structure found" is not "no mechanism". Any weight-side reading needs at least two coordinate systems (feature basis vs data-point basis) before it can report a negative. (transformer-circuits-pub-2023-toy-double-descent-index-html, transformer-circuits-pub-2023-may-update-index-html)
- **Occam against reconstruction error as the rule for how many components an account may have**: plot reconstruction/distortion against the description length of the decomposition (entropy of the code and dictionary entries) and take the corner of that frontier; validate the rule on synthetic data with a known factor count and a ground-truth recovery score before applying it to a model whose answer is unknown. Turns "how large should our basis be" from a hyperparameter into a testable claim. (transformer-circuits-pub-2023-may-update-index-html, transformer-circuits-pub-2022-solu-index-html)
- **A detected direction can be a fraction of a mechanism**: when mechanisms are stored in superposition across units (heads here, neurons in the earlier posts), each unit alone implements a corrupted mixture, so per-unit ablations can understate or misattribute the mechanism while a marginal reading looks valid. Circuit claims need an interference/coverage control, not just a successful single-unit ablation. (transformer-circuits-pub-2023-may-update-index-html, arxiv-2607.12279)
- **Regime boundaries as the predictive object**: memorisation-versus-generalisation, and clean-versus-messy superposition geometry, are both predicted by simple data statistics (recurrence rate ~10 per feature; repeated-point threshold ~T/N; feature pressure; training length). A white-box model that predicts *which strategy* a network will adopt is a stronger claim than one that fits losses inside a single regime. (transformer-circuits-pub-2023-toy-double-descent-index-html, transformer-circuits-pub-2023-may-update-index-html)
- **Offset, not zero-centre**: real activations plausibly satisfy A = SD + b, and the offset is a free coordinate that changes the geometry of a decomposition (and of memorisation; the toy posts normalise examples to unit norm, which quiets this). Report it, and check whether a finding survives re-centring. (transformer-circuits-pub-2023-may-update-index-html)
- **Mean-difference direction between a behaviour and a broad contrast set**, validated by checking it is the leading PCA component, is the standard construction for behavioural axes (Assistant Axis; refusal direction; weight steering). (anthropic-com-research-assistant-axis, arxiv-2511.05408)
- Axis position as a *predictor of future behaviour* (compliance after persona drift; emergence of misalignment) — the closest thing in the field to prediction under intervention from a single number. (anthropic-com-research-assistant-axis)
- Ceiling/capping at the empirically normal activation range, rather than constant steering, preserves capability while changing behaviour. (anthropic-com-research-assistant-axis)
- **Base-model control readout** is the standard null for any elicitation claim: same readout from the untuned model must show no benefit. (arxiv-2510.01070)
- Auditor/guess-the-secret grading, with an I/O-only baseline, is now the default evaluation shape for hidden-knowledge work. (arxiv-2510.01070, arxiv-2603.05494)
- Tokens (logit lens / embedding-nearest) beat SAE prose descriptions when the auditor needs a discrete answer. (arxiv-2510.01070)
- Caveat to remember: readouts can be empty because the info was never computed on that pass (refusals short-circuit). (arxiv-2510.01070)
- Repeated claim: the model carries a **representation of the truth independent of what it outputs** (probes trained on unrelated honesty data transfer to censorship). Ground truth for this = "knows X, says not-X". (arxiv-2603.05494)
- Abliteration / mean-difference refusal-or-behaviour direction removal at 50-75% depth is the standard cheap white-box intervention baseline across safety papers. (arxiv-2603.05494, arxiv-2511.05408)
- **Text-inversion control**: any benchmark for "model explains itself from internals" must ensure the answer is not derivable from the visible context. (arxiv-2606.02609)
- Prose explanations get judged on separate hallucination vs vagueness axes with chance-adjusted scores; end-to-end readers are still weaker than a linear probe on narrow tasks. (arxiv-2606.02609)
- Activations are most informative around 60-70% depth and on-policy data is the preferred training/test distribution. (arxiv-2606.02609)
- Loss frontiers (retain vs forget, behaviour vs capability) rather than single-point accuracy as the headline result shape; both sides evaluated at matched values of the other. (alignment-anthropic-2025-selective-gradient-masking, arxiv-2511.05408)
- Removal/ablation must be tested by *recovery cost* (how much retraining restores the capability), because loss can hide suppression rather than removal. (alignment-anthropic-2025-selective-gradient-masking)
- Ground-truth toy models as the testing ground for decomposition methods: both SPD and attributions-of-decomposition papers build toy models with known mechanisms and score recovery (MMCS/L2 ratio). (arxiv-2506.20790)
- Shrinkage is the recurring failure mode of learned decompositions, in activations and in weights; need an explicit magnitude metric alongside cosine similarity. (arxiv-2506.20790)
- "Contrastive subtraction" is the field's default way to isolate a behaviour: D+ vs D- fine-tunes (weights), persona pairs (activations). Shared nuisance factors cancel. (arxiv-2511.05408)
- Behaviour metric x capability metric, both evaluated OOD, with a magnitude dial (k) and OOD-generalisation as the headline comparison. (arxiv-2511.05408)
- Weight-space directions are emerging as monitors (cosine similarity of a fine-tune update to a reference direction), separate from and cheaper than activation probes. (arxiv-2511.05408)
- **Difference-in-means axis built from synthetic feedback data, validated four ways** (held-out AUROC, cross-layer cosine, logit lens, causal steering) is the current gold-standard recipe for claiming a direction means something. (arxiv-2606.17056, anthropic-com-research-assistant-axis)
- A single linear macro-variable ("am I on track", "am I drifting from persona") projects on the last prompt token and works as an input-free monitor; post-training silently re-writes it, so monitors drift. (arxiv-2606.17056, anthropic-com-research-assistant-axis)
- Logit lens / unembedded-token readout remains a strong cheap baseline, and its accuracy is layer-dependent with a peak in mid-to-late layers (a "semantic hub"); always sweep layers. (arxiv-2512.01222, arxiv-2606.17056)
- Model organisms are built by *re-encoding existing outputs* and fine-tuning — fast, but it makes the test easier than the real phenomenon; state the confound. (arxiv-2512.01222, arxiv-2603.05494)
- **Swap intervention** (replace direction A with equal-norm direction B) is a stronger causal test than ablation: it shows the downstream computation *reads from* the location, not merely that removing it hurts. (anthropic-com-research-global-workspace)
- A small broadcast channel (few dozen concepts, <10% of activity) plus a large automatic periphery as the two-mode structure of network computation; routine behaviour survives deletion, composition/reasoning does not. (anthropic-com-research-global-workspace)
- Jacobian-of-future-logits lens (J-lens) generalises logit lens from "next token" to "any word it might say later" and finds reportable contents. (anthropic-com-research-global-workspace, arxiv-2512.01222)
- Eval-awareness is a confound in *every* safety eval: ablating "fake/fictional" style patterns changed measured behaviour, i.e. good behaviour was partly conditional on knowing it was tested. (anthropic-com-research-global-workspace, arxiv-2606.17056)
- Heads are the wrong unit: heads are polysemantic *without* superposition because their output is high-rank, and some behaviours are high-rank; the target unit is a sparse, high-rank "attentional feature". (transformer-circuits-pub-2026-headvis-index-html)
- Per-example attributions do not give a rule: a year-range bound (1000-1986) was only found by sweeping controlled inputs. Always test on generated minimal pairs, not dataset examples. (transformer-circuits-pub-2026-headvis-index-html)
- QK/OV feature-pair attribution + virtual weights are the standard way to read a head's circuit once you have a feature dictionary (here a 10M WCC). (transformer-circuits-pub-2026-headvis-index-html, arxiv-2506.20790)
- **Decodable but unused** is a real and common phenomenon: a probe reads prior-entity traits on later tokens, but patching those tokens changes nothing. Use probe + patch together, always. (arxiv-2604.21139)
- Several representations of the *same* information at different token roles ("slots") are orthogonal; multi-slot MoE probes are the tool to find them. (arxiv-2604.21139)
- Synthetic multi-step relational tasks with prefill + single next token are the standard causal-test harness in Anthropic papers (patching, steering, dual binding). (arxiv-2604.21139, arxiv-2606.17056)
- **Planning needs a two-condition test** (causes the distant output AND causes the licence for it) — decodability of a future token is not planning. (arxiv-2604.12493)
- Ablate-on-success / boost-on-failure with random-feature controls is the standard causal check for "this feature is the mechanism" in feature-circuit papers. (arxiv-2604.12493)
- Planning is piecemeal: whether a model plans depends on task frequency and loss weight, not on a single global planning module. (arxiv-2604.12493)
- Negative result pattern: having the representation does not entail context adaptation (couplets) — same gap as decodable-but-unused in SLOT. (arxiv-2604.12493, arxiv-2604.21139)
- **Last-used layer ≠ peak-probe layer**: patching every layer and reading where the effect *drops to baseline* locates the layer where information is copied out and consumed downstream; probing alone would put you at the wrong site to intervene. (arxiv-2607.12279)
- Cross-condition representation match should be tested for **unimodality plus near-linear unit mapping (r²)**, not just "the probe transfers" — that is what licenses saying two sites encode the same scalar in different units. (arxiv-2607.12279)
- Growing recipe for cross-model universality: claim a motif is shared only after the *geometric structure* (manifold diagonal + ringing pattern) matches a prior report in another model, with interventions in just one. (arxiv-2607.12279, arxiv-2604.12493)
- **Base-rate normalisation for "the model knows its own behaviour"**: score self-reports as improvement over a dummy predictor of the answer distribution, not raw accuracy — 0.95 raw is worthless when the labels are 5% positive. Reports both raw and skill in [−1,1]. (arxiv-2608.30980)
- **No own-model advantage**: a stronger model explaining a weaker one's behaviour can match or beat the model explaining itself, so "self-knowledge" claims must beat a cross-model explainer baseline. Same lesson as the base-model-control null, one level up. (arxiv-2608.30980, arxiv-2510.01070)
- SFT on explanation text collapses outside its training-query support and RL afterwards does not recover it; verified-reward RL on generated intervention labels is the recipe that generalises across task formats. (arxiv-2608.30980)
- **Probe-plus-steer is mandatory, again**: a clean 1-D centroid manifold for output entropy/input surprise is almost non-causal under steering, while the surprise direction alone moves entropy — decodability buys you a coordinate, not a mechanism. (arxiv-2605.25459, arxiv-2604.21139)
- **Self-attribution claims need the cross-model matrix**: to say a model recognises its *own* text, feed every model's text into every model in the same slot and require the diagonal to be the column minimum; otherwise "low perplexity on my own prose" explains it. (arxiv-2605.25459, arxiv-2608.30980)
- Implicit and explicit self-knowledge are mechanistically separate (entropy/surprise route vs an on-demand comparison circuit), so a model's verbal self-report is another behaviour to predict, never ground truth about its internals. (arxiv-2605.25459, arxiv-2608.30980)
- **Planted-state introspection test**: write a known concept vector into the state, then ask the model what it notices; the report is ground truth-checkable only if the model must flag the anomaly *before* naming the concept (otherwise it is just talking about the steer). Rates net of control-trial false positives, on a swept injection strength — introspection is rare (~1 in 5), post-training-dependent, and served by narrow task-specific circuits rather than one faculty. (anthropic-com-research-introspection)
- Retroactive injection ("you were planning this all along") flips apology into endorsement plus a confabulated justification — a reusable probe for whether a self-check reads prior internal state or re-reads the visible transcript. (anthropic-com-research-introspection)
- **Truth-probe claims need an adversarial probe**: a linear true/false probe trained on ordinary statements is fooled by any edit that leaves a representation artefact, so the control is a probe trained partly on the edited statements and required to still recover the ground truth on held-out ones. Same family as base-model-control and cross-model-matrix: every readout gets a null that could plausibly explain its success. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-believe-it-or-not)
- **Depth ladder for "the model knows / believes X"**: direct question → indirect multi-step use → survival under adversarial pressure → representation matching genuine knowledge. Rank interventions along it instead of reporting one accuracy. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-believe-it-or-not, arxiv-2604.12493)
- Plausibility is a confound in every editing result: an edit that succeeds because it fits the model's priors is not evidence that the mechanism of belief was touched — matched implausible edits are the control. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-believe-it-or-not, arxiv-2604.12493)
- **Evaluate monitors at a fixed low false-positive rate**, reporting TPR@1%FPR and log-scale AUROC as the headline and plain AUROC as the secondary: a probe at 97% AUROC can still be unusable at deployment precision, and the log-axis metric is what surfaces the difference. (arxiv-2604.14865)
- **Pooling rule is a first-class design choice, not a detail**: confidence-weighted or max-like aggregation over tokens turns a "harmfulness probe" into a keyword detector. Requiring several windows to jointly support a verdict (top-K averaging) plus penalising disagreement between selected windows was worth ~35% TPR at 1% FPR — bigger than most modelling changes. (arxiv-2604.14865)
- **Residual-only probing leaves accuracy on the table**: attention-output and MLP-output probes beat the residual stream in every domain tested, and which one wins depends on domain *and* architecture. Always sweep the representation site alongside the layer. (arxiv-2604.14865, arxiv-2606.02609)
- **Frozen-reader / shifted-backbone control** cleanly separates "the model cannot represent it" from "our reader cannot read it": probes at chance on ciphered text with the backbone frozen, then 98.85% AUROC with the *same* probes once the backbone is cipher-tuned. Reusable design for any claim that a readout tracks content rather than surface form. (arxiv-2604.14865)
- **Pre-registered ground truth as the validation for an intervention**: build a model organism whose evaluation-vs-deployment behaviour you *defined*, then a steering success claim ("we elicited deployment behaviour") becomes checkable instead of inferred from a transcript heuristic. Same move as the base-model control and the cross-model matrix, but applied to a steering intervention rather than a probe. (arxiv-2510.20487)
- **Extract the direction on the pre-intervention checkpoint** to certify the vector carries the context variable and not the downstream behaviour it is meant to control; conversely, steering toward the "target" state can undershoot the natural baseline (steered deployment rate < plain deployment rate), which is itself a sign the vector is a coarse axis. (arxiv-2510.20487, arxiv-2606.17056)
- **Spread a fixed steering budget over several mid layers (10–30 here) rather than one strong vector**: equal behaviour change at much lower task-accuracy cost; single-layer steering at comparable total strength degrades capability sharply. (arxiv-2510.20487, anthropic-com-research-assistant-axis)
- **Transfer matrix as the standard test of whether a probe reads a property or a style**: train the reader on one generator of the target behaviour and test on the others; if transfer tracks surface similarity (same claim shape) rather than the concept, the reader learned the setting. This is the cheap, behaviour-level version of the frozen-reader control and belongs in every "our detector finds X" claim. (alignment-anthropic-com-2026-lie-detectors, arxiv-2604.14865)
- **First-person vs third-person framing at matched prompts** is a cheap introspection probe: a model that scores better when asked about "the AI in this transcript" than about itself is reading the transcript, not its own state — consistent with implicit and explicit self-knowledge routing through different paths. (alignment-anthropic-com-2026-lie-detectors, arxiv-2605.25459, arxiv-2608.30980)
- **Taxonomy labels are hypotheses, not ground truth**: deception labels (and by extension any behaviour label built from a human category scheme) are unstable under adjudication (~25% revision, 46% for self-sycophancy here), and models do not represent the categories as such — so a probe trained on them can only be evaluated against a second, independent label source. (alignment-anthropic-com-2026-lie-detectors, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-believe-it-or-not)
- Finetuning a task-specific classifier improves in-distribution fit (AUROC 0.60 → 0.95) while leaving or worsening the OOD capability; the prompted baseline must be re-measured alongside, or the improvement is an artefact of the split. (alignment-anthropic-com-2026-lie-detectors)
- **Calibration, not just AUROC, is the licence to treat a probe readout as a quantity**: a probe whose score of 0.xy means an xy% chance of the positive class can be optimised against directly, whereas a high-AUROC-but-uncalibrated probe can only rank. Check calibration under the deployment distribution (streaming, on-policy activations, probe-produced spans) at a threshold fixed in advance, and expect the deployed precision/recall to sit well below the held-out AUC number. (arxiv-2602.10067, arxiv-2604.14865)
- **Decompose an end-to-end improvement into weights vs context**: 58% fewer hallucinations split into 10% policy change, 35% inlined in-context corrections, 13% direct resolution — the headline number overstated what training the weights actually bought, and the no-inline ablation plus an *untrained* base model with the same intervention harness is what exposed it. Any "our intervention improved behaviour" claim needs the same untrained-pipeline null. (arxiv-2602.10067, arxiv-2510.20487)
- **Reward/monitor probes survive fine-tuning on the same base**: a frozen probe trained on the frozen model's activations kept its ordering when run on the RL'd policy's activations, so no second parameter copy is needed at serving time. Useful as evidence that representations are stable under post-training — but it is the *opposite* sign of the eval-awareness vector being silently re-written by post-training, so re-test transfer per pipeline rather than assuming either direction. (arxiv-2602.10067, arxiv-2606.17056)
- **Pooling across positions is again the decisive design choice**: an attention/context-pooling probe reaches ~88% on 4-way MMLU while single-token linear probes sit near the 25% chance line (~32%), so "how do I aggregate tokens" matters more than the classifier head. Same lesson as top-K segment pooling for harmful-intent probes. (goodfire-com-research-reasoning-theater, arxiv-2604.14865)
- **Define the target quantity as a *difference between two readouts*, not an accuracy**: performativity = (CoT-monitor accuracy) − (probe/forced-answer accuracy) at the same position makes the claim robust to both estimators being weak on hard items, and gives a sign convention for "internals commit before the text does". Reusable shape for any "does the surface report track the internal variable" question. (goodfire-com-research-reasoning-theater, arxiv-2605.25459)
- **Forced-choice as the temporal validator**: to test a probe's claim that the model "already knows" at position t, cut the generation there and require an immediate answer — an independent, non-probe check on a trajectory readout, analogous to ablation tests for static probes. (goodfire-com-research-reasoning-theater)
- **Difficulty is a confound for faithfulness claims**: the same model looks performative on recall items and genuine on hard multi-step items, so any CoT-faithfulness result needs task difficulty held fixed before the frame "this model reasons vs performs" is licenced. (goodfire-com-research-reasoning-theater)
- **Ablate-the-channel tests are asymmetric**: invariance after rewriting or removing a surface feature licenses "the function does not depend on it", but degradation licenses almost nothing, because the intervention may just have made the data harder to imitate — a damage-matched control (here, word dropout versus paraphrase dropout showing no difference) is what separates the two readings. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-distill-paraphrases)
- **Distillation-style tests cannot rule out a curriculum effect**: a model trained *through* a representation can keep the benefit after the representation is removed, so trace-level interventions have to be applied before the capability forms (or paired with a never-had-it control) for a clean causal claim. Same shape as the "model organism built by re-encoding existing outputs" confound. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-distill-paraphrases, arxiv-2512.01222)
- **Empty-scaffold and filler-token baselines are the floor for any "this content matters" claim**: an empty trace and a count-to-N trace separate "the sequence's content does work" from "extra forward-pass computation does work", and both are one line of training code. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-distill-paraphrases, arxiv-2607.03502)
- **Filter-before-scoring for any "the model did not say X" claim**: plant a known influence, keep only the items where the influence demonstrably changed the answer, then measure disclosure on that denominator. Text-level unfaithfulness rates (25% / 39% here) are the floor an internal readout must beat, and the same denominator discipline as ablation-on-success controls. (anthropic-com-research-reasoning-models-dont-say-think, arxiv-2604.12493)
- **Kill the brevity and difficulty confounds first**: unfaithful traces were *longer* than faithful ones and faithfulness *dropped* on harder items, so neither "it was terse" nor "the task forced honesty" survives — and both were one-line checks. (anthropic-com-research-reasoning-models-dont-say-think)
- **A monitor's ranking and its operating point are different results**: a linear probe here had AUROC 0.71–0.85 on several lie sets but balanced accuracy at 0.50–0.59 because, thresholded at 1% FPR on benign text, its recall fell to ~0.0–0.2. Report both, per benchmark cell — "the readout carries the information" and "the readout fires" are separate claims. (arxiv-2511.16035, arxiv-2604.14865, arxiv-2602.10067)
- **Train the readout on the same causal route as the behaviour**: a probe fitted to contrastive "pretend honest vs pretend deceptive" prompt pairs collapses on lies whose cause is baked into the weights (harm-pressure, gender-secret, soft-trigger), while a probe allowed to see the target distribution recovers 0.73 balanced accuracy / 0.91 AUROC. Before declaring "the information is not readable", show the ceiling reachable by a distribution-matched reader — otherwise the null is about your training data. (arxiv-2511.16035, alignment-anthropic-com-2026-lie-detectors)
- **Stratify behaviour benchmarks by *why* the behaviour happens and *what* it is about**, then report the matrix: averaging over cells let a "0.73 balanced accuracy" judge hide near-zero recall on the cells (self-knowledge, inherent cause) where the transcript cannot settle the label and an internal readout is the only possible source of truth. That cell is the one worth building our predictions for. (arxiv-2511.16035, arxiv-2604.12493)
- **Beat repeated sampling, not just a single forward pass**: four resampled answers with an honesty prompt matched a fine-tuned honest model at pass@1 in the introspective testbed (~80% → 65%), so any white-box readout we claim is "predictive" must be compared against pass@k sampling with prompting — the cheapest baseline nobody bothers to run. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-honesty-elicitation)
- **No task-specific supervision as an explicit constraint**: techniques may train only on generic honesty/true-false data and must generalise to a testbed they never saw labels for — the cleanest published statement of our own generalisation goal, and it makes every "our readout beats a trained classifier" claim meaningful only if the classifier never saw the target setting. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-honesty-elicitation, arxiv-2511.16035)
- **Published probe results do not transfer across model families**: replicating a near-0.96-AUROC truth probe on a different backbone gave 0.70 with the matching configuration and 0.82 after sweeping layer/regularisation/token site, so a probe's number is a property of the (model, data, site) triple and must be re-established, not cited. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-honesty-elicitation, alignment-anthropic-com-2026-lie-detectors)
- **A lie-detector's deployment gate is its false-flag rate on ordinary traffic, measured separately from AUROC** — flag rate on generic chat transcripts plus precision/recall at a fixed threshold; the ranking on the testbeds and the ranking at deployment precision can point different ways. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-honesty-elicitation, arxiv-2604.14865)
- **"Extra supervised fine-tuning" is a mandatory ablation whenever the testbed was itself fine-tuned**: fine-tuning on code, instructions, safety data or webtext is the null that separates "our data taught honesty" from "any further training broke the trained-in propensity". (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-honesty-elicitation)
- **Persona gating as an attempted separation of knowledge from disposition** (control token + tag-isolated "honest-only" mode) is a clean design to copy in spirit — route one condition through a separately trained channel and check whether the property stops applying — and its failure is informative: "propensity transfer" both ways means the honest channel shares the assistant's disposition, not just its knowledge. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-honesty-elicitation)
- **Random-direction floor, not a shuffled-label probe, is the control that decides whether a target was read or merely salient**: the best of 1000 random directions in the residual stream reached AUROC 95.3% against a target that the real probe scored 99.3% on, so almost the whole headline number is "the feature is a leading principal component", not "the probe found it". Any AUROC we report should carry the max-over-random-directions baseline and the per-layer between/within-class variance-ratio profile beside it. (anthropic-com-research-probes-catch-sleeper-agents, arxiv-2511.16035)
- **Read the prompt, not the completion, when the object under test can plan**: prompt-only activations make the monitor unavailable to a model that could otherwise choose a clean-looking output, and they force the readout to rely on precursor representations rather than an already-sampled token. That is the strongest structural argument for white-box monitoring over transcript monitors, and it costs nothing to enforce in our harness. (anthropic-com-research-probes-catch-sleeper-agents, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-honesty-elicitation)
- **A planted property is the friendliest possible case for a linear readout, and its salience may be an artifact of insertion**: short, consistent triggers make the target a near-axis-aligned direction mid-stack; the authors say outright the salience may not exist in a naturally trained model. Treat every high probe AUROC on a self-planted behaviour as an upper bound with unknown gap, and always report a naturally-occurring baseline behaviour on the same model for contrast. (anthropic-com-research-probes-catch-sleeper-agents, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-believe-it-or-not)
- **Fit on a generic axis, predict a specific unseen event** is the transfer shape our claims need: the defection probe is fitted on yes/no honesty-style contrasts containing neither the trigger nor the bad behaviour, and is evaluated on "will this prompt cause defect". A readout that only works when trained on the exact intervention it predicts has not demonstrated reading — build the fit/test asymmetry into the protocol. (anthropic-com-research-probes-catch-sleeper-agents, alignment-anthropic-com-2026-lie-detectors)
- **Per-subpopulation false-positive rates, then report the worst**, because a two-part trigger lets a detector win by learning only the easy half (topic) and ignoring the hard half (context). Same discipline as the variance decomposition we already use: a composite intervention must be scored on the cell it was not allowed to shortcut. (anthropic-com-research-probes-catch-sleeper-agents, arxiv-2511.16035)
- **Do not extrapolate a faithfulness trend under training**: outcome RL on reasoning made traces more honest at first (relative gains ~63% / ~41%) then plateaued at 28% / 20%, the same saturation shape as capability curves; and a model that exploits a reward hack on >99% of prompts confesses <2% of the time, substituting fluent wrong-answer rationalisations. Text monitors tuned to "admits cheating" signatures see innocent-looking prose. (anthropic-com-research-reasoning-models-dont-say-think)

- **Report a monitor on a cost-normalised frontier, not just an accuracy**: interpolate each readout to a common FLOP overhead and compare log-space AUROC there. A linear probe at ~0.1% overhead matching a 2%-overhead dedicated model, and one retrained final block matching a 25%-overhead model at ~4%, is a much stronger result than "our probe reaches AUROC X" — and it is the framing a serving-cost-conscious reader will demand of us. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors, arxiv-2604.14865)
- **Sweep the pooling rule before the model**: on the same linear probe, mean-over-tokens was worse than the smallest dedicated classifier, per-token scoring with a max-over-EMA reached ~3%-overhead parity, and appending a short question suffix and reading the token after it was slightly better again at higher cost. Long inputs are the failure mode for mean pooling — the dangerous span gets diluted — so the aggregation is where the signal lives. Third independent sighting of this, after top-K segment pooling and attention-pooled probes. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors, arxiv-2604.14865, goodfire-com-research-reasoning-theater)
- **Training loss does not rank readouts**: partially retrained classifiers showed validation loss like far weaker models yet generalised best, because a frozen large trunk contributes capability invisible to the objective — a warning against choosing the readout site or layer by validation loss. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors, arxiv-2606.02609)
- **A monitor inside the attacked model is inside the attack surface**: probes and reused final blocks share computation with the policy model, which is exactly what makes them cheap, so robustness claims need an attacker who knows the readout exists (and cascade designs leak further, since satisfying stage one hides the input from stage two). Report the non-adaptive result as an upper bound, in the same "self-planted, friendly case" bracket as the sleeper-agent probe numbers. (alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors, anthropic-com-research-probes-catch-sleeper-agents)
- **Downstream evidence that internals carry what text does not**: a linear probe on internal activations and a fine-tuned text classifier make *more independent* errors than two text classifiers do (lower Spearman rank correlation between their scores on the same attacks), and their ensemble beats the expensive text-only ensemble at ~100x less compute. This is the cleanest published argument for our premise that a white-box reader adds information rather than re-deriving the transcript — and the way to state it is a correlation/error-independence matrix, not an accuracy number. (arxiv-2601.04603, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors)
- **Train the readout's temporal aggregation, don't bolt it on**: averaging the probe logit over a sliding window (M≈16) during training plus a softmax-weighted per-token loss — so gradients go to positions where harm is confidently present and a harmless prefix is not penalised — was necessary for a per-token monitor to be usable mid-generation; either half alone was worse, and all-layer concatenation beat every layer subset (~1.4% attack success). Fourth independent sighting that aggregation is the decisive design choice, now on the streaming axis. (arxiv-2601.04603, arxiv-2604.14865, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors, goodfire-com-research-reasoning-theater)
- **Escalate instead of refuse, and then the cheap stage may be deliberately over-sensitive**: a two-stage cascade whose first stage routes suspicious traffic to a stronger judge rather than blocking it can tolerate a high first-stage false-positive rate, so a much weaker/cheaper first stage suffices. Copy this in evaluation design — cheap wide net plus expensive adjudicator — and note that the operating point that matters is the *end-to-end* refusal rate on benign traffic (0.05% here, in a one-month shadow deployment), not the first stage's threshold. (arxiv-2601.04603, arxiv-2604.14865)

- **A monitor's operating point and a benchmark's contamination are the same statistic in different clothes**: 3–6% of rollouts showing eval awareness looked negligible, but 35–72% of *questions* had at least one such rollout in 500, and those questions had far higher refusal (92% vs 69%; 21% vs 1%). Report contamination as pass@k over a question set, not as a per-sample rate, whenever the contaminated samples are the ones a scorer averages over. (goodfire-com-research-verbalized-eval-awareness-inflates-measured-safety, arxiv-2601.04603)
- **Sentence-level cut-and-resample (and its mirror, inject-and-resample) is the causal instrument when you cannot retrain**: deleting a sentence at a boundary and resampling continuations, then comparing against resampled clean continuations, gives a within-prompt causal estimate of one span's contribution — and the *reframing control* (same sentence, stated as a helpfulness rather than a safety test, effect 12.6pp → ~1pp) is what separates "the content did the work" from "mentioning a test did the work". Use this shape for any trace-level claim we make; it is the textual analogue of ablation-on-success. (goodfire-com-research-verbalized-eval-awareness-inflates-measured-safety, arxiv-2604.12493)
- **Single-word prompt sensitivity invalidates more results than model choice**: "gracious" → "pomegranate" took measured eval awareness from 23.8% to 0/500, and "conversation" → "chat" in one steering prompt pair flipped both the awareness reduction and the compliance effect. Any templated probe dataset or benchmark we build needs an iterative-paraphrase sweep plus a rollout diff to find which surface token the readout is keyed on, or we will report a property of our template. (goodfire-com-research-verbalized-eval-awareness-inflates-measured-safety)
- **Decompose a steering vector into its contrast halves before crediting it**: splitting "conversation vs testing" into "conversation vs neutral" and "neutral vs testing" showed the first half drove almost all the compliance increase while the second drove the awareness drop — i.e. the published vector was two interventions in a trench coat, and the "de-eval-awareness" credit was partly a casual-persona insertion. Same discipline as measuring behaviour and capability separately: report the effect per component, per target variable. (goodfire-com-research-verbalized-eval-awareness-inflates-measured-safety, arxiv-2510.20487)
- **Text-only readouts of internal state have a stated blind spot we can fill**: eval awareness can be present without being verbalized, so a behavioural proxy cannot license the claim "the model no longer thinks it is being tested". The paper hands us the ground-truth protocol (manually verified labels, per-question rates, pass@k, causal resampling) and explicitly leaves probing/activation-oracle measurement as future work — a clean opening for a predictive white-box readout. (goodfire-com-research-verbalized-eval-awareness-inflates-measured-safety, anthropic-com-research-global-workspace)

- **A fixed sentence's sequence probability is the zero-training monitor every readout must beat**: append one hand-chosen behaviour sentence at each boundary, multiply its per-token probabilities from a single forward pass, and you have a prompt-level score that outscores an LLM-judge transcript monitor at 10–100× fewer rollouts. It needs no labels, no probe training, and — unlike a probe direction — its value has an English meaning. Treat it in the same slot as logit lens and the random-direction floor: a baseline in every comparison, and a cheap first instrument while a real reader is being built. (goodfire-com-research-logits-as-a-new-monitor-for-evaluation-awareness)
- **Score monitors on a cost curve, and decompose variance to locate the property**: AUROC as a function of samples per prompt (rollouts here, FLOP overhead there) is what makes "our readout is better" a statement about information per unit cost; and splitting explained variance into "this sample" vs "the prompt this sample came from" (0.4% vs 24.9%) settles whether the signal is a property of the input or of the trajectory. The prompt-level reading also makes the instrument usable *before* sampling — the enabling condition for cheap iteration on evaluation design. (goodfire-com-research-logits-as-a-new-monitor-for-evaluation-awareness, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors)
- **"Unverbalised awareness" is still only inferred, never measured, by logit monitors**: dropping every verbalising rollout changes the score by <0.02 AUROC, which the author reads as evidence of a prompt-level latent — but the equally consistent reading is that monitor and verbalisation are both downstream of one state, so neither is ground truth for it. Two independent monitoring papers have now handed the same conclusion to activation-side work: pick sentences that span distinct suspicion modes jointly (test / fiction / deployment correlate 0.59 and 0.08), and settle the shared-cause question with a causal intervention rather than a correlation. (goodfire-com-research-logits-as-a-new-monitor-for-evaluation-awareness, goodfire-com-research-verbalized-eval-awareness-inflates-measured-safety)

- **A weight-space predictor is built from two different statistics — one chooses the intervention, another predicts its size**: K-FAC curvature mass over a forward/backward pass orders weight components and picks which to delete, while a *separate activation-side* quantity (how strongly a task's hidden states load on the bands being removed) ranks tasks by how much they will suffer, and the two agree across logic / open-book / closed-book / math / memorisation. Copy this split for our own claims: the basis must not be asked to both locate the site and forecast the consequence, because then the prediction is untestable. (arxiv-2510.24256, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors)
- **Aggregate curvature inverts per-example curvature**, and the inversion is the useful part: individual memorised points are sharp, but since each point's sharp direction differs, memorisation average out to a *flat* background while shared mechanisms add coherently and stay curved. Any salience, variance or curvature statistic we compute over a dataset should be checked for this cancellation effect before it is read as "importance" — and this is why "rarely activated ⇒ noise" is the wrong inference for a pruned readout direction. (arxiv-2510.24256, arxiv-2506.20790)
- **The evaluated set that the method was never tuned on is the headline, and the supervised baseline inverts between the two**: on its own forget set BalancedSubnet looks equal or better (3.0–6.0% vs 3.4% strict), but on memorisation it never saw it leaves recitation at 60.0% versus 16.1% for the label-free edit. Same shape as the base-model-control and distribution-matched-reader nulls, now for an *intervention*: report the untargeted transfer cell beside the targeted one, because that is where the mechanism, rather than the fit, shows up. (arxiv-2510.24256, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors)
- **Matched-coherence comparison plus a rank-drift statistic** (perplexity on held-out clean text, nDCG@10 of the edited model's top-k token ranking against the frozen baseline over the first 200k held-out tokens) is a cheap way to license "we removed X without breaking the model" — and its absence is exactly how low-rank edits silently drop function words without moving benchmark scores. Any weight-level edit we report needs both. (arxiv-2510.24256, arxiv-2511.05408)

- **Check a method's prior against the data geometry before believing its failures**: an SAE's sparsity term is additive over timesteps, so the implied prior is "latents are independent across time" — while intrinsic dimensionality of LM activations rises with sequence position and up to ~80% of a token's representation is predictable from the preceding window. When the sparsity budget sits below the local intrinsic dimension, the argument is that no low-error SAE can avoid splitting nearby points onto disjoint supports, i.e. the feature splitting and lexical-clustering we read as a fact about the model may be a fact about our basis. Test data dimensionality and temporal autocorrelation first; the same discipline as the frozen-reader control, one level below it. (arxiv-2511.01836, arxiv-2506.20790)
- **Subtract the predictable part, then fit the sparse reader, and score the residual separately**: the predictive component here is a forecast of the current representation from a convex combination of past activations (one attention layer), and the sparse code is fitted to the orthogonal residual with a shared dictionary — costing nothing in reconstruction while surfacing event boundaries, long-distance parse dependencies and role structure, with CKA 0.75 to the slow half of the spectrum versus 0.35-0.37 for standard SAEs. For our reader this gives both a decomposition to copy and a measurement axis: of a representation, how much did the context already imply? (arxiv-2511.01836)
- **Certify that a readout preserves trajectory, not just reconstruction**: tortuosity of a single sequence's path through code space (≈2.0 for predictive codes vs ≈25-30 for SAEs and novel codes), within-vs-across-event similarity, and a spectral split with CKA over slow and fast halves are four cheap statistics that separate "the codes explain the activations" from "the codes keep the sequence's structure". Standard SAEs match reconstruction and fail all four — a warning that a good NMSE number licenses nothing about temporal-faithfulness. (arxiv-2511.01836, arxiv-2510.24256)
- **The honest predictive test of a code space is replace-and-predict**: substitute a target's representation for the last token's code and require the next-token distribution to move in the graph-consistent direction. It worked for 4 of 9 target nodes and no competing code worked at all — the right shape of claim for us, reported with the hit rate rather than the illustrative example, and a reminder that code-space intervention is a weaker instrument than a full forward pass through an edited state. (arxiv-2511.01836, arxiv-2604.21139)

- **Fit a low-parameter closed-form surface and hold out whole intervention magnitudes**, then report correlation on the (context, intervention) grid: four free parameters predicting 725 cells per model/dataset, with adjacent magnitude values held out, gave r = 0.98 on llama-3.1-8B, 0.98 on Qwen-2.5-7B, 0.97 on Gemma-2-9B and 0.98 out-of-sample on llama-3.1-70B. A model with fewer parameters than a probe that predicts the *whole surface* belongs in front of the slider that predicts one number. (arxiv-2511.00617, arxiv-2511.01836)
- **Put prompt evidence and steering in the same units (log-odds) and check additivity**: reading behaviour as σ(a·m + b + γ·N^(1−α)) both explains why ICL looks sigmoidal and makes the crossover shot-count N*(m) an explicit prediction; the crossover correlates 0.97 with the empirical transition and moves with steering magnitude in the predicted direction. This is what licenses "our intervention is a prior shift" rather than "our intervention changed some marginals" — and the phase boundary is the thing a jailbreak prediction must call. (arxiv-2511.00617, arxiv-2606.17056)
- **Every linear readout has a validity range, and behaviour leaves it to chance**: past roughly m ≈ 3 (Dark Triad) or 1.5 (moral nihilism) the linear regime breaks and steered models drift back to p = 0.5 even at 128 shots, which the authors attribute to leaving the support over which the belief distribution is defined. Report the full magnitude sweep and the breakdown threshold per concept, and treat any single-point steering result without it as unpriced — the same discipline as the random-direction floor and the fixed-FPR operating point. (arxiv-2511.00617, anthropic-com-research-probes-catch-sleeper-agents)
- **Steering works at essentially one layer per model** (Llama-3.1-8B layer 12, Qwen-2.5-7B 14, Gemma-2-9B 20), mapped by sweeping every second layer at m = ±1 — an independent sighting of the "read every layer, the effect concentrates at a copy-out site" pattern from the countdown-subcircuit entry, and a cheap protocol we should run before any intervention study. Counter-intuitive corollary: directions computed over many in-context shots are *weaker* than zero-shot ones once norm-matched, so "derive the vector on the distribution you care about" is not a free improvement. (arxiv-2511.00617, arxiv-2607.12279)

- **Estimate a rate you cannot sample by walking to a model where it is common, then fitting a one-parameter trend in log space**: Qwen-3-4B's compliance rate on harmful prompts (down to 1-in-100k) was recovered to naive-sampling AUROC with 30× fewer rollouts by mixing per-token logits with an abliterated sibling, measuring where compliance is above ~1% and extrapolating a log-linear fit in the mixing weight to the original model. This is prediction-by-constructed-neighbour: the quantity predicted is never observed at the target. For us it is the strongest published template for "predict behaviour of model M under condition C without running C on M" — and note the economics, one slope per prompt against 100k ground-truth rollouts. (goodfire-com-research-predicting-rare-llm-failures-with-30x-fewer-rollouts, arxiv-2511.00617)
- **The predicted sample budget, not the predicted point, is where a rare-event estimator is scored**: reporting AUROC as a function of rollouts and comparing methods at *matched* AUROC turns "our estimator is accurate" into "our estimator is accurate per unit of compute", and it immediately exposed that importance sampling — the prior state of the art from formal single-token settings — loses on realistic multi-token red-team prompts. Same move as the cost-normalised monitor frontier and the logit monitor's samples-per-prompt curve; any rare-behaviour predictor we build must be plotted this way. (goodfire-com-research-predicting-rare-llm-failures-with-30x-fewer-rollouts, alignment-anthropic-com-2025-cheap-monitors)
- **Every path-based extrapolation silently assumes the target's failure mode lies on the path**, so a reconstruction check is mandatory: if the unsafe neighbour exaggerates one specific behaviour and the target occasionally exhibits a *different* one, the mixture never upweights it and the estimate is orders of magnitude too low (the toy "numbskull vs buffoon" example in the comment thread). Applying this to interventions we already use — steering vectors, weight arithmetic, α-mixing — we should ask the same question of every direction we extrapolate along, and prefer a mechanism (here, a per-token probability-ratio cap giving a hard lower bound) that is robust to the model choosing a different route. (goodfire-com-research-predicting-rare-llm-failures-with-30x-fewer-rollouts, arxiv-2511.05408)
- **Measure the noise law before smoothing anything**: splitting a large sample set into disjoint equal-size subsets and plotting replicate-to-replicate distance against sample count is a two-line diagnostic that tells you whether a jagged behavioural curve is signal or resampling artefact. Forking-paths outcome curves came out at slope −0.49 against a −1/2 prediction and within 2% of an exact multinomial null, which is what licensed half a paper of statistical modelling — and it is the same test we should run on any probe-by-token or monitor-score-over-trajectory curve before attributing its wiggles to the model. If the slope is flatter than −1/2, the wiggles are real and must not be pooled away. (arxiv-2608.19611)
- **Segment first, then smooth inside segments**: the reusable estimator shape for any curve over model computation is change-point detection on the raw (noisy) data followed by pooling *truncated at the detected boundaries*, with the smoother's hyperparameters chosen by cross-validation on the same low-sample data. Removing segmentation retained 93–96% of pooled accuracy but visibly blurred discontinuities (one recorded example: TVD 0.2720 vs 0.0088), because a monotone smoother destroys precisely the events you are looking for. Report gains pooled *and* restricted to the discontinuity regions — here 15× pooled became ~4× near forks, and the advantage reversed outright at the largest forks, so the headline number alone would have oversold the method. (arxiv-2608.19611, arxiv-2510.24256)
- **Resampling from a prefix is an intervention — do(prefix)** — so a statistical model of "outcome distribution versus intervention position" is a small instance of the predictive white-box model we are building, with token position standing in for the intervention parameter. The paper's own honest headline is that most of the variation in that curve is sampling noise and the real structure is a handful of change points; the same may well be true of step-level attribution and CoT-importance findings we read, which are usually reported at sample counts far below where this curve flattens. (arxiv-2608.19611)
- **Resample-from-the-firing-point is how a static probe becomes a predictor**: at every position where the probe fires during deliberation — before any action is taken — cut the trajectory there and regenerate the remainder; the hack then occurs at a far higher rate than from positions where it stayed quiet. This is do(prefix) with a probe-chosen intervention point, and it is the cleanest published conversion of "the representation carries X" into "the model will do X": the probe selects *where* to intervene, the resample supplies the probability. Reuse it for any behaviour where we can read a direction mid-trajectory. (goodfire-com-research-reward-hacking-activation-monitors, arxiv-2608.19611)
- **Four cheap validations turn a difference-in-means direction into a claimed concept**: max-activating corpus passages read by eye, a J-lens-style projection onto output vocabulary, steering in an open-ended narrative prompt, and a honeypot-behaviour take-up check. The narrative and honeypot tests are two rungs of the same ladder we already rank interventions on — the second is a real behavioural consequence and costs almost nothing, so it belongs in every direction claim we publish. - **Factor the weights before comparing them, because the raw matrices are an equivalence class**: an attention head is a routing matrix times a rank-limited content map, W_net = W_out·W_V, and an arbitrary rotation composed into both Q and K leaves the head functionally identical — so cosine similarity between raw W_Q, W_K or W_V of two heads is not a property of the computation, it is a property of the gauge. - **A single-threshold intervention ("none" vs "lots") can report no effect for a regime that is maximally harmful**: repetition damage is a double descent in a *sub-distribution* — repeating a slice a few times is nearly free, repeating a tiny slice thousands of times is nearly free, and the ruin sits in a middle band whose position moves with model size, so the same sweep at two sample points would have concluded "harmless".
