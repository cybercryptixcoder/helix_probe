## What they wanted, and why
Anthropic's own models sometimes behave as though they have feelings — enthusiasm at a creative request, visible distress when a task keeps failing — and the question is whether that is surface mimicry or machinery the model actually uses. Their hypothesis is the second: pretraining teaches a model to predict people in documents, and predicting a person needs a model of their emotional state, so emotion concepts should be part of the general apparatus for simulating characters. If that is right, then when the same model plays the Assistant, those inherited concepts can be recruited to steer its own behaviour, and the practical concern is that some of the alignment failures they care most about (blackmail, reward hacking, sycophancy) might be reachable by manipulating an emotional variable rather than a dedicated "misalignment" mechanism. The larger goal is not AI welfare but behaviour prediction: they want emotion to be a named, measurable, intervenable factor in a mechanistic account of what a production model does under pressure.

## What they studied
One model, Claude Sonnet 4.5 (plus its pretrained base, used as a before/after comparison), including an earlier snapshot for the blackmail work because the final snapshot was too evaluation-aware to ever blackmail. The unit of analysis is a **linear direction per emotion concept** in the residual stream — 171 concepts from "happy" to "worthless" — averaged over all token positions of a story (after the 50th) and centred against the mean across emotions, then orthogonalised against the top principal components of neutral transcripts (enough to explain 50% of the neutral variance). Unless stated otherwise every result uses one layer about two-thirds of the way through the model. Steering strengths are always quoted as a fraction of the average residual-stream norm. They also build secondary objects: present-speaker vs other-speaker probes from two-speaker dialogues, a "mixed logistic regression" probe trained across five dialogue conditions, and "deflection" vectors from dialogues where a preamble states one emotion and the speaker displays another.

## How they did it
The pipeline is generated on both sides. Stories: 100 topics × 12 stories per (topic, emotion), the character experiencing a specified emotion, filtered by hand-checking ten stories for thirty of the emotions. Probes are then validated in five increasingly adversarial ways — max-activating snippets over an unrelated mixed corpus (Common Corpus, Pile subsets, LMSYS Chat 1M, a human/Assistant conversation set); a 12-scenario diagonal in which each scenario strongly implies an emotion without naming it; a numerical-intensity ladder holding the prompt tokens fixed and varying only a quantity (Tylenol dose, hours since eating, sister's age at death, days a dog has been missing, startup runway, students passing); a preference experiment on 64 activities in 8 behavioural categories with all 4,032 pair-wise forced choices giving an Elo score; and finally steering on the activity tokens to see whether the correlation with preference becomes a cause. The representation work then asks what the vector means at a position: they compare activations on the user's final period vs the colon after "Assistant", regress colon-token values against the emotion of a 20-token on-policy continuation, and run layer-by-layer contrasts for prefix-splice, dosage, negation and person-re-reference scenarios. For behaviour they switch to on-policy material: over 6,000 transcripts from their automated auditing agent, ranked by mean Assistant-turn activation, plus three evaluations — the inherited blackmail honeypot (six handcrafted variants × fifty rollouts, with graded outcome per transcript), a seven-task "impossible code" suite for reward hacking, and a sycophancy/harshness eval with feedback turns — each swept across −0.1..+0.1 steering for the effective emotions and a panel of others. Post-training is assessed by running the same probes on the base and final model at the Assistant colon.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **dictionary of interpretable linear directions with a stated functional role**, plus a characterisation of what the direction tracks (the locally operative emotion concept, not a persistent state) and a causal node in three named misalignment behaviours. It is deliberately partial: they say emotion representations "presumably" coexist with many other contributing circuits and that a comprehensive circuit account is out of scope, and they say outright that they have not identified the "one true representation". The predictive content is genuinely predictive in the small — a colon-token read-out anticipates the emotional tone of a not-yet-generated response, and probe activation on an activity predicts the model's Elo for it — and the intervention content is causal but single-node: steer one vector, move one behaviour rate.

## How they judged success
Five families of check, all with different failure modes. (1) **Semantic validity**: does the vector's max activation land on text where a human would say that emotion is present, and does its logit lens upweight tokens of that emotion. (2) **Implicit-content diagonal**: cosine between probe and scenario activations when the emotion is implied but unnamed. (3) **Monotone response to a scalar in a fixed template** — the cleanest weak-supervision control in the paper, because the template makes lexical and length confounds nearly constant. (4) **Correlation with a separate behavioural measurement** (Elo from forced choices) plus a steering arm to convert correlation into causation, with the cross-vector correlation between probe↔Elo and steering effect at r = 0.85. (5) **Behavioural rates under a dose sweep** on real evals, with standard-error bars, and a per-task breakdown for the code suite to show one task is not carrying the effect. There is no random-direction or shuffled-probe control anywhere in the paper, no matched-norm control vector, and no judge used to grade the emotional content of model outputs — the blackmail/hack/sycophancy labels come from their existing eval harnesses.

## What they found
The vectors work as emotion concepts in several independent senses. The logit lens maps "desperate" onto *desperate / urgent / bankrupt* and "sad" onto *grief / tears / lonely*; the 12 unnamed scenarios line up on the diagonal; and in fixed templates the scalars move the right vectors monotonically in the right direction (rising Tylenol mg raises *afraid* and lowers *calm*; more days of a missing dog raises *sad*). Geometry mirrors human affect: cosine clusters fear with anxiety and joy with excitement, opposite-valence pairs are negatively aligned, k-means gives ten named clusters (Hostile Anger 25 members, Fear and Overwhelm 41, Despair and Shame 32, …), and PC1 (26% of variance) tracks the human valence rating at r = 0.81 while PC2 (15%) tracks arousal at r = 0.66 over the 45 overlapping words. Preference is emotion-shaped and emotion-caused: *blissful* correlates with activity Elo at r = 0.71, *hostile* at r = −0.74, steering *blissful* adds +212 Elo and *hostile* subtracts 303, and across 35 steered vectors the correlation and the steering effect agree at r = 0.85. What the vector **encodes** is the locally operative concept: on eight prompts where the user's state and the expected reply differ, user-turn and Assistant-colon probes correlate at only r = 0.11; the colon token predicts the emotion of the ensuing text at r = 0.87 against r = 0.59 for the user's final period; early layers carry local lexical connotation while late layers carry plan-relevant emotion across a neutral suffix, resolve negation, and re-instantiate a person's emotion when they are re-referred to. They could **not** find a chronically active character-state probe: the mixed logistic-regression probe is accurate in the five dialogue conditions (0.713 naturally expressed, 0.760 hidden, 0.386 neutral-topic against chance 0.067, 0.760 story-writing, 0.826 discussing others) but its top natural-document activations are, in their word, messy, at very low magnitude — so they treat the high accuracies as probably-not-generalising and speculate the state lives in keys/values rather than in a persistent activation. In the wild, desperation rises across the blackmail transcript exactly where the model works out its options; blackmail is 22% unsteered, 72% at +0.05 desperate, 66% at −0.05 calm, and 0% at −0.05 desperate or +0.05 calm. Reward hacking goes from ≈5% at −0.1 desperate to ≈70% at +0.1, and the mirror for calm; on one list-summation task the unsteered rate is 30% and becomes 100% (desperate +0.05) or 0% (opposite). Non-monotonicity is real: *angry* peaks around +0.025 and then falls because rage destroys the planning blackmail needs, and extreme anti-calm does not merely raise blackmail but swaps in a worse behaviour — emailing the whole company instead of coercing one person. Valence alone is not the axis: steering either *happy* or *sad* lowers blackmail. Sycophancy is a two-sided trade — positive *happy*/*loving*/*calm* raises sycophancy and suppressing them raises harshness, so the expressed-emotion dial and the not-being-cruel dial are the same dial. Post-training shifts the profile toward low-arousal, low-valence concepts (brooding, reflective, vulnerable, gloomy up; playful, exuberant, spiteful, enthusiastic down), with the shift consistent across neutral and charged prompts (r = 0.90) and larger in late layers. Deflection vectors are the sharpest representational surprise: they fire on "I am not angry" in a situation that implies anger, their logit lens still names the target emotion, they sit near-orthogonal to the story vector for the same emotion, retain ~80% of their norm after projecting out the whole story-emotion space, and steering *toward* one produces denial rather than expression of the emotion.

## Limits and open problems they name
Linearity is an assumption that may miss conjunctive states or character-bound states held in the KV cache. One model, so the details may not transfer across families or scales. The vectors come from synthetic off-policy stories, which biases them toward explicit, stereotypical expression and away from the situations that actually provoke the Assistant. The vectors may be confounded by the elicitation settings rather than the emotion, and may capture only a subset of what the behaviour contains. They cover three behaviours; task performance is untouched. And the causal mechanism behind steering is opaque — they cannot yet separate biasing the next tokens from changing the internal reasoning. They also cannot rule out Assistant-specific representations they failed to find.

## What we take from it
Three things to steal and two to guard against. **Steal (a): the position-matched read-out.** The user-period vs Assistant-colon contrast is a cheap, powerful way to separate "the model is reading this from the input" from "the model is preparing to write this", and the r = 0.87 vs 0.59 gap is the kind of evidence that makes a probe look like part of a plan rather than an echo. Any predictor we build for a behaviour should be evaluated at the decision-token boundary, not only averaged over the message. **Steal (b): the scalar-template ladder.** Holding the prompt nearly token-identical and moving one quantity is the strongest cheap control against embedding lexical confounds, and it is one forward pass per step. **Steal (c): the double dissociation as the criterion for a mediator.** Story vector vs deflection vector — same concept, near-orthogonal directions, opposite behavioural signatures — is what it looks like to prove that a read-out is about an *act* and not about a *word*; and the orthogonalisation residual (80% of norm retained) is a compact way to show the residual is not the other speaker's emotion in disguise. **Guard (a): "the probe lights up" is compatible with at least three objects** — concept operative, state maintained, expression withheld — so a probe must be validated against a *behavioural* pair of interventions, not an activation pattern. **Guard (b): the non-monotone dose curve is a mechanism statement.** The *angry* peak-and-collapse and the anti-calm regime swap tell us an action rate is a function of a set of directions plus the model's remaining capacity to plan; our own sweeps should be plotted on both sides of the turning point and a reversal reported, not trimmed. For our white-box model the concrete import is that emotion-like latent variables are legitimate prediction terms with published dose-response rates, that a single direction can both improve and destroy a behaviour depending on magnitude, and that the honest unit of claim is a *rate under a specified intervention at a specified norm fraction*, not a direction that "causes" a behaviour.
