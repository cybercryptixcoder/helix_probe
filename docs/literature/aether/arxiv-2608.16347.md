# Architecture-Dependent Causal Transfer of Activation States Across Large Language Models

- **Key:** arxiv-2608.16347
- **Authors / organisation:** Fernando Cárdenas Piepereit (independent; affiliation given as Velez-Malaga, Spain)

- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why

The motivating engineering picture is model-to-model communication: when two systems talk they currently decode one another's text and re-encode it, which costs tokens, latency and some fidelity. If activation states could be handed over directly, that intermediate layer would disappear. Whether that is even possible turns on a prior empirical question the paper takes seriously: are independently trained models' internal spaces similar enough that a learned map between them can be exploited, not merely measured. The framing is careful about the difference between "these representations are similar" and "a similarity can be projected onto a target and shown to change its behaviour in a controlled way". The paper sets out to walk that ladder — similarity, then retrieval from projected states, then end-to-end causal effect during generation — under success criteria written down before each test ran. A secondary goal is to weigh the Platonic Representation Hypothesis against the data rather than cite it and move on.

## What they studied

Four open-weight models chosen so that training organisation and attention architecture vary somewhat independently: Qwen2-0.5B (Alibaba, causal), Phi-3-mini-128k-instruct (Microsoft, causal, 4-bit), Mistral-7B-v0.1 (Mistral AI, causal, 4-bit), and FLAN-T5-base whose encoder (Google, bidirectional) supplies the fourth stream. Two units of analysis. For alignment, per-layer mean-pooled hidden states over token spans for a small hand-built concept-pair stimulus set: 12 RELATED pairs that are topically or associatively linked but lexically dissimilar, and 12 SYNONYM pairs that are near-synonymous but lexically dissimilar. For transfer, a single sentence- or question-level pooled state at the mid-depth fraction (0.5), with 20 held-out questions as the retrieval candidate pool.

## How they did it

Three experiments, three pre-registered criteria. **(1) Alignment.** Extract mean-pooled representations with special tokens excluded and, for the causal models, token position 0 excluded as well — the authors report that including it drove cosine similarity to near 1.0 for arbitrary word pairs, which they attribute to a single dominating early-position activation. Score each model pair with CKA, orthogonal Procrustes, and mutual *k*-nearest-neighbour alignment, and test RELATED and SYNONYM clustering separately by permutation against a pair-similarity null, with Benjamini–Hochberg correction across all 60 model × layer × pair-type tests. A random-initialisation null of the same architecture was run for the one pair that fit in memory (Qwen2 / FLAN-T5). **(2) Projection.** Train a small MLP to map source hidden states into target hidden-state space, fit and evaluated on data disjoint from the concept pairs, and score by top-1 retrieval: is the target's true representation for a held-out question the nearest neighbour of the projected source state among the 20 candidates (chance 5%)? An untrained MLP of the same shape is the architecture control. **(3) Causal transfer.** At each generation step, re-run the whole prefix through the model with no KV cache and overwrite the mid-layer hidden state at the last prompt position with the projected source vector, then let the model generate 25 tokens at temperature 0.8. Pool the output text with the target's own method and ask whether its nearest native representation among the 20 is the source question; 10 questions × 3 seeds × 2 conditions = 180 runs; paired McNemar against a negative control that injects the projected state of a fixed wrong question; FDR across the three pairs. Two artefacts were found in a pilot and fixed identically in both arms: a repetition penalty of 1.3 (4-bit sampling degenerates without it) and injection at the last token of a multi-token placeholder rather than at position 0.

## What counts as an explanation here

An explicit transport map plus an interventional result: a projection network is the claimed correspondence between two models' spaces, and the explanation of "the state transferred" is a causal effect measured under a matched negative-control injection. The paper deliberately narrows what that licenses — it claims transfer of a representational *vehicle* (an activation pattern with a demonstrable downstream causal effect) and explicitly not of *content*, meaning or shared reference, borrowing the philosophy-of-mind distinction and the symbol-grounding problem to fix the scope. It is partial by construction: a single mid-depth hook, one projected vector, no account of what the target does with it.

## How they judged success

Per-experiment, fixed in advance. Alignment: FDR-corrected permutation *p*-values per model × layer × pair-type, plus trained-versus-untrained metric values. Projection: top-1 retrieval on 20 candidates with 95% intervals, FDR across pairs, untrained-MLP baseline. Causal: real-versus-negative-control retrieval accuracy per trial, McNemar on paired trials, FDR across the three pairs, 30 trials per pair. Supporting controls: the lexically-disguised SYNONYM set as a triviality test, the random-initialisation null, and the position-0 exclusion motivated by known massive-activation and attention-sink findings.

## What they found

**Alignment is real and needs trained weights, but the metric you pick decides the answer.** On Qwen2 / FLAN-T5 at middle layers, CKA read 0.013–0.015 for the trained pair against 0.81–0.82 for the randomly initialised pair, and Procrustes 0.11–0.12 against 0.95 — both inverted, which the authors attribute to a few high-magnitude dimensions dominating linear-kernel and rotation-based metrics. Mutual *k*-NN behaves the other way, 0.18–0.30 trained against 0.05–0.06 untrained, a factor of 3–6×, and became the primary metric. RELATED pairs clustered above the null at almost all layers for Qwen2, FLAN-T5 and Mistral (*p*_fdr < 0.05) and were borderline for Phi-3 (≈0.054), while 0 of 20 tests were significant on untrained models. The harder SYNONYM control largely failed: not significant after correction for Qwen2, FLAN-T5 or Phi-3 (*p*_fdr 0.67–0.91), with Phi-3 numerically *below* the null at some layers (0.689 observed vs 0.805 null at layer 8); only Mistral was robust at every layer. Cross-model alignment among the three causal models (CKA 0.16–0.42, mutual *k*-NN 0.19–0.58) was consistently higher than between any causal model and the FLAN-T5 encoder (CKA 0.08–0.10), i.e. attention family predicted alignment better than developer or data origin.

**Projection beats chance for the causal pairs only.** Top-1 retrieval on 20 held-out questions: Qwen2→Phi-3 45%, Qwen2→Mistral 50%, Phi-3→Mistral 45% (all *p*_fdr < 0.0001; untrained-MLP baselines 5%, 15%, 5%), while Qwen2→FLAN-T5 sat at exactly 5.0% and was not significant (*p* = 0.657).

**Causal transfer succeeded for exactly one pair out of three, and modestly.** Qwen2→Phi-3 gave 23.3% real against 0.0% negative control, McNemar *p* = 0.0156, FDR-corrected 0.047, 95% interval [10%, 40%] on 30 trials. Both Mistral-targeted pairs gave 0.0% real against 3.3% control — no effect — even though Mistral had shown some of the strongest hidden-state retrieval accuracy. That is the paper's most useful negative: hidden-state projection accuracy did not predict generation-level causal success. The authors decline to adjudicate between model scale, instruction-tuning status, layer calibration for Mistral's depth, and 4-bit quantisation as explanations.

**A qualitative observation worth keeping.** In the trials that counted as successes, the generated text was typically not the right answer to the injected question but material thematically adjacent to it — an injected question about the square of 9 produced an area-of-a-triangle calculation, a question about bicycle wheels produced text about animal leg counts. The retrieval criterion was met while the content demonstrably was not carried, which is the empirical form of the vehicle/content distinction the discussion is built on.

## Limits and open problems they name

Small and mid-scale open-weight models only (0.5B–7B), two of four 4-bit, CPU-bound execution; frontier models untested and flagged as open. The one positive end-to-end result is 30 trials wide, its FDR-corrected *p* just under convention, and the authors say to read it as real but not robust. Random-initialisation nulls exist for only one of the six pairs, so Phi-3 and Mistral significance rests on the permutation tests alone. The SYNONYM control failed in three of four models, which caps how far the RELATED clustering can be read as lexicon-independent semantic alignment rather than co-occurrence structure. The switch to mutual *k*-NN was a post-hoc choice after seeing the CKA/Procrustes inversion, which makes their numbers not directly comparable with prior work reporting raw CKA. No information-theoretic characterisation of the channel (capacity, coding scheme, error rate) despite the token-overhead motivation, and the root cause of the Mistral failures is unidentified. There is also a threat-model section: activation-level transfer removes the last text-like surface a monitor could read, extending the encoded-reasoning and steganographic-collusion risk family one layer further from legibility, scoped as proof-of-concept rather than deployment-ready.

## What we take from it

- **A warning, and the sharpest one in the folder.** Representation-level agreement between two networks is not evidence that a read-out is functionally portable. The same study gets 45–50% hidden-state retrieval into Mistral and 0.0% generation-level causal effect, so any of our read-outs that are validated by similarity-to-a-reference or by cross-model retrieval accuracy must not be described as validated for prediction. The only number that licenses the word "transfer" here is the intervention, and it existed for one pair in three.
- **A metric-selection warning.** CKA and Procrustes inverted the trained-versus-untrained ordering because a handful of high-magnitude dimensions dominate them, while a rank-based *k*-NN measure showed the expected direction. Concrete habit: when comparing representations across models or checkpoints, run a random-initialisation null and at least one scale-invariant metric alongside the linear-kernel one, and treat a metric that says untrained models align better than trained ones as a defect in the metric before a finding about the models.
- **A method we can copy directly.** Fully re-running the prefix and overwriting one mid-layer position, with the cache dropped entirely and short generations, is a clean way to do activation patching without position-id and KV-cache bookkeeping. It costs quadratic compute in sequence length, which is nothing at these lengths. Their two pilot fixes transfer as rules: injection point must not be position 0 in a causal model, and decoding artefacts (repetition) must be tuned in both arms so the intervention is not credited with them.
- **A framing for our own claims.** "Causal transfer of a vehicle, not of content" is exactly the boundary a predictive white-box model needs to state about itself, and the paper's own qualitative result — correct question retrieved from text that is topically adjacent but substantively wrong — is the demonstration of why the boundary is needed rather than pedantic.
- **A caution about the source.** This is a single-author preprint with a disclosure that AI agents implemented and debugged all code, ran the statistics and drafted the text, under a human who directed scope; the reference list was AI-checked. The pre-registration, the reported nulls and the negative results are what make it citable, but nothing here has been through independent replication, and the effect sizes are small enough that a second run could plausibly find nothing.
