# Beyond Data Filtering: Knowledge Localization for Capability Removal in LLMs

- **Key:** alignment-anthropic-com-2025-selective-gradient-masking
- **Authors / organisation:** Igor Shilov, Alex Cloud, Aryo Pradipta Gema, Jacob Goldman-Wetzler, Nina Panickssery, Henry Sleight, Erik Jones, Cem Anil (Anthropic Fellows Program, Anthropic, Imperial College London, University of Edinburgh, Constellation)
- **Date:** 2025-12-08
- **Link:** https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/selective-gradient-masking
- **Kind:** blog/research post
- **Read from:** full text (the whole page: introduction, method, results, mechanistic section, limitations; no figures inspected, so the retain/forget trade-off curve and the gradient-norm panels are known only from the prose that accompanies them; the linked paper and code repo at the top of the post were NOT fetched)
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Models pick up dual-use knowledge during pretraining, and refusal training cannot be relied on to hold, so the authors want the dangerous knowledge never to be stored in the parts of the network that get used for everything else. Data filtering is the mainstream answer and it has a structural problem: labelling is expensive, much of the harmful content sits inside otherwise benign documents, and the binary keep/drop decision is taken at document granularity, so a miss keeps the knowledge and an aggressive cut throws away neighbouring general knowledge. Their proposal replaces *deciding what to exclude* with *deciding where to store*, which is a step toward capability removal as a property of training rather than a post-hoc edit. It is also the clearest instance in this folder of an architecture-shaped answer to an interpretability question — knowledge is addressed by *parameter location*, not by a learned feature.

## What they studied
A 254M-parameter dense transformer trained on English Wikipedia, with biology articles (3.7% of tokens, labelled by Wikipedia's own content classification) as the target knowledge to remove. The unit of analysis is *parameters*, not activations: in every transformer block certain attention heads and MLP neurons are designated "forget" and the remainder "retain". A separate, much smaller controlled experiment uses bilingual TinyStories (English retain, Spanish forget) with models from 8M to 64M parameters, to study how the split behaves as scale changes.

## How they did it
Three phases, and the third is what makes it more than ordinary data filtering. (1) *Parameter designation*: choose a subset of heads and MLP neurons per block to be the removable set. (2) *Selective gradient masking*: when a training document is labelled dangerous, backprop as usual but zero the gradient on all retain parameters, so only the forget set is updated; retain data updates everything. Unlabelled data is deliberately left unmasked — allowed to update all parameters — which is the opposite of what someone worried about leakage would write. (3) *Ablation*: after training, permanently zero the forget parameters and measure what is left. The comparison arms are "weak filtering" (drop biology articles only) and "strict filtering" (also drop medicine, chemistry and environment articles), both trained normally, so the contrast is filtering-versus-localisation at matched data. Robustness is tested by adversarial recovery fine-tuning on a 50/50 mix of biology and general data, tracking how many steps/tokens it takes to return to baseline biology performance; the comparison method is RMU, a post-training unlearning procedure. The mechanistic experiment repeats training on TinyStories and then examines gradient norms on *unlabelled* data — no masking applied — to see which parameter set each data type would have updated anyway.

## What counts as an explanation here
A parameter-level localisation claim, not a feature description: the explanation is "this slice of weights is where the model's biology knowledge lives, therefore deleting it deletes the knowledge and nothing else". It is partial by design (a designated set, sized and placed by the trainer's choice, not discovered) and it makes a prediction — that data of the same type will route into the same slice even when nothing forces it to. The "absorption" claim is the only genuinely mechanistic part, and the evidence offered for it is a gradient-norm asymmetry on unlabelled bilingual data rather than an intervention on the learned circuit.

## How they judged success
Forget loss (loss on the removed domain, higher = more removed) plotted against retain loss (loss on everything else), compared as a trade-off curve across two filtering baselines rather than as a single number; retention of adjacent fields (medicine, chemistry, environment) reported as a qualitative contrast with strict filtering; compute overhead for matching retain loss against standard training; and recovery resistance measured in adversarial fine-tuning steps and tokens versus RMU. No capability benchmark is run — the authors say so, and name WMDP as the evaluation they did not do. No seed error bars, no random-designation control (i.e. no forget set chosen at random and ablated to show the effect is not just "we deleted some weights"), and no matched-loss comparison at equal parameter budget beyond the 5% compute figure.

## What they found
The 254M Wikipedia model: SGTM dominates both filters on the retain/forget curve at every point they show, and the explanation they give is the interesting part — document-level labelling means non-biology articles still carry embedded biology, so weak filtering leaks and strict filtering pays in adjacent fields, while localisation can capture biology wherever it appears. SGTM kept more of the neighbouring fields than strict filtering while removing more biology than weak filtering. Cost: about 5% extra compute to reach the same retain loss as standard training. Adversarial recovery: RMU reacquired baseline biology performance in 50 fine-tuning steps (13M tokens), SGTM needed 350 steps (92M tokens), roughly seven times more, and SGTM's resistance matched strict filtering rather than merely beating post-hoc unlearning. Mechanistic: on unlabelled data, forget-type content shows larger gradient norms in forget parameters and retain-type content in retain parameters, which they read as the self-reinforcing absorption that explains the label-noise robustness; the effect also strengthens with model scale, with less and less leakage of forget information into retain parameters from 8M to 64M parameters, though the concrete leakage numbers are only in the figure.

## Limits and open problems they name
Small models only (up to 254M) and dense transformers only — mixture-of-experts is untested and is now the common architecture at scale; removal is measured by loss, never by a capability benchmark like WMDP; scaling behaviour is unknown. They also state plainly that SGTM cannot do anything about in-context attacks where the adversary supplies the knowledge in the prompt, which goes some way to putting it on the same footing as filtering, and they suggest pairing it with input filtering and output monitoring. A dual-model deployment idea follows: train once, keep both the full model and the ablated one.

## What we take from it
- **A warning against reading location as mechanism.** The pre-ablation "explanation" is a trainer-imposed partition of parameters; the evidence that the model agrees with the partition is a gradient-norm asymmetry on unlabelled data, not an intervention. For our own white-box model the analogue is a partition of activations into "the part that computes X" that we *chose* — and the only thing that upgrades chosen to discovered is showing the rest of the model treats the partition as real when nothing forces it to.
- **Absorption is the cheapest available test of a locality claim, and it costs one forward-backward pass.** The experiment is: take held-out data of the target type, apply *no* masking, and measure where the gradient lands. If it concentrates where the training pressure concentrated the parameter, the partition is self-sustaining; if it spreads, the partition was an artefact of the masking. We can run this on any direction or dictionary we claim is a compartment, using gradient attribution rather than parameter sets.
- **The robustness metric is a recovery budget, not an accuracy drop.** Measuring removal as "how many fine-tuning steps until the behaviour returns" (and comparing against the cheapest alternative unlearner) turns "is it gone or merely suppressed" into a number, and it is the right shape for any claim of ours that a behaviour was eliminated rather than hidden. Note the honest ceiling they report: the method merely *matches* the strict-data-filtering arm, which is the baseline that had access to a cleaner label set.
- **Their strongest argument is a labelling-granularity argument, and that generalises to every classifier-gated pipeline.** Whole-document decisions versus per-token containment is the same defect as a data filter that subtracts two judge scores, or a coarse applicability classifier leaving "applicable but not applied" examples behind (see `alignment-anthropic-com-2025-auditing-mo-replication`). Any of our pipelines that gates on a label should be checked for what it does to the content that straddles the label boundary.
- **Read the compute line as part of the claim.** A 5% training-compute penalty for capabilities that must not exist is cheap, and stating the cost beside the trade-off is the habit; a trade-off curve without its price is not a recommendation.
