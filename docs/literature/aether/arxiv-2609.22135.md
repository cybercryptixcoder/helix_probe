## What they wanted, and why
Representation-engineering practice picks a single injection layer for activation steering by consulting a linear probe: wherever the concept reads out most accurately is taken to be where injecting it will work best. The authors observe that this equation of *readability* with *steerability* is almost never stated and has never been tested causally, and that if it is false then a large fraction of published steering results may have been intervened at layers that do essentially nothing — understating what the method can achieve and misattributing failures to directions rather than to layer choice. They test it across omni-modal models, where text, audio and image all land in one residual stream, using emotion as a deliberately convenient concept that is linearly readable and directionally steerable. The larger goal is a *selection criterion* for intervention sites: if there is a layer band whose steering effectiveness is stable across architectures while probing peaks are not, the field has a rule that does not require re-deriving a probing curve per model, and the beginning of a theory that predicts where intervention works from a property of the forward pass.

## What they studied
Three independently built omni-modal LLMs: Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (28-layer Thinker backbone, hidden 3584, the primary model), Phi-4-Multimodal-Instruct (3.8B LLM backbone, 32 layers, hidden 3072) and MiniCPM-o-4.5 (36 layers, hidden 4096); no shared LLM backbone, tokenizer or modality-encoder lineage. Data is a self-built fixed set of 5 emotions × 3 modalities × 25 samples (375 total), plus GoEmotions, RAVDESS speech and EmoSet images for scaled-up and cross-domain arms, and ISEAR/DailyDialog for a domain-shift control. The unit of analysis is a **per-layer, mean-pooled residual-stream vector** and, derived from it, a single unit *direction* — a whitened class-mean difference with the top two dataset principal components projected out — so the study is about layers and directions, not features, heads or circuits. Probe training is linear on whitened activations with 5-fold cross-validation; steering is additive injection whose norm is a fixed fraction α of the current hidden-state norm at each targeted layer.

## How they did it
Two curves are measured over the depth of each model in the same representation space and then compared peak to peak. (1) *Readability*: for every layer, mean-pool the hidden state, whiten it, fit a linear 5-way emotion probe, and record accuracy and cross-modal transfer accuracy (probe trained on one modality, applied to another). (2) *Steerability*: for every layer, extract the emotion direction at that layer, inject it during generation of 30 neutral prompts at α = +0.12 (also swept ±0.04/0.08/0.12), and score the change in target-emotion probability as judged by an *external* classifier (a distilroberta emotion model folded into the 5-class space), with VADER valence as a cross-check and an independent LLM judge at n = 150. Every steering condition is paired with a same-magnitude random direction, and an out-of-distribution/degradation rate is monitored throughout. A 28-layer single-layer sweep on Qwen (3 emotions × 2 α × 5 seeds × 20 prompts, 16,800 generations) gives the full layer-wise causal map; twelve layer-*pair* injections test whether pairs are super-additive; and the same scan is repeated on the other two models under normalized-depth mapping. Two dissociation criteria are **pre-registered** before the cross-model extension — layer-wise Pearson r < 0.30, or peak separation ≥ 5 layers — and results failing a pre-registered threshold are reported rather than retried. A logit-lens analysis (projecting each layer's state through the unembedding and tracking the target emotion token's vocabulary rank) orders the forward pass; the ordering motivates a two-factor model of steering effect (probe accuracy above a threshold) × (remaining layers above a threshold), whose two free parameters are grid-searched per model to predict the steering peak. Causal control extends to non-text inputs (100 RAVDESS utterances, 102 EmoSet images) and to a 5×5 cross-injection matrix scoring each direction by its on-target increment minus its largest off-target increment, and language/reasoning damage is checked with WikiText perplexity and GSM8K accuracy.

## What counts as an explanation here
A **dissociation plus a mechanism sketch**: two layer-wise curves whose peaks are shown to be far apart under control, an account of *why* in terms of where in the forward pass each operation acts (the representation becomes stable, then becomes linearly separable enough for a probe to read, then is committed to a token — and steering works in the stable-but-uncommitted window), and a falsifiable two-factor formula intended to predict the intervention-optimal layer from diagnostics that need no intervention. It is deliberately partial: the formula is claimed as a first step, not a law, and the authors name its failure mode (a third, architecture-specific factor) as the central open problem. It does predict — it is tested by whether the predicted peak layer matches the measured one, and it fails visibly on one of three models — but it explains layer *selection*, not what the injected direction means or why a given emotion is steerable and another is not.

## How they judged success
The steering metric is Δ_target: the change in an external classifier's probability for the target emotion between steered and baseline generations, averaged over prompts, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals from 1,000 resamples over 5 seeds; effects are counted only when the interval excludes zero. Controls are (a) a paired random direction of the same magnitude in every condition, (b) VADER valence as a second read-out, (c) an independent LLM judge on a larger prompt set with a reported per-sample Spearman correlation to the primary evaluator, and (d) monitoring of an OOD/degradation rate. Cross-model success is judged against pre-registered criteria (dissociation = peak gap ≥ 5 layers or r < 0.30; that criterion is carried across architectures unchanged), not against a post-hoc reading of the curves. Side effects are measured as perplexity on WikiText, an LLM fluency score, and GSM8K accuracy. The predictive model is scored by absolute layer error between predicted and measured steering peak. There is no human rating and no downstream-task benchmark beyond GSM8K.

## What they found
The assumption fails, and the failure is asymmetric in an informative way. On Qwen2.5-Omni the cross-modal text-target probe peaks at L27, while single-layer injection of the same direction at L27 changes judged joy by +0.02 with an interval containing zero — statistically indistinguishable from its own random-direction control (+0.02) — whereas the same direction injected in normalized fashion across L10/14/18/22 gives +0.549 [0.393, 0.693], roughly 26× larger with non-overlapping intervals; a centroid-direction baseline at the same layers matches (+0.573) and a single mid-to-late layer (L18) alone reaches +0.152, about 7× the probe-best single layer, so **position rather than layer count carries the effect**. The full 28-layer sweep is near zero at both ends and single-peaked mid-to-late (joy best at L18, +0.194; anger L17, +0.079; sadness L20, +0.040), and pairs injected in the mid-to-late corridor are super-additive in 8/12 (joy) and 7/12 (anger) combinations. Across the three architectures the probe-best layer lands at normalized depths 1.00, 0.81 and 0.29 — nearly the whole network, and on MiniCPM the ordering is reversed, probing peaking in the *early* network — while the steering-best layer stays inside a narrow mid-to-late band of normalized depth [0.48, 0.74]; peak gaps are 7, 10 and 9 layers and all pass the pre-registered criterion, with layer-wise correlations 0.45, 0.55 and 0.36. The logit-lens ordering is steering peak (L19) → probing saturation (L20) → vocabulary commitment (L23), so the hypothesis that commitment precedes saturation is rejected while the other two pre-registered hypotheses hold, which the authors read as the representation stabilising and then being linearized into token logits, leaving the causal window in the uncommitted state. The two-factor predictor hits Qwen exactly (L20 predicted, L20 measured), misses Phi-4 by one layer, and fails MiniCPM by nine layers — the authors attribute this to a third, model-specific lower bound on the intervenable zone and leave it open. The steering recipe generalises across input modality (joy +0.55 text, +0.67 audio-context, +0.33 image-context; sadness/anger moderate and context-dependent) and to one other model (Phi-4 +0.415), while MiniCPM is a null result (+0.022) consistent with its other diagnostics; the independent judge gives +0.246 for joy with a random control of −0.007 and correlates 0.809 with the automatic evaluator. Only joy is direction-specific above their threshold in both models (0.363 and 0.469 on the diagonal-minus-largest-off-diagonal score), which they tie to its unique corner of the valence–arousal circumplex; and the geometric finding behind it is that the shared emotion structure is organised by continuous valence and arousal (single-axis transfer 0.853/0.696) far more than by discrete category (5-class transfer 0.558), with orthogonalizing the two axes collapsing it to 0.176, near chance. Side effects are modest (perplexity 5.62 → 8.39, fluency −0.03, GSM8K 16% → 18%).

## Limits and open problems they name
All three models are ~7B, so whether the mid-to-late zone survives at 70B+ is untested. Emotion labels and datasets are mainly English. Everything is linear — probes and directions — and non-linear read-outs such as SAEs may reveal structure the paper cannot see. Causal validation concentrates on injection toward text generation; within-modality steering (e.g. an audio direction producing audio) is left open. The two-factor model is exact on one model, approximate on a second and fails on a third, so some architecture-related actionable-zone factor is missing, and formally characterising it is named as the central open problem. I add four of my own, marked: (i) the headline 26× compares a **multi-layer** injection against a **single-layer** one, so the number conflates position with layer count — the paper's own single-layer comparison is the one that isolates position, and it gives about 7×; (ii) the read-out and the injection are scored by the same external emotion classifier whose agreement with the second judge is moderate and whose emotional concept may itself be biased toward the strong, unambiguous exemplar (anger agrees only 0.19 with the LLM judge), so the emotion-specificity results partly measure evaluator structure; (iii) the probing curve's peak is measured on *cross-modal transfer* into text, not on within-modality accuracy, so "probe-best" in Table III and in the headline is a transfer-peak, and a different definition could move it; (iv) the safety-steering transfer the paper mentions in future work is reported as directional evidence without controls, so nothing here establishes that the band is the right site for concepts other than emotion.

## What we take from it
- **A measure and a design rule: separate the read-out site from the intervention site, and test them as two curves rather than assuming one.** Any probe we build that is meant to *predict* behaviour under intervention must report where it reads best and where the behaviour actually moves, because this paper's central result is that these are different layers with a causal gap. Cheapest version for us: one layer-wise sweep of our intervention, plotted against one layer-wise sweep of our read-out accuracy, with the peak separation stated.
- **A framing for what a probe accuracy number means:** readability measures "the information is present and linearly separable here"; intervenability measures "the downstream path can still be rewritten from here". Those are different properties of a residual stream, and a white-box model that equates them will mis-locate its own causal handles.
- **A protocol to copy: pre-registered criteria before extending to new models.** The authors fix the dissociation criterion (r < 0.30, or ≥ 5 layers apart) on the primary model and carry it unchanged to the other two, and when the correlation criterion *nearly* passes they say so and adopt the peak-gap criterion as primary instead of re-tuning. This is exactly the discipline our claims need when we test a read-out on a second model: fix the threshold first, report the near-misses, do not retune per model.
- **A cheap control worth adopting everywhere:** every steering condition paired with a same-magnitude random direction, plus a degradation/OOD rate monitored throughout, plus a second evaluator with the agreement between the two reported. The paper's probe-best result is *indistinguishable from random* (+0.02 vs +0.02) — a warning that an effect this small without a random control would look like a weak-but-real finding.
- **A warning about ratio headline metrics:** the 26× is a ratio of two recipes (multi-layer vs single), and the ablations that decompose it sit in a baseline column rather than in the claim. When we report "method A is N× better than the obvious baseline B", the layer-count, direction-variant and dose ablations belong in the same table, or the ratio belongs in the discussion.
