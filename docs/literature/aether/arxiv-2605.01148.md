# Arithmetic in the Wild: Llama uses Base-10 Addition to Reason About Cyclic Concepts

- **Key:** arxiv-2605.01148
- **Authors / organisation:** Sheridan Feucht, Tal Haklay, Usha Bhalla, Daniel Wurgaft, Can Rager, Raphaël Sarfati, Jack Merullo, Thomas McGrath, Owen Lewis, Ekdeep Singh Lubana, Thomas Fel, Atticus Geiger — Goodfire, with Northeastern, Technion IIT, Harvard and Stanford
- **Date:** 2026-05-01 (Goodfire post "A Geometric Calculator Inside a Neural Network", The Neural Geometry Series, 2026-05-14)
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01148 (post: https://www.goodfire.com/research/a-geometric-calculator)
- **Kind:** paper (plus the companion research post)
- **Read from:** full text — the post in full (~9.7k chars, all four footnotes) and the arXiv HTML v1 (~123k chars: main body §1–§7 plus Limitations and Appendices A–H, including the DAS training tables, the Fourier-probe R² sweep and all neuron-ablation tables). **No figure inspected**: every number below is a prose statement or a table cell, so no curve shape, activation heatmap or Fourier-plane geometry is quoted.
- **Relevance to us:** 3 (central)

## What they wanted, and why
Language models represent cyclic concepts — months, weekdays, hours — on circles in activation space, and the modular-arithmetic literature on small transformers says a model that has circles should compute by rotating along them. This paper asks whether the *computation* follows the *geometry*: when Llama-3.1-8B answers "what month is six months after August", does it add in the period of the concept (mod 12), or does it do something else and only convert to the concept's circle at the end? The larger goal is the neural-geometry agenda they state on the post: representation geometry alone explains little if you cannot say which computation operates over it, so the paper is a case study in joining a geometric claim, a causal abstraction of the task, and the concrete parameters that implement it. It is also a direct continuation of the folder's manifold work — a manifold is only interesting as an object a mechanism *runs on*.

## What they studied
Llama-3.1-8B only, at the last token position, one MLP (layer 18, a gated SiLU MLP) and the residual stream around it. Unit of analysis spans three levels at once: low-rank residual subspaces found by distributed alignment search (DAS), linear Fourier probe directions (sine/cosine pairs for each period T), and 28 individual MLP neurons identified by how much their down-projection row lies in the output subspace. Tasks are templates over months (p = 12), weekdays (p = 7), 24-hour time (p = 24) with offsets up to twice the cycle length, plus plain "a+b=" addition over 1–100 as a non-cyclic control.

## How they did it
1. **Behaviour first.** Score every concept × offset combination. All-range accuracy is 65.3% months, 71.4% weekdays, 70.8% hours, 97.2% addition. Within one cycle (offset ≤ p) it is 81.9 / 91.8 / 97.0%. Split by whether the answer wraps past the cycle's start month/day/hour, accuracy is 100% months (sum ≤ 12) versus 55.0% (sum > 12), and the same for weekdays (95.2 / 64.9) and hours (100 / 60.6). Explicit "what is (a+b) mod k" prompts are answered poorly for nearly all k, including 7, 12 and 24.
2. **Coarse localisation.** Whole-residual interchange patching over 4,096 counterfactual pairs finds the output appearing at the last token after layer 18, the offset arriving there at layer 15, and the input concept only separable at its own token position — consistent with a copy of the input into the last token around layer 18.
3. **DAS.** With the model frozen, learn an orthonormal k-dimensional R that maximises interchange-intervention accuracy (IIA) against a hand-written causal model of the task (base, concept-number, sum, output-number, with the base being 7 / 12 / 24 / ∞). Train separately per task, k ≤ 8 (≤ 16 for hours and addition), sweep layers 0–31, use 4,096 pairs (3,584 train / 512 test). Input concept and offset are patchable >95% before the layer-18 MLP and drop sharply after it; the output concept becomes patchable just after it. Best test IIA: months 93.4 / 97.5 / 85.2%, weekdays 98.7 / 98.5 / 98.3%, hours 94.0 / 95.6 / 94.3%, addition 61.7 / 63.8 / 96.6% (input / offset / output).
4. **Circular probes.** Replicate the Engels et al. circle probe (PCA to 5 dimensions, least-squares sine/cosine fit). Circles are recoverable at the input-concept token across layers, but at the final token they do not appear until layers 22–25 — i.e. the layer-18 combination happens *before* the output circle exists.
5. **The decisive test: cross-task patching.** Patch within the union of two tasks' subspaces. Addition → cyclic: force sum 6+8 = 14 into any months prompt and the model emits February in at least 40% of interventions, comparable to its clean months accuracy. Cyclic → addition: patch a months prompt into any "a+b=" prompt and read out the pre-modulo sum; "six months after August" makes the model say 14, and the diagonal stays clean for different pre-modulo sums that collapse to the same month (9 vs 21 → both September). For prompts the model gets wrong, this exposes the correct internal sum in 63% of interventions, and the intervention never raises a month token.
6. **Fourier probes.** Train affine sine/cosine probes for every period 1 ≤ T ≤ 150 on the addition task only; periods {2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100} are reliably recovered, starting around layer 15. These directions are near-orthogonal to each other (one exception: cos/sin at T = 20, cosine −0.21) and overlap substantially with the DAS output subspaces. Steering at layer 18 by rewriting the Fourier coefficients to a target sum (Algorithm 1: preserve the observed radius, set the phase to 2πn′/T, patch each probe direction by the residual needed) shifts the answer to the intended concept on all four tasks at α = 10; per-prompt results are noisier, and "three months after January" largely fails while "seven months after January" works across targets.
7. **Neuron-level mechanism.** Rank every layer-18 neuron by ω = ‖R·d_i‖/‖d_i‖ (share of its down row inside the task's output subspace), threshold 0.4 by eye. 28 addition neurons, 16 for months, 15 weekdays, 26 hours, and every cyclic task's set is a strict subset of the addition set bar one hours neuron; pairwise Pearson correlation of ω across tasks r ≥ 0.70. Zero-ablating the 28 drops addition 94.6 → 24.1%, months 81.25 → 36.8%, weekdays 91.8 → 40.8%, hours 93.1 → 50.7%; flipping their signs is catastrophic (~2.97% on addition). Keeping *only* these 28 and zeroing every other layer-18 neuron retains most accuracy (addition 86.4%, months 77.8%, weekdays 89.8%). Clustering by 1 − |cos| of the down rows recovers the Fourier period of each neuron; the same neurons fire with the same periodic patterns on all four tasks, and 17/28 show "split" behaviour on cyclic tasks, using the gate vector to read one summand and the up vector to read the other — which turns element-wise multiplication into a two-operand product.

## What counts as an explanation here
A causal abstraction of the task (five variables with explicit mechanisms) realised in specific low-rank subspaces, plus a geometric account of what those subspaces contain (Fourier features at periods 2/5/10/20/50/100), plus the concrete neurons that write into that geometry. It is deliberately partial — it explains the addition step and the concept↔number conversion, and it rewrites the answer's circle at the end rather than rotating on it. It predicts: patch between tasks at layer 18 and you get exactly the pre-modulo sum or the modded concept in the way the abstraction says; steer the Fourier phase and the answer moves to the matching concept; ablate the 28 and accuracy collapses for every addition-shaped task.

## How they judged success
Interchange-intervention accuracy per causal variable and task (DAS); clean vs patched accuracy for cross-task patching, including a required failure mode (patching months into addition should never emit a month); probe R² across layers and periods; steering diagonals (target-sum vs emitted concept) at a fixed α; ablation / retain-only / sign-flip accuracy against a chance baseline per task; unseen prompt templates to check the neuron set is not template-specific; principal angles between DAS subspaces across tasks with random-subspace baseline.

## What they found
The model does **not** perform modular addition in the concept's period. It converts the concept to a number, adds in base-10 Fourier space, and maps the sum back to concept space in late layers. Evidence: circular structure is absent at the final token in layer 18 even though the combination has happened there; a months prompt patched into addition exposes the raw pre-modulo sum (14, and a +100 echo at 114, consistent with the hundreds place never being needed for cyclic sums); an addition sum patched into months is decoded as a month; Fourier probes trained only on addition are causal on months, weekdays and hours; 28 neurons in layer 18 — 0.2% of that MLP — carry the sum, partitioned into disjoint, near-perfectly-orthogonal clusters per period, each cluster's down rows summing to a vector whose angle is the sum modulo that period. Smaller periods (2, 5, 10) coexist with period 100 to sharpen the read-out: ablating just the two parity neurons blurs probability onto neighbours with the wrong parity. Errors are regular, not random: they hug diagonals of the pre-modulo-sum heatmap and grow with sum magnitude (months accuracy 100% at sum ≤ 12, 68.1% in 13–24, 30.8% in 25–36), which the authors read as conversion arithmetic degrading, not as the sum being miscomputed — the addition→months patch recovers the right answer where the clean model fails, mirroring the 63% result in the other direction.

## Limits and open problems they name
One 8B model, so nothing about the mechanism generalises across architectures or scales; three cyclic concepts and only forward-addition-style questions (backward offsets and distance queries are out of scope); the *concept-to-number* and *number-to-concept* mappings are assumed, not analysed — that is handed to future work; neuron selection is a hand-set threshold (τ = 0.4) and misses larger-period neurons, which is where the residual error is (the retain-only arm falls to 86% mostly on large summands). Mine, from the appendices: the weekdays alignment is unresolved — weekday↔number embeddings are noisy, patching needs an ad-hoc +4 offset, and the steering enumeration (Saturday = 0) is admitted to have no good explanation, so 1/4 of the "all four tasks" claim rests on a correspondence the authors cannot derive; they also concede the input-argument variables for plain addition are poorly separable (IIA 61.7 / 63.8%) while the output is clean (96.6%), so "the same mechanism" is better evidenced at the output than at the inputs; and the steering needs α = 10, which they read as the Fourier features alone not overriding downstream computation.

## What we take from it
- **A template for joining geometry and causal abstraction, which is exactly the shape a predictive white-box model should take.** DAS gives subspace, layer and variable without assuming what is inside; the probes then say what the subspace *is* (periods); the neuron set says where it is computed. Three measurements, one object. Worth adopting wholesale as our own reporting order.
- **A held-out cross-task transfer test that a probe cannot fake.** Fourier probes were fit only on "a+b=" and then steered months, weekdays and hours; that is a stronger faithfulness argument than in-distribution probe accuracy, and it required no labels from the transfer tasks. This is the cheapest available substitute for the folder's recurring complaint that probes prove correlation.
- **A concrete "ablation your own theory invented" step.** Retain-only ablation (zero every other neuron in the sublayer and keep the 28) both validates the set and *localises the gap to the oracle*: accuracy falls mostly on large summands, which is direct evidence for the neurons the threshold missed. Much better than reporting one ablation number.
- **Error structure as evidence, not noise.** The regular diagonal errors plus the patch-rescues-failed-prompts result turn "accuracy falls with sum size" into "the sum is right and the conversion is failing" — one of the few places in this folder where an error analysis actually supports a mechanism claim.
- **Warning: a circle in representation space is not a computational basis.** The paper's headline is a negative on exactly the inference the folder often makes implicitly (structure implies algorithm). Wherever we show a manifold, we now owe the argument that the mechanism consumes it — and here it did not.
- **Warning: the two-complement gated-MLP reading.** 17/28 neurons use gate for one operand and up for the other, which is why they can multiply instead of add. If our own read-outs treat an MLP neuron as one linear write with one scalar activation, we will miss arithmetic that lives in the pairing of gate and up.
